## The big picture (read this first)

Today's page is one long argument about **how the Torah tells us how far a law reaches** — and about the
limits of pure logic when it tries to do that work on its own. Almost every move on the daf is a *kal
vachomer*, an a fortiori inference, that looks airtight — and almost every one is then shown to leak, so
that the verse is needed after all.

> **מִשּׁוּם דְּאִיכָּא לְמֵימַר מֵעִיקָּרָא דְּדִינָא פִּירְכָא.** "Because one can say the refutation is
> present **from the outset.**" This is the daf's engine. A *kal vachomer* is only as strong as the case it
> is built on; if that source has a stringency of its own, the inference collapses, and the Torah's extra
> word turns out to have been doing real work all along.

Three things happen, in order:

1. **The fat-in-milk dispute is finally put to rest.** Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi split (back on 113b) over
   whether one is flogged for *chelev* cooked in milk. Today the Gemara offers a second reading, then a
   third, and tests them against a Tosefta about whey, blood, and bones (114a).
2. **The scope of the prohibition is derived, word by word.** How do we know the ban reaches a cow's and a
   ewe's milk, an older and a younger "sister," and even a mother's *own* meat in its *own* milk? A cascade
   of *kal vachomer* attempts, each answered by Rav Ashi's "refutation from the outset" and by a chain of
   *yochiach* counter-cases — a horse, a slave, diverse seeds (114a–114b).
3. **Eating and benefit are added to cooking.** The verse forbids only *cooking* a kid in milk. Rav Ashi
   derives that it is forbidden to **eat**, and then to **benefit from**, and a baraita splits Rabbi Meir
   and Rabbi Yehuda over how one may hand a carcass to a resident alien or a gentile (114b).

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **מֵי חָלָב** *(mei chalav)* | **Whey** — the liquid left after milk is curdled. One who cooks meat in whey is exempt, since whey is not "milk" for this prohibition, and the daf uses the case to support Reish Lakish. |
| **אֵין אִיסּוּר חָל עַל אִיסּוּר** *(ein issur chal al issur)* | "**A prohibition does not take effect on a prohibition.**" The axis of the opening dispute — can the milk-ban land on fat that is already forbidden to eat? |
| **מָר אֲמַר חֲדָא וּמָר אֲמַר חֲדָא** *(mar amar chada)* | "**One Sage said one thing and one said another** — and they do not disagree." A reconciliation move — the two rulings speak of different cases, not opposite views. |
| **מֵעִיקָּרָא דְּדִינָא פִּירְכָא** *(me'ikara d'dina pircha)* | "**The refutation is present from the outset.**" Rav Ashi's repeated answer — the *kal vachomer* fails because its very source carries a stringency the target case lacks. |
| **הַרְבָּעָה** *(harba'ah)* | **Mating**, crossbreeding. A goat may be mated with its kid (same species) but a cow may not — a difference the a fortiori tries to exploit. |
| **דִּיר לְהִתְעַשֵּׂר** *(dir lehit'aser)* | The **pen for tithing** animals. An older "sister" has already passed through it in a prior year; a younger one has not — which is how the two sisters are told apart. |
| **מִבֵּינַיָּא** *(mibeinaya)* | "**From between them.**" Deriving a third case from the **common denominator** (*tzad hashaveh*) of two established cases, rather than from either one alone. |
| **...יוֹכִיחַ** *(yochiach)* | "**Let X prove it.**" A counter-case brought to break an a fortiori — pointing to a domain where the same logic yields the opposite result. |
| **פְּרִי עִם הָאֵם** *(pri im ha'em)* | "**Fruit with the mother.**" The offspring paired with its own dam — set against *pri im pri*, offspring with offspring, throughout the 114b chain. |
| **שְׁנֵי גּוּפִים / גּוּף אֶחָד** *(shnei gufim / guf echad)* | "**Two bodies / one body.**" Rav Ashi's decisive distinction — a kid and its mother's milk were never one thing, but a mother's meat and its milk once were. |
| **לֹא תֹאכַל כׇּל תּוֹעֵבָה** *(lo tochal kol to'evah)* | "**You shall not eat any abominable thing.**" Rav Ashi's source that anything the Torah calls abominable — meat in milk included — is forbidden to eat, not only to cook. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Meir** — fourth-generation Tanna, foremost student of Rabbi Akiva; here he reads the carcass verse
  expansively, permitting both giving and selling to either a resident alien or a gentile.
- **Rabbi Yehuda** (bar Ilai) — fourth-generation Tanna, also a student of Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Meir's
  frequent disputant; reads the verse "as written" — giving to the resident alien, selling to the gentile.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav Ashi** — sixth-generation Amora, of Mata Meḥasya, the great redactor of the Talmud; the dominant
  voice of the daf — the "refutation from the outset," the two-bodies/one-body distinction, and the
  derivation that meat-in-milk is forbidden to eat and to benefit from. (**Rav Ashi**, רב אשי — not **Rav
  Asi**, the earlier Babylonian, and not **Rabbi Asi** of Tiberias below.)
- **Rav Aḥadvoi bar Ami** — a minor Amora; offers the horse-brother-of-a-mule counter-case. Beyond a handful
  of such exchanges I would not claim much of his biography.
- **Mar son of Ravina** (Mar bar Ravina) — fifth/sixth-generation Amora, known for his piety; offers the
  Canaanite-slave counter-case. (**Mar bar Ravina** — not to be confused with the redactor Ravina himself,
  nor with Mar bar Rav Ashi.)
- **Rav Idi bar Avin** — a Babylonian Amora; offers the diverse-seeds counter-case.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Ami** and **Rabbi Asi** — third-generation, the leading pair of Tiberias; their dispute over
  flogging for fat-in-milk, carried from 113b, is resolved here. (Rabbi **Asi** — with *Rabbi* — is the
  Eretz-Yisrael Amora, distinct from Rav Ashi the redactor.)
- **Reish Lakish** (Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish) — second-generation Amora of Tiberias, brother-in-law and great
  disputant of Rabbi Yochanan; limits "whey is like milk" to susceptibility to impurity alone.
- **Rabbi Abbahu** — third-generation Amora of Caesarea, a student of Rabbi Yochanan; his rule (in the name
  of **Rabbi Elazar** ben Pedat) that a prohibition on eating carries a prohibition on benefit supplies the
  benefit-ban here.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 114a — Two more ways to read the fat-in-milk dispute

The daf opens still inside yesterday's split between **Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi** over cooking forbidden
*chelev* in milk. On 113b the Gemara read it as a dispute over *cooking*. Now two alternatives:

**Some say the opposite (איכא דאמרי).** With regard to *cooking*, all agree one **is** flogged — a
prohibition landing on a prohibition is not even at issue there. Where they split is *eating*. The one who
says **not flogged** applies *ein issur chal al issur* — the fat was already forbidden to eat before the
milk arrived. The one who says **flogged** holds the Torah deliberately expressed eating **in the language
of cooking**, so that whoever is flogged for cooking is flogged for eating too.

**And if you wish, say (ואיבעית אימא).** **מָר אֲמַר חֲדָא, וּמָר אֲמַר חֲדָא, וְלָא פְּלִיגִי** — "one Sage
said one thing and one said another, and they do not disagree." The one who said *flogged* was speaking of
**cooking**; the one who said *not flogged*, of **eating**. Everyone agrees a prohibition does not take
effect where one already sits.

**The Tosefta objection.** A Tosefta (8:3) is brought against them:

> **הַמְבַשֵּׁל בְּמֵי חָלָב פָּטוּר... הַפִּגּוּל וְהַנּוֹתָר וְהַטָּמֵא שֶׁבִּשְּׁלָן בְּחָלָב חַיָּיב!**
> Cooking meat in **whey**, or blood in milk, or bones, tendons, horns, and hooves in milk — **exempt**. But
> **piggul, notar, and impure** offering-meat cooked in milk — **liable!**

If one is liable for cooking already-forbidden *piggul* and *notar* in milk, then a prohibition **does** land
on a prohibition — against the reading just given. **The answer.** This *tanna* holds that a prohibition
takes effect on a prohibition; Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi, as *amora'im*, may side with the *tannaim* who hold
otherwise. It is an old tannaitic dispute.

**Reish Lakish on whey.** The Tosefta's opening — *cooking in whey is exempt* — supports **Reish Lakish**. A
mishna (Makhshirin 6:5) teaches that **whey is like milk** and olive-secretion is like oil. Reish Lakish
narrows it: that equation is only for **rendering seeds susceptible to impurity**; for **meat-in-milk**, whey
is **not** like milk. The Tosefta bears him out.

### 114a — Cow and ewe, older sister and younger

Now a run of baraitot on the phrase *"in its mother's milk,"* written three times in the Torah, each
generating scope by *kal vachomer* — and each, the Gemara shows, still needing its verse.

**Cow and ewe.** From *"its mother's milk"* I know only the mother **goat's** milk. Whence a **cow** and a
**ewe**? A *kal vachomer*: the mother, which may be **mated** with the kid (same species), is nonetheless
forbidden for **cooking**; a cow or ewe, which may **not** be mated with it, surely should be! **So why the
verse?** **Rav Ashi:** *me'ikara d'dina pircha* — the refutation sits at the source. The mother is stringent
in a way a cow is not — one may not **slaughter** it and its offspring on the same day. So the inference is
flawed, and *"in its mother's milk"* is needed to include cow and ewe.

**The older sister.** A second baraita, on the third mention: I know only the mother's milk — a mother under
a year, not yet entered the pen to be tithed. Whence the **older sister**, over a year, already through the
pen? *Kal vachomer*: the mother, which **does** enter the pen with the kid, is forbidden for cooking; the
sister, which does **not**, surely should be! Again **Rav Ashi:** the refutation is at the source — the
mother is forbidden for slaughter with the kid, the sister is not. The verse is needed.

**The younger sister.** Whence the **younger sister** — one not yet through the pen? **מִבֵּינַיָּא** — from
the **common denominator** of the mother and the older sister. Neither shares the younger sister's leniency,
yet each is included; their common element is simply that **both are meat and forbidden to cook in milk**, so
the younger sister comes along. **The challenge:** if a *tzad hashaveh* works, then the **older** sister,
too, could have been derived *mibeinaya* — from the mother and a cow — and no verse would be needed for her!
**The concession:** indeed so. So what is the extra *"in its mother's milk"* for? It is needed for the next
baraita.

### 114b — A mother's own meat in its own milk

The next baraita asks the sharpest case: the verse forbids cooking the **kid** in its mother's milk — whence
that a **mother's own meat** may not be cooked in **its own** milk? A *kal vachomer* offers itself: where
*fruit-with-fruit* is **not** forbidden (two offspring may be slaughtered on one day) yet *fruit-with-mother*
**is** (slaughter), then where *fruit-with-fruit* **is** forbidden (cooking a kid in milk), *fruit-with-mother*
surely should be! **Why, then, the verse?** Because the inference can be refuted — and now the *yochiach*
chain:

- **The horse (Rav Aḥadvoi bar Ami).** A horse born of a stallion and mare that is the *brother of a mule*:
  *fruit-with-fruit* is forbidden (one may not mate it with the mule) yet *fruit-with-mother* is permitted
  (it may be mated with its own dam). **Rejected:** there it is the **sire's seed** that forbids, not the
  shared mother — proven by a mule whose case runs the other way.
- **The slave (Mar son of Ravina).** A Canaanite slave who is the *brother of a freed sister*:
  *fruit-with-fruit* forbidden (he may not be with his freed sister) yet *fruit-with-mother* permitted (he
  may be with his slave mother). **Rejected:** there it is the **bill of manumission** that forbids — proven
  by the mirror case of a slave whose mother was freed.
- **Diverse seeds (Rav Idi bar Avin).** *Kilayim*: *fruit-with-fruit* forbidden (two species sown together)
  yet *fruit-with-mother* permitted (any seed sown in the soil). **Rejected:** diverse seeds are forbidden
  only **by way of** the mother — the soil; wheat and barley in a single jug are not forbidden at all.

**Rav Ashi settles it.** The a fortiori was never sound, because *fruit-with-fruit* and *fruit-with-mother*
are not comparable:

> **מָה לִפְרִי עִם פְּרִי שֶׁכֵּן שְׁנֵי גּוּפִים, תֹּאמַר בִּפְרִי עִם הָאֵם שֶׁכֵּן גּוּף אֶחָד.** A kid
> and its mother's milk are **two bodies** that were never one; a mother's meat and its own milk were once a
> **single body.** They cannot be equated — and so the verse *"in its mother's milk"* was needed.

### 114b — Forbidden to eat, forbidden to benefit

The written verse forbids only *cooking*. **Rav Ashi:** whence that meat in milk is forbidden to **eat**?
From **״לֹא תֹאכַל כׇּל תּוֹעֵבָה״** (Deut. 14:3) — *anything I have made abominable to you is under "you
shall not eat."* And whence forbidden to **benefit** from? Per **Rabbi Abbahu** in the name of **Rabbi
Elazar:** wherever the Torah says *"shall not eat,"* both eating **and** benefit are forbidden, unless the
verse says otherwise — as it does for a **carcass**, which may be sold to a foreigner.

That exception opens a baraita on the carcass verse (Deut. 14:21):

- **Rabbi Meir:** the word *"or"* (*"give it… or sell it"*) teaches that one may transfer a carcass to
  **either** a resident alien or a gentile, **either** by giving or by selling.
- **Rabbi Yehuda:** the words are **as written** — to the resident alien by **giving**, to the gentile by
  **selling**. The *"or"* is there to teach **priority** — you are commanded to sustain the resident alien,
  and not the gentile — which Rabbi Meir says needs no verse, being plain reason.

The daf closes with a **mnemonic** (*siman*) for the topics ahead — *Shabbat; plows; diverse seeds; a mother
and its offspring; sending away the nest* — and the opening word of the next challenge, **אֶלָּא מֵעַתָּה**,
"if that is so," which spills straight into tomorrow's daf.

## The distinctions, side by side

The heart of 114b is the *yochiach* chain — three domains dragged in to break the a fortiori from
*fruit-with-fruit* to *fruit-with-mother*, and each shown to prove nothing because a hidden factor, not
motherhood, was doing the work:

| Counter-case offered | Fruit with fruit | Fruit with mother | Why it proves nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Horse**, brother of a mule | Forbidden — not mated with the mule | Permitted — mated with its own dam | The **sire's seed** forbids the pair, not the shared mother |
| **Slave**, brother of a freed sister | Forbidden — barred from the freed sister | Permitted — allowed to the slave mother | The **bill of manumission** forbids her, not motherhood |
| **Diverse seeds** (*kilayim*) | Forbidden — two species together | Permitted — any seed in the soil | Forbidden only **by way of** the mother — the soil itself |

The chain ends not with a fourth counter-case but with Rav Ashi's flat refusal to compare the two at all —
**two bodies** versus **one body** — which is why the verse, not the logic, carries the law.

## One line to carry with you

> Almost every inference on the page looks watertight and then leaks. The lesson is the Gemara's own — a
> *kal vachomer* is only as sound as the case it stands on, and where the source hides a stringency of its
> own, the Torah's extra word was never spare.
