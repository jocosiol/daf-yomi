## The big picture (read this first)

The daf opens still inside yesterday's argument. A chest-compartment (*teivat ha-migdal*) stands in a
house with a source of impurity sealed in its drawer, and the Gemara is pinning down exactly *why* the two
tannaim rule as they do. The whole first half turns on one fault line R' Yosei sits astride:

> Does **hidden impurity break through and rise** — *tum'a temuna boka'at ve-ola* — defiling what is above
> it even through a covering with no hole? R' Yosei, we are told, holds it does **not**. And then the
> Gemara catches him seeming to hold that it **does**.

The trap is sprung by the famous mishna of the **dog dead on the threshold** (*Oholot* 11:7): a dog ate an
olive-bulk of a corpse, died lying half-in and half-out of the doorway, and now the question is whether the
house is impure. R' Yosei rules "from the lintel inward — impure." Take that at face value and R' Yosei is
saying the impurity buried in the dog's neck rises through the flesh into the house — hidden impurity
breaking through. That is the exact opposite of what we just established.

**Rava's rescue is the hinge of the page:** R' Yosei never meant "look where the impurity lies." He meant
*"רוֹאִין אֶת חֲלַל הַטּוּמְאָה"* — **one looks at the hollow cavity** around the impurity. The dog defiles the
house only the ordinary way, by a *tent* drawing impurity along a handbreadth of hollow neck; and R' Yosei
is quarrelling with R' Meir on **two** fronts at once — is it the neck's *width* or its *hollow* that
matters, and is the *whole* house impure or only the part under the tent. Hidden impurity still does not
break through.

The second half changes gear. R' Shimon's baraita of the **three impurities** — each defiling by exactly
two of contact, carrying, and tent — turns out to prove R' Shimon is the tanna who *disagrees* with R'
Yosei that a tent counts as "touching." Then the mishna's thread on **thigh bones** resumes: a sealed one is
pure because you cannot reach the flesh, and the daf presses that principle until it nearly proves too much
(an animal in its hide? a kidney in its fat?), before turning to eggs and to the strange half-earth mouse.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **טוּמְאָה טְמוּנָה בּוֹקַעַת וְעוֹלָה** *(tum'a temuna boka'at ve-ola)* | **Hidden impurity breaks through and rises** — impurity sealed under a covering defiles what is above it even with no hole. The fault line of the whole first half; R' Yosei holds it does *not*. |
| **רוֹאִין אֶת חֲלַל הַטּוּמְאָה** *(ro'in et chalal ha-tum'a)* | **One looks at the hollow cavity** of the impurity. Rava's rereading of R' Yosei's word *ro'in* — not where the impurity lies, but whether the neck holds a hollow handbreadth. |
| **פּוֹתֵחַ טֶפַח** *(pote'aḥ tefaḥ)* | An **opening of a handbreadth**. R' Meir's measure: if the dog's neck is a handbreadth *wide* it forms a tent and imports impurity. R' Yosei answers: only if the *hollow* is a handbreadth. |
| **הַמְשָׁכָה** *(hamshacha)* | **Drawing** — the way a proper tent carries impurity from its source *along* to the person or house. What actually makes the dog defile, on Rava's reading — no breaking-through needed. |
| **מַשְׁקוֹף** *(mashkof / shakof)* | The **lintel** of the doorway. R' Yosei's line: from the lintel *inward* the house is impure, from the lintel *outward* it is pure. |
| **מְלֹא תַרְוָוד רָקָב** *(melo tarvad rakav)* | A **full ladle of corpse-decay dust**. In R' Shimon's baraita it defiles by carrying and tent but *not* contact — you cannot touch every grain at once. |
| **עֶצֶם כִּשְׂעוֹרָה** *(etzem kis'ora)* | A **bone the size of a barley grain**. Defiles by contact and carrying but *not* in a tent — a law given at Sinai (Tosafot: derived from a verse in Nazir). |
| **גּוֹלֵל וְדוֹפֵק** *(golel va-dofek)* | The **grave cover and grave wall** that support it. Defiles by contact and tent but *not* by carrying. |
| **מַגָּע / מַשָּׂא / אֹהֶל** *(maga / masa / ohel)* | The **three routes** of corpse-impurity — contact, carrying, and tent. R' Shimon's grid asks which one each source lacks. |
| **אֶת שֶׁאֶפְשָׁר לִיגַּע** *(et she-efshar liga)* | **"That which one can touch."** The rule dividing sealed from perforated — one who can reach the flesh is impure, one who cannot is pure. |
| **מְחוּסַּר נְקִיבָה / מְחוּסַּר מַעֲשֶׂה** *(meḥusar nekiva / meḥusar ma'aseh)* | **Lacking perforation / lacking an action.** Rav Oshaya's question: is a bone one *means* to perforate still "sealed," or does the mere intent already make it defile? |
| **עַכְבָּר שֶׁחֶצְיוֹ בָּשָׂר** *(achbar she-ḥetzyo basar)* | The mishna's **half-flesh, half-earth mouse** — thought to generate from the ground. Touch the flesh, impure; touch the earth, pure. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim (the *Oholot* dispute and R' Shimon's baraita)**

- **Rabbi Meir** — third-generation Tanna, foremost student of R' Akiva; in the dog mishna he measures by the
  *width* of the neck: a handbreadth wide and it imports impurity.
- **Rabbi Yosei** (ben Ḥalafta) — third–fourth-generation Tanna of Tzippori, also a student of R' Akiva; the
  figure the whole first half revolves around. Holds hidden impurity does *not* break through, and — on
  Rava's reading — looks to the neck's *hollow*, not its width.
- **Rabbi Elazar** — the Tanna named in the dog mishna (mouth inward, house pure; mouth outward, house
  impure, since impurity exits through the rear). Most likely R' Elazar ben Shammua; I would not press the
  identification harder than that.
- **Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira** — Tanna of Netzivin (Nisibis) in Babylonia; rules the house impure either way,
  since the impurity can leave through mouth or rear.
- **Rabbi Shimon** (bar Yoḥai) — third-generation Tanna, student of R' Akiva; his baraita of the three
  impurities is the tanna who *disagrees* with R' Yosei — for R' Shimon a tent is *not* "touching," which is
  why dust can defile in a tent yet not by contact.
- **Rabbi Yehuda** (bar Ilai) — third-generation Tanna, student of R' Akiva; in the mouse mishna he extends
  impurity to the earth *adjacent* to the flesh.

**Amoraim**

- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation head of Maḥoza; author of the rescue — R' Yosei "looks at
  the hollow cavity" and argues on two fronts. (**Rava**, רבא — not **Rabbah**, רבה; no Rabbah appears here.)
- **Rav Aḥa, son of Rava** — fifth-generation Babylonian Amora; teaches Rava's reading as *explicit* in the
  mishna. (Distinguish **Rav Aḥa son of Rava** from **Rav Aḥa bar Rav Ashi** — a different sage.)
- **Abaye** — fourth-generation head of Pumbedita; fields R' Zeira's challenge with the animal's orifices.
- **Rabbi Zeira** — third-generation Amora who went up from Bavel to Tiberias; presses that an animal in its
  hide should not defile if a sealed bone does not.
- **Rav Pappa** — fifth-generation Babylonian Amora, student of Abaye and Rava; presses the parallel case of
  a kidney sheathed in its fat.
- **Rav Oshaya** — the Amora who raises and then resolves the "intended to perforate" dilemma. A minor voice
  here (the text shifts between *Rav* and *Rabbi* Oshaya); I would not build a biography on him.
- **Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi** — first-generation Amora of Lod (Lydda), Eretz Yisrael; qualifies the mouse —
  impure by its flesh only if flesh has formed along the creature's *entire* length. (Not R' Yehoshua the
  Tanna.)

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 126a — On which clause did R' Yosei rule pure?

**Where we are.** The chest-compartment argument from 125b runs on. The Gemara asks *"אַהֵיָיא?"* — to which
clause does R' Yosei's ruling of *pure* attach? Not the latter clause (a chest in the entrance), since *the
first tanna deems that pure too*. Rather, the first tanna held: impurity inside the compartment makes the
house impure — *"אִי מִשּׁוּם דְּדֶרֶךְ טוּמְאָה לָצֵאת, וְאִי מִשּׁוּם דְּטוּמְאָה טְמוּנָה בּוֹקַעַת"* — either
because impurity tends to *exit*, or because *hidden impurity breaks through*.

**R' Yosei's two answers.** To "impurity tends to exit," R' Yosei replies: *"יָכוֹל הוּא לְהוֹצִיאָהּ לַחֲצָאִין
אוֹ לְשׂוֹרְפָהּ בִּמְקוֹמָהּ"* — one could remove it *piecemeal* or *burn it in place*, so it need not exit. And
to "hidden impurity breaks through," he replies flatly: *"טוּמְאָה טְמוּנָה אֵינָהּ בּוֹקַעַת"* — **hidden
impurity does not break through.** Hold onto that line.

### 126a — The contradiction: R' Yosei against R' Yosei

**The clash.** *"וְרָמֵי דְּרַבִּי יוֹסֵי אַדְּרַבִּי יוֹסֵי"* — the Gemara sets R' Yosei against himself, from the
mishna of the dog. A dog ate corpse-flesh, died on the threshold with its neck facing in, and:

- **R' Meir:** *"אִם יֵשׁ בְּצַוָּארוֹ פּוֹתֵחַ טֶפַח – מֵבִיא אֶת הַטּוּמְאָה"* — if the neck is a handbreadth wide it
  imports impurity; if not, it does not.
- **R' Yosei:** *"רוֹאִין מִכְּנֶגֶד הַשָּׁקוֹף וְלִפְנִים – הַבַּיִת טָמֵא"* — one looks; from the lintel inward the
  house is impure, from the lintel outward pure.
- **R' Elazar:** mouth inward — pure; mouth outward — impure, *"מִפְּנֵי שֶׁטּוּמְאָה יוֹצְאָה דֶּרֶךְ שׁוּלָיו"*,
  because impurity exits through the rear.
- **R' Yehuda ben Beteira:** either way, impure.

**The problem.** *"מַאי לָאו, אַאֵין בְּצַוָּארוֹ פּוֹתֵחַ טֶפַח קָאֵי רַבִּי יוֹסֵי?"* — surely R' Yosei is
speaking where the neck is *not* a handbreadth, and still rules impure from the lintel inward. But then the
impurity in the dog's neck is *hidden*, with no tent to draw it — so *"וּשְׁמַע מִינַּהּ: טוּמְאָה טְמוּנָה
בּוֹקַעַת!"* — **learn from it that hidden impurity breaks through**, exactly against what we just fixed.

### 126a — Rava: "one looks at the hollow"

**The rescue.** *"אָמַר רָבָא: רוֹאִין אֶת חֲלַל הַטּוּמְאָה קָתָנֵי"* — R' Yosei's word *ro'in* does not mean "look
where the impurity lies." It means **look at the hollow cavity** of the neck — is there a handbreadth of
*empty space*, enough to form a tent that *draws* the impurity? And *"רַבִּי יוֹסֵי בְּתַרְתֵּי פְּלִיג"* — R'
Yosei is disagreeing with R' Meir on **two** points at once:

1. **Width versus hollow.** To R' Meir's "a handbreadth *wide* imports impurity," R' Yosei answers
   *"אֲנַן בָּתַר חֲלָלַהּ אָזְלִינַן"* — **we follow the hollow**, not the outer width; the neck's flesh does not
   count, only a handbreadth of open cavity.
2. **Whole house versus part.** To "the *whole* house is impure," R' Yosei answers: *from the lintel inward*
   impure, *from the lintel outward* pure — only the stretch the tent actually overlies.

So R' Yosei defiles the house the ordinary way — a tent *drawing* impurity through a hollow neck — and hidden
impurity still does not break through. No contradiction.

**Made explicit.** *"רַב אַחָא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרָבָא מַתְנֵי לַהּ בְּהֶדְיָא"* — Rav Aḥa son of Rava simply *teaches the
mishna that way*: R' Yosei says outright, "one looks at the hollow cavity of the impurity."

### 126a — Who disagrees with R' Yosei? R' Shimon

**The question.** R' Yosei calls a tent a kind of "touching." *"וּמַאן תַּנָּא דִּפְלִיג עֲלֵיהּ?"* — and who is
the tanna who holds a tent is *not* touching, so that the half-olive one *touches* and the half a tent
*overlies* do **not** combine (the *Oholot* 3:1 mishna cited on 125b)? *"רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן הוּא"* — it is R'
Shimon.

### 126b — R' Shimon's three impurities

**The baraita.** *"שָׁלֹשׁ טוּמְאוֹת פּוֹרְשׁוֹת מִן הַמֵּת, שְׁתַּיִם בְּכׇל אַחַת וּשְׁלִישִׁית אֵין בָּהֶן"* — three
impurities separate from the corpse, each with *two* of the three routes and *not* the third:

- **A ladle of corpse-dust** (*melo tarvad rakav*): carrying and tent — *not contact*, since you cannot touch
  every grain at once. *This* is the proof: dust defiles in a tent but not by touch, so for R' Shimon a tent
  is **not** touching — against R' Yosei.
- **A barley-grain bone** (*etzem kis'ora*): contact and carrying — *not tent* (a law from Sinai).
- **A grave cover and wall** (*golel va-dofek*): contact and tent — *not carrying*.

And the missing route of each is supplied *"עִם אַחַת מֵהֶן"* — by joining one of the other two.

### 126b — Sealed thigh bones: "that which one can touch"

**The baraita.** The mishna's thigh-bone thread resumes. *"בְּנִבְלָתָהּ – וְלֹא בְּקוּלִית סְתוּמָה"* — "its
carcass" defiles, but not a *sealed* thigh bone. Might even a *perforated* one stay pure? *"תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר
הַנּוֹגֵעַ … יִטְמָא"* — the verse teaches: *"אֶת שֶׁאֶפְשָׁר לִיגַּע – טָמֵא, וְאֶת שֶׁאִי אֶפְשָׁר לִיגַּע –
טָהוֹר"* — **what one can touch is impure, what one cannot is pure.** Sealed, you cannot reach the marrow;
perforated, you can.

**Pressing the principle too far.** If "cannot touch" means pure, two objections land:

- **R' Zeira to Abaye:** *"בְּהֵמָה בְּעוֹרָהּ לֹא תְּטַמֵּא?"* — then an animal still in its hide should not
  defile, for you touch only the hide! **Abaye:** *"פּוֹק חֲזִי כַּמָּה נְקָבִים יֵשׁ בָּהּ"* — go see how many
  *orifices* it has — eyes, nostrils, mouth — through which you *can* reach the flesh.
- **Rav Pappa to Rava:** *"כּוּלְיָא בְּחֶלְבָּהּ לֹא תְּטַמֵּא?"* — then a kidney sheathed in its fat should not
  defile! **Rava:** *"תָּא חֲזִי כַּמָּה חוּטִין נִמְשָׁכִין הֵימֶנָּה"* — come see how many *sinews* run out of
  it that you can touch.

**Rav Oshaya's dilemma.** *"חִישֵּׁב עָלֶיהָ לְנוֹקְבָהּ וְלֹא נִיקְּבָה, מַהוּ?"* — he *meant* to perforate the
bone but has not yet. Is *"מְחוּסַּר נְקִיבָה כִּמְחוּסַּר מַעֲשֶׂה"* — is a bone lacking perforation like one
lacking a real action, still sealed? He resolves it himself: *"לָאו כִּמְחוּסַּר מַעֲשֶׂה דָּמֵי"* — **not** like
lacking an action; the intent is enough, and it defiles as if already open.

### 126b — The mishna: eggs, and the half-earth mouse

**The mishna.** *"בֵּיצַת הַשֶּׁרֶץ הַמְרוּקֶּמֶת טְהוֹרָה"* — a creeping-thing's egg in which an embryo has formed
is pure (sealed); *"נִיקְּבָה כׇּל שֶׁהוּא – טָמֵא"*, perforated the least bit, impure. And the *"עַכְבָּר שֶׁחֶצְיוֹ
בָּשָׂר וְחֶצְיוֹ אֲדָמָה"* — a mouse half flesh, half earth (thought to grow from the ground): touch the flesh,
impure; the earth, pure. **R' Yehuda:** even the earth *adjacent to* the flesh is impure.

**The derivations.** *"הַטְּמֵאִים"* includes the egg and the thigh bone of a creeping thing. Might even an
*undeveloped* egg defile? *"הַשֶּׁרֶץ"* — just as a creeping thing has *formed*, so only a *developed* egg.
Might even an *unperforated* one? Again *"אֶת שֶׁאֶפְשָׁר לִיגַּע"*. And how large a hole? *"כְּחוּט הַשַּׂעֲרָה"*
— **the width of a hair**, since with a hair's strand you can reach inside.

**The mouse.** *"אָמַר רַבִּי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בֶּן לֵוִי: וְהוּא שֶׁהִשְׁרִיץ עַל פְּנֵי כּוּלּוֹ"* — R' Yehoshua ben Levi
qualifies it: impure by its flesh only where flesh has formed along the creature's *whole length*. The daf
then opens a baraita on whether a *sea*-mouse defiles — the thread that carries into 127a.

## The distinctions, side by side

R' Shimon's baraita is a perfect grid: three impurities from the corpse, each defiling by exactly two of the
three routes. The missing route of each is supplied only by *joining* one of the others. Every cell traces to
the walkthrough above.

| Source | Contact (*maga*) | Carrying (*masa*) | Tent (*ohel*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ladle of corpse-dust** (*melo tarvad rakav*) | — cannot touch every grain | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Barley-grain bone** (*etzem kis'ora*) | ✓ | ✓ | — law from Sinai |
| **Grave cover & wall** (*golel va-dofek*) | ✓ | — | ✓ |

## One line to carry with you

> A word can carry a hidden second meaning, and a page can turn on catching it: R' Yosei's *"one looks"* was
> never about *where the impurity lies* but about *whether there is a hollow to look into* — and the whole
> contradiction dissolves the moment you read the cavity instead of the corpse.

    - A grave cover and grave wall (golel va-dofek), which defile by contact and in a tent but not by carrying.
    - The half-flesh, half-earth mouse, which defiles by contact wherever its flesh has fully formed.
  correct: c
  why: Golel va-dofek — maga and ohel, but not masa; its carrying is supplied by joining one of the others.
- q: Why is a sealed thigh bone of a carcass pure to the touch, per the baraita on "benivlatah"?
  opts:
    - Because the marrow inside a sealed bone is always considered nullified and treated as absent.
    - Because a carcass bone never defiles unless it also carries an olive-bulk of flesh upon it.
    - Because one cannot reach the flesh through it — what one can touch is impure, what one cannot is pure.
    - Because a bone only defiles once a decree of the Sages has extended impurity to its outer surface.
  correct: c
  why: Et she-efshar liga tamei, et she-i efshar liga tahor — sealed, the flesh is out of reach; perforated, it can be touched.
- q: How does Abaye answer R' Zeira's challenge that an animal in its hide should not defile?
  opts:
    - By ruling that a hide is nullified to the animal, so touching it counts as touching the flesh.
    - By pointing out how many orifices — eyes, nostrils, mouth — let one reach the flesh within.
    - By distinguishing a living animal, which never defiles, from its carcass, which always does.
    - By explaining that the hide itself becomes a source of impurity once the animal has died.
  correct: b
  why: Pok chazi kama nekavim yesh bah — through the eyes, nose, and mouth one can indeed touch the flesh.
- q: What does Rav Oshaya conclude about a thigh bone one intended to perforate but has not yet perforated?
  opts:
    - That lacking perforation is not like lacking an action, so it defiles even before it is opened.
    - That lacking perforation is exactly like lacking an action, so it stays sealed and remains pure.
    - That intent alone never matters for impurity, so only an actual hole can ever change its status.
    - That it defiles by carrying but not by contact until the perforation is physically completed.
  correct: a
  why: Mechusar nekiva lav ke-mechusar ma'aseh dami — the intent suffices and it defiles as if already open.
- q: In the new mishna, when does a creeping-thing's egg defile, and how large must the perforation be?
  opts:
    - Only once an embryo has formed and it is perforated even the width of a strand of hair.
    - As soon as it is laid, whether or not an embryo has formed, provided any hole exists at all.
    - Only if an embryo has formed and the hole is at least a handbreadth wide, like the dog's neck.
    - Whenever it is carried, since carrying defiles an egg even while it remains fully sealed.
  correct: a
  why: Ha-merukemet, and nikva kol shehu — perforated k'chut ha-sa'ara, a hair's width, since with a hair one can touch inside.
