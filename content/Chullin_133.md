## The big picture (read this first)

The daf opens still inside a baraita begun on 132b — the list of Temple **rites reserved to
the sons of Aaron** (receiving and sprinkling the blood, the *sota* water, breaking the
heifer's neck, purifying a leper, lifting the hands for the priestly blessing). Its closing
line is what carries into today: a priest who **does not believe** in these rites has no
share in the gifts. Read the reason back the other way and you get the challenge to Rav
Ḥisda — *believing* is the test, not *expertise* — and the whole thread of yesterday snaps
into focus.

But the heart of 133a is not halakha at all. It is one of the great ethical passages of Shas
on a deceptively small question — **how should a priest take what is his?** Snatch the gift
from a child's hands out of love for the mitzva, or is snatching contempt? From the single
word **"and he shall give"** the Gemara rules it is contempt: the owner gives; the priest
does not grab. Then **Abaye** tells his own story — a staged retreat from **seizing** the
gifts, to merely **asking** for them, to **waiting silently**, to **withdrawing his hand
altogether** — each step triggered by a line he overheard.

> The modest withdraw their hands; the greedy divide. Abaye keeps taking one day a year —
> Erev Yom Kippur — only so his fellow priests will not forget that he is a priest at all.

Woven into this is the sharp warning about **teaching an unworthy student** — dramatized in
the dream that rebukes Rava for eating a gift he could have done without. Only then does the
daf turn technical: when a partnership with a priest or gentile must be **marked**, the
famous baraita of the **twenty-four priestly gifts**, and the principle that closes the page
— **we follow the obligation**, so a gift clings to the limb that bears it, not to whoever
owns the bulk of the animal.

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**
- **Rabbi Meir** — fourth-generation Tanna, disciple of R' Akiva; his baraita reads Samuel's sons as sinning only by *asking* for their Levite portion aloud.

**Babylonian Amoraim**
- **Rav** (Abba Arikha) — first-generation Amora, founder of the academy at Sura; source of the ruling on the jaw's veins and (via Rabbi Abba and Rav Huna) on which priest receives the gift.
- **Rabbi Abba** — third-generation Amora who studied under Rav Huna in Babylon before settling in Eretz Yisrael; transmits Rav's ruling here.
- **Rav Huna** — second-generation Amora, head of Sura, foremost disciple of Rav; both a link in that chain and the author of the partnership ruling ("partner in the head — exempt from the jaw").
- **Rav Yosef** (bar Ḥiyya) — third-generation Amora, head of Pumbedita; poses the "seizing gifts" question and permits assigning gifts to a poor scholar.
- **Rava** — fourth-generation Amora of Meḥoza, himself a priest of the house of Eli; reports Rav Yosef's question, and later eats the gift in the story. (**Rava** רבא, not **Rabbah** רבה.)
- **Abaye** — fourth-generation Amora, head of Pumbedita, also a priest of the house of Eli; tells the staged story of how he learned to stop seizing the gifts.
- **Rav Safra** — third-to-fourth-generation Amora renowned for scrupulous honesty; declines the gift in the story and is rebuked in a dream.
- **Rav Dimi** — fourth-generation traveler between Eretz Yisrael and Babylon; gives Abaye the plain sense of the dream's verse.
- **Rav Yehuda** (bar Yeḥezkel) — second-generation Amora, founder of Pumbedita; teaches (from Rav) that one who teaches an unworthy student falls into Gehenna.
- **Rabbi Zeira** — third-generation Amora, Babylon-born, later of Eretz Yisrael; likens teaching an unworthy student to idolatry.
- **Ḥiyya bar Rav** — early Amora, son of Rav; holds that a partner in one limb is exempt from all the gifts — the view the Gemara conclusively refutes. (**Ḥiyya bar Rav**, not the Tanna **Rabbi Ḥiyya**.)
- **Rav Adda bar Ahava** — here the *later* one, a contemporary of Rava and Rav Pappa; interprets the disqualified-consecrated baraita before Rav Pappa. (Not the earlier Rav Adda bar Ahava, disciple of Rav.)
- **Rav Pappa** — fifth-generation Amora, head of the academy at Naresh; disciple of Rava and Abaye.
- **Rav Ḥisda** — third-generation Amora of Sura; explains what misled Ḥiyya bar Rav and cites the baraita of the twenty-four gifts.

**Minor figures**
- **Mar Yoḥana, son of Rav Ḥana bar Adda** (or **bar Bizna**) — the host in the story; the tradition is unsure of his father's name, and little else is known of him.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 133a — believing, seizing, and withdrawing

**The rites of Aaron's sons.** The baraita finishes its list — receiving and sprinkling the
blood, the *sota* water, breaking the heifer's neck, purifying a leper, the priestly blessing
**whether inside or outside** the Temple — all derived from **"among the sons of Aaron"**: any
rite *stated to the sons of Aaron*. Its coda: a priest who **does not believe** in these has
no portion in the priesthood. **The inference.** The reason is that he *does not believe* — so
one who **does** believe, **even if not expert**, keeps his share. That contradicts Rav Ḥisda
(132b), who withheld the gift from a priest not expert in all twenty-four.

**The veins of the jaw.** **Rabbi Abba said Rav Huna said Rav:** the **veins in the jaw are
forbidden** because of their blood, and the jaw-gift is **not given to a priest who does not
know how to remove them** — lest he eat the blood. **But that is not so:** any preparation
removes the blood — roasting **drains** it, and cooking after **cutting and salting** drains
it too. The concern dissolves.

**Love or contempt?** **Rava** reports that **Rav Yosef examined his students:** a priest who
**seizes** the gifts — is he showing *love* for the mitzva or *contempt* for it? **Rava
resolved it** from **"and he shall give"** (Deut. 18:3) — the owner gives, the priest **does
not take on his own**. Seizing is contempt.

**Abaye's four postures.** Abaye tells it as his own history:
- **At first** he **seized** the gifts, thinking he showed love for the mitzva.
- **Once he heard "and he shall give,"** he stopped seizing but would **say, "Give me."**
- **Once he heard** the baraita on Samuel's sons — "they turned aside after profit" (I Sam.
  8:3), which **R' Meir** reads as sinning merely by **asking for their Levite portion aloud**
  — he stopped asking too, but would **take if offered**.
- **Once he heard** that **"the modest withdraw their hands and the gluttons divide,"** he
  stopped taking even when offered — **except on Erev Yom Kippur**, when he took, **to affirm
  himself among the priests** (so his priesthood would not be forgotten).

**Why not just bless?** **Let him spread his hands** for the priestly blessing all year, and
his priesthood is known! — **His schedule constrained him** (*ansei lei idnei*): teaching his
students kept him from the synagogue at the hour the blessing was said.

**Assigning gifts to a poor scholar.** **Rav Yosef:** a priest with a hard-pressed **Torah
scholar** in his neighborhood may **assign** his gifts to him — **even before they reach his
own hand** — provided he is one of the **acquaintances of priesthood and Levites**, with a
standing arrangement that makes the gifts as good as his.

**The calf, the tongue, and the dream.** **Rava and Rav Safra** visited **Mar Yoḥana son of
Rav Ḥana bar Adda** (some say **bar Bizna**), who served a prime calf. **Rava** told the host's
priest-attendant: **assign us the gifts, for I wish to eat tongue with mustard.** He assigned
them; **Rava ate, Rav Safra did not.** In a dream they read to Rav Safra: **"As one who removes
a garment on a cold day, as vinegar on natron, so is one who sings songs to a heavy heart"**
(Prov. 25:20). Rav Safra feared he had **transgressed Rav Yosef's ruling** by declining. **Rav
Yosef reassured him:** my leniency was for a priest who assigns to **another of his own choice**,
not an **attendant** who assigns **under his host's pressure** — and it was for one who **cannot
afford** the meat otherwise, whereas **Rava could**. **Then why the dream?** It was aimed at
**Rava**. *Then read it to Rava!* — **Rava was under reproach** (*nazuf*) and received no
heavenly message, so it came to Rav Safra instead.

**And the plain meaning?** **Abaye asked Rav Dimi** what the verse means simply. **About one who
teaches an unworthy student.** So too **Rav Yehuda said Rav:** one who teaches an unworthy
student **falls into Gehenna** (from Job 20:26, where *sarid* means a Torah scholar); and
**Rabbi Zeira said Rav:** it is like **throwing a stone at Markulis** — an act of worship for
that idol (Prov. 26:8).

**Into the mishna — marking a partnership.** The mishna required one who partners with a priest
or gentile to **mark** the animal. **Even with a gentile?** A baraita seems to disagree:
partnership **with a priest** must be marked, but **with a gentile, and disqualified consecrated
animals, need not** — so how can our mishna demand marking with a gentile?

### 133b — the money-box, the twenty-four gifts, and the obligation

**Where does he sit?** The resolution turns on visibility. The baraita (no mark needed) speaks
of a gentile **sitting at the butcher's block** — plainly a partner. **But a priest sitting
there** is no proof; onlookers say **"he is buying meat."** *Then say the same of the gentile!*
— **Rather**, the gentile sits **at the money-box** (*kasfeta*). **But a priest at the box** is
no proof; **"the butcher trusts him"** to guard it. *Say the same of the gentile!* — **There is
no such trust in a gentile.** Or: **an ordinary gentile shouts** at the seller over prices, so
his partnership is obvious; a priest, being modest, defers — so his must be **marked**.

**Why is a redeemed offering obvious?** The baraita said disqualified consecrated animals **need
no mark** — apparently their status is evident. **But** a mishna (Bekhorot 31a) has them **sold,
slaughtered, and weighed** in the market like ordinary meat! **Rav Adda bar Ahava** explained
before **Rav Pappa:** the baraita means those **sold at home** — a **firstborn and a tithe**,
whose redemption value has no sanctity, and which the Sages therefore barred from the market;
selling them at home is itself the visible sign.

**Partner in one limb.** **Rav Huna:** a partner **in the head** is exempt only **from the
jaw**; **in the leg**, only **from the foreleg**; **in the innards**, only **from the maw** —
obligated in the rest. **Ḥiyya bar Rav:** a partner **in even one** of them is exempt **from all
three**. A baraita ("the head is mine and the rest yours — even a hundredth of the head, he is
exempt") is pressed against Ḥiyya bar Rav, and a second, explicit baraita — **exempt from the
jaw but obligated in all the rest** — settles it: **the refutation of Ḥiyya bar Rav is
conclusive** (*teyuvta*).

**What misled him.** **Rav Ḥisda:** Ḥiyya bar Rav was misled by the baraita of the **twenty-four
priestly gifts** — all given to Aaron and his sons by **a generalization and a detail** and **a
covenant of salt**, so that fulfilling or violating one is as if for all. Its list:
- **Ten in the Temple** — a sin offering; a bird sin offering; a definite guilt offering; a
  provisional guilt offering; communal peace offerings; the leper's *log* of oil; the two loaves;
  the shewbread; the leftovers of meal offerings; and the *omer* offering.
- **Four in Jerusalem** — the firstborn; the first fruits; the portions raised from the thanks
  offering and the nazirite's ram; and the hides of offerings.
- **Ten in the boundaries** — *teruma*; *teruma* of the tithe; *ḥalla*; the first shearing; the
  gifts (foreleg, jaw, maw); redemption of the firstborn son; redemption of a firstborn donkey;
  an ancestral field; a dedicated field; and the robbery of a convert.

Ḥiyya bar Rav thought that because the tanna **counts foreleg, jaw, and maw as one**, they *are*
one indivisible gift. **Not so:** the tanna also counts the thanks-offering portions and the
nazirite's ram as one — not because they are one, but because they are **similar**. So too the
three gifts are grouped for **similarity**, not fused into one obligation.

**Whose is the head?** A dilemma: a priest sells the butcher the **head**, keeping the rest —
**"the head is yours and the rest is mine."** Do **we follow the obligation** (the limb bearing
the jaw, now the Israelite's — so he is obligated), or the **principal of the animal** (still the
priest's — so exempt)? **Come and hear:** one who buys a gentile's **wool** before shearing is
**exempt** from the first shearing — **and this is a stringency in the foreleg, jaw, and maw**:
one who buys a limb from a priest before slaughter **is** obligated in its gift, even though it
is not the animal's principal. **Conclude: we follow the obligation.**

**A word of retention.** The mishna's close: if the seller said **"except for the gifts,"** the
buyer is **exempt** — they were never sold to him (the thread that opens tomorrow's daf).

## The distinctions, side by side

The sharpest argument on the page is Rav Huna against Ḥiyya bar Rav — how far a partnership in
one part of the animal reaches. The Gemara settles it decisively for Rav Huna.

| If a priest or gentile is a partner in… | Rav Huna (the halakha) | Ḥiyya bar Rav (refuted) |
|---|---|---|
| the head | exempt from the **jaw** only | exempt from **all three** |
| the leg | exempt from the **foreleg** only | exempt from **all three** |
| the innards | exempt from the **maw** only | exempt from **all three** |

## One line to carry with you

> The gift is real either way — the question the daf will not let go of is the hand that takes
> it. Seize it and you scorn the mitzva; wait for it to be given and the same gift becomes an
> honor. The modest withdraw their hands.
