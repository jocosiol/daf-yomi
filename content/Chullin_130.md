## The big picture (read this first)

A new chapter, **הַזְּרוֹעַ וְהַלְּחָיַיִם** — *the foreleg and the jaw* — and a new subject: not what may be
eaten, but what must be **given away**. When an ordinary (non-sacred) animal is slaughtered, three cuts belong
to the kohen — the **foreleg**, the **jaw** (with its cheeks and tongue), and the **maw** (the fourth stomach).
The opening mishna fixes their scope with four sharp contrasts: these gifts apply *in the Land and outside it*,
*in Temple times and after*, and *to non-sacred animals — but not to sacrificial ones*.

That last line is the daf's engine. It feels backwards. Sacrificial animals already surrender their **breast
and thigh** (*chazeh v'shok*) to the kohen; non-sacred animals surrender nothing of the sort. So if the lesser
category (non-sacred) owes the gifts, surely the greater (sacrificial) should owe them *too* — a textbook
**kal vaḥomer**. The mishna blocks it with a verse, and the Gemara then spends the whole first amud testing
whether the verse is really needed, or whether the *kal vaḥomer* can be refuted on its own.

> Every "obvious" a fortiori hides an assumption. The Gemara's method here is to hunt for the one feature that
> makes non-sacred animals special — and each time it finds one, to ask whether some *other* animal already
> lacks it and still owes the gifts.

Then the mirror image: now that sacrificial animals *keep* their breast and thigh and non-sacred ones owe the
gifts, could non-sacred animals owe the breast and thigh too? Another verse — **"זֶה"**, *this* and nothing
else — shuts it. And once we have that spare word "this," the Gemara asks what *else* it teaches, landing on
**Rav Ḥisda's** striking ruling: one who **destroys or eats** the priestly gifts before handing them over owes
the kohen **nothing** — because they are "money that has no claimants." The rest of the daf is a barrage of
sources testing whether that can really be true.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **זְרוֹעַ, לְחָיַיִם, קֵבָה** *(zeroa, lechayayim, keivah)* | **The foreleg, the jaw, and the maw** — the three gifts of the priesthood owed from every non-sacred slaughtered animal (Deut. 18:3). |
| **מַתְּנוֹת כְּהוּנָּה** *(matnot kehuna)* | **The priestly gifts.** On this daf, specifically the foreleg, jaw and maw — as against the breast and thigh, which come only from sacrifices. |
| **חָזֶה וָשׁוֹק** *(chazeh v'shok)* | The **breast and thigh**, waved and given to the kohen from peace-offerings (*shelamim*). Owed only from sacrificial animals, never from non-sacred ones. |
| **קַל וָחוֹמֶר** *(kal vaḥomer)* | An **a fortiori** inference. The daf's whole first amud is one long attempt to build — and then to refute — such an inference. |
| **אֵין לוֹ אֶלָּא מַה שֶּׁאָמוּר בָּעִנְיָן** *(ein lo ela ma she-amur ba-inyan)* | **"The kohen has only what the passage states"** — the breast and thigh from sacrifices, and no more. The verse's own limitation. |
| **נִכְנָס לַדִּיר לְהִתְעַשֵּׂר** *(nichnas la-dir l'hit'aser)* | **"Enters the pen to be tithed"** — subject to the animal tithe (*ma'aser behema*). The recurring "unique feature" the Gemara keeps invoking to block the *kal vaḥomer*. |
| **מוּם קָבוּעַ / מוּם עוֹבֵר** *(mum kavu'a / mum over)* | A **permanent** vs. a **temporary blemish.** Whether the blemish came before or after consecration decides everything in the mishna's two lists. |
| **תְּנוּפָה** *(tenufa)* | **Waving** — the rite the breast and thigh require. Since it can only be done "before the Lord," it cannot be performed on a non-sacred animal at all. |
| **מַזִּיק מַתְּנוֹת כְּהוּנָּה** *(mazik matnot kehuna)* | **One who damages the priestly gifts** (or eats them) before giving them over. Rav Ḥisda's ruling: he is exempt from paying. |
| **מָמוֹן שֶׁאֵין לוֹ תּוֹבְעִים** *(mamon she-ein lo tovim)* | **"Money that has no claimants."** Since the owner may give the gifts to *any* kohen, no single kohen can sue for them — one reason for Rav Ḥisda's exemption. |
| **לְהוֹצִיאָן / לְחוֹלְקָן בְּדַיָּינִין** *(l'hotzian / l'cholkan b'dayanin)* | To **extract** the gifts through the court vs. merely to **distribute** them through the court. The whole dispute over Rav Ḥisda turns on which of these the sources mean. |
| **מַתָּנוֹת שֶׁלֹּא הוּרְמוּ כְּמִי שֶׁהוּרְמוּ** *(matanot she-lo hurmu k'mi she-hurmu)* | **"Gifts not yet separated are as though separated"** — one tanna's view, by which the gifts already belong to the kohen even before they are set aside. |
| **מִדַּת חֲסִידוּת** *(middat chasidut)* | **An attribute of piety** — behavior beyond the strict law. Rav Ḥisda's escape hatch: where a source seems to make one pay, he reads it as pious practice, not obligation. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira** — second-generation Tanna who lived in **Netzivin** (Nisibis), in Babylonia;
  here the author of the baraita reading *mishpat* ("judgment") into the verse, which the Gemara turns against
  Rav Ḥisda.
- **Rabbi Eliezer** (ben Hyrcanus) — second-generation Tanna, student of Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai; in the
  *Pe'a* mishna, the one who rules that a traveling homeowner who took gifts of the poor **repays** them. His
  view is famously *not* the accepted law, which Rava uses to blunt the objection.
- The **Sages** (*Chachamim*) — the majority voice in that same *Pe'a* mishna, who exempt the poor traveler
  because "he was poor at that time."

**Amoraim**

- **Rav Ḥisda** — third-generation Babylonian Amora of **Sura**, student of Rav; the central figure of the
  second half — his ruling that damaging or eating the gifts carries no liability drives the rest of the daf.
- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation head of **Maḥoza**; defends and sharpens Rav Ḥisda,
  dismissing the *Pe'a* objection ("the tanna taught *he pays* — and you call it piety?!").
- **Rabbi Yonatan** (ben Elazar) — first-to-second-generation Eretz-Yisrael Amora; source of the teaching that
  one does **not** give a priestly gift to a kohen who is an *am ha'aretz*.
- **Rav Shmuel bar Naḥmani** — second-to-third-generation Eretz-Yisrael Amora and celebrated aggadist; the
  tradent who transmits Rabbi Yonatan's teaching. (Note the pairing: **Rav Shmuel bar Naḥmani said Rabbi
  Yonatan said** — the transmitter, then his master.)

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 130a — The mishna: three gifts, and why sacrifices are spared

The chapter opens: the **foreleg, jaw and maw** are given to the kohen from non-sacred animals, and the mishna
brackets their reach — *in the Land and outside*, *with the Temple and without*, but **to non-sacred animals
and not to sacrificial ones.**

Why spell out the exemption for sacrifices? Because reason pulls the other way — *"שֶׁהָיָה בַּדִּין,"* it would
have been right to infer it by **kal vaḥomer**:

> If **non-sacred** animals, which owe *no* breast and thigh, nonetheless owe the gifts — then **sacrificial**
> animals, which *do* owe the breast and thigh, should surely owe the gifts too!

So a verse is required to block it: *"וָאֶתֵּן אֹתָם לְאַהֲרֹן"* — "and I have given **them** to Aaron" (Lev.
7:34). The word **"them"** (*otam*) restricts the kohen to *"מַה שֶּׁאָמוּר בָּעִנְיָן"* — only what that passage
names, the breast and thigh, and nothing more from sacrifices.

### 130a — The mishna's two lists: the redeemed blemished animal

Before the Gemara, the mishna adds a paired ruling about consecrated animals that were later redeemed, split
by **when the blemish appeared**. Where a **permanent blemish preceded** consecration, only the animal's
*value* was ever sanctified; once redeemed it reverts almost fully to non-sacred status. Where **consecration
came first** (or a temporary blemish preceded it and a permanent one followed), the sanctity had already taken
hold in the body, and redemption leaves heavy traces. The six tests that separate them are laid out in the
grid below.

### 130a — The *kal vaḥomer* chase: could sacrifices owe the gifts after all?

The Gemara reads the mishna's inference precisely: the *reason* sacrifices are exempt is only *"טַעְמָא דִּכְתַב
רַחֲמָנָא אוֹתָם"* — because the verse wrote "them." **Without** that word, the *kal vaḥomer* would have stood.
Really? The Gemara now tries to **refute the *kal vaḥomer* on its own** — for if it collapses, the verse is
doing no work. Watch the pattern: each time it names the feature that makes non-sacred animals "special," it
answers by finding another animal that lacks that feature yet still owes the gifts.

- **Refute it:** what is unique about non-sacred animals? They owe the **firstborn** (*bechora*), which
  sacrifices do not. So the inference is not airtight.
- **Then derive from non-sacred *males*** — they never bear young, owe no firstborn, yet owe the gifts. *What's
  unique about males?* They owe the **first shearing** (*reishit hagez*).
- **Then from male goats** — exempt from firstborn *and* first shearing, yet owe the gifts. *What's unique?*
  They **enter the pen to be tithed** (*ma'aser behema*).
- **Then from old male goats** — which have *already* passed through the pen. *What's unique?* They too
  **entered the pen to be tithed.**
- **Then from a purchased or orphaned animal** — never itself subject to the animal tithe. *What's unique?*
  Animals **of their type** enter the pen to be tithed.

**And there the wall cracks.** *"בְּמִינָן קָאָמְרַתְּ?"* — you rest it on *their type* entering the pen? But
sacrificial animals also have non-sacred animals **of their type** that enter the pen! So the *kal vaḥomer*
holds after all — and *only* the verse "them" keeps sacrifices out of the gifts.

### 130a–b — The mirror question: could non-sacred animals owe breast and thigh?

Now the inference runs the other direction. Since sacrifices are exempt from the gifts yet owe breast and
thigh, then non-sacred animals, which owe the gifts, should owe **breast and thigh** by *kal vaḥomer*. The
answer is a second limiting word: *"וְזֶה יִהְיֶה מִשְׁפַּט הַכֹּהֲנִים"* — **"this"** (*zeh*) — foreleg, jaw and
maw, **yes**; anything else, **no**.

But wait — that implies that *without* "this," non-sacred animals *would* owe breast and thigh. Yet breast and
thigh require **waving** (*tenufa*), and where could a non-sacred animal be waved? **Outside** the Temple is
impossible — the verse says the waving is *"לִפְנֵי ה׳,"* before the Lord (130b). **Inside** is impossible too —
you would be dragging a non-sacred animal into the **courtyard**. Since the rite simply cannot be performed,
no verse is needed to exempt non-sacred animals from breast and thigh.

### 130b — So why "this"? Rav Ḥisda's exemption

If the word isn't needed for *that*, what is *"זֶה"* teaching? **Rav Ḥisda's** ruling:

> *"הַמַּזִּיק מַתְּנוֹת כְּהוּנָּה אוֹ שֶׁאֲכָלָן — פָּטוּר מִלְּשַׁלֵּם."* One who **damages** the priestly gifts,
> or **eats** them before handing them over, is **exempt from paying** the kohen.

"This" — only these very items, not their monetary value, is what the kohen may claim. Examining the ruling
itself (*gufa*), the Gemara offers **two possible reasons**: either the exclusive word "this," or because the
gifts are *"מָמוֹן שֶׁאֵין לוֹ תּוֹבְעִים"* — **money with no claimants**, since the owner may hand them to any
kohen and so none can personally sue.

### 130b — The sources pile on: are the gifts "money with claimants"?

A run of challenges now tests whether the gifts can, in fact, be pried out in court:

- **A baraita:** *"מִשְׁפַּט הַכֹּהֲנִים"* teaches the gifts are a *din*, a **judgment** — for what? Not for
  **extracting** them through judges (which would give the kohen a claim, against Rav Ḥisda)? **No** — only for
  **distributing** them through judges, i.e. the court directs the owner to which *kind* of kohen to give. This
  dovetails with **Rabbi Yonatan's** rule (via Rav Shmuel bar Naḥmani): one does **not** give a gift to a kohen
  who is an *am ha'aretz* — *"לְמַעַן יֶחֶזְקוּ בְּתוֹרַת ה׳,"* only those who hold fast to Torah have a portion.
- **Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira's baraita:** "this" teaches that only foreleg/jaw/maw are a "judgment," not breast
  and thigh. For what? If for *distributing* — but breast and thigh are distributed by judges too! So it must
  be for **extracting** — contradicting Rav Ḥisda. The Gemara answers: the baraita speaks of gifts that
  **already reached the kohen's hand while still untithed** (*b'tivlaihu*), and this tanna holds *"מַתָּנוֹת
  שֶׁלֹּא הוּרְמוּ כְּמִי שֶׁהוּרְמוּ,"* unseparated gifts count as separated — so they are already his, and taking
  them back is theft.
- **The *Pe'a* mishna:** a homeowner traveling and out of funds may take gleanings and poor-tithe, and **"when
  he returns he repays"** — Rabbi Eliezer. So one *can* be made to pay for gifts he never held! **Rav Ḥisda:**
  that is only *middat chasidut*, an attribute of piety. **Rava** objects sharply: the tanna said *he pays* —
  and you call it piety? And besides, Rabbi Eliezer's view isn't the law to begin with. **Rather** the
  objection is from the *seifa*: the Sages exempt him *because he was poor at that time* — implying a *wealthy*
  traveler **would** pay. Why should he? — let it be like one who damages the gifts! Rav Ḥisda holds firm:
  even then, it is **piety**, not law.
- **A final baraita:** a homeowner who eats his own **untithed** produce, or a Levi who eats his untithed
  tithes, is **exempt** — *"אֵין לְךָ בָּהֶן אֶלָּא מִשְּׁעַת הֲרָמָה,"* the kohen has rights only from the moment
  of separation onward. This implies that *after* separation he **would** pay — why, if damaging the gifts
  carries no liability? — and the daf breaks off mid-answer (*"הָכָא נָמֵי,"* here too...), to be resolved on 131.

## The distinctions, side by side

The heart of the first amud is the **kal vaḥomer chase**: each attempt to prove that sacrificial animals owe
the gifts is met by naming one feature the proposed source has and sacrifices lack — until that feature turns
out to describe sacrifices too, and the verse must do the work instead. Every row traces to the walkthrough.

| Try to derive "sacrifices owe the gifts" from… | The unique feature that blocks it |
|---|---|
| **Non-sacred animals** | They owe the **firstborn** |
| **Non-sacred males** | They owe the **first shearing** |
| **Male goats** | They **enter the pen to be tithed** |
| **Old male goats** | They **already entered the pen to be tithed** |
| **Purchased / orphaned animals** | Animals **of their type** enter the pen to be tithed |
| *…and the rebuttal* | Sacrifices **also** have non-sacred animals of their type that enter the pen — so only the verse "them" exempts them |

---

## One line to carry with you

> A *kal vaḥomer* is only as strong as the difference it ignores. The Gemara refuses to let the verse do easy
> work: it presses the inference through every animal it can, and only when no difference is left standing does
> it concede that the exemption of sacrifices rests on Scripture alone.
