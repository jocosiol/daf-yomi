## The big picture (read this first)

Today's page reads like grammar. The chapter is asking, over and over, one structural question about food and
impurity: **when is a part joined to food treated as the food itself?** The mishna it leans on (from *Okatzin*)
gives three answers, and the entire daf is the Gemara sourcing them in the verses.

A part of food is one of three things:

> A **handle** (*yad*) — the stalk or bone you hold but do not eat. A **cover** (*shomer*) — the husk, shell,
> or hide that guards the food. Or **neither** (like hair). A handle **imports** impurity into the food and
> **exports** it out to whatever it touches, but does **not join** the food to make up the requisite measure.
> A cover does **all three**. Neither-nor does nothing.

The work of the daf is to find each of those rules in the Torah — and here is the twist that drives the whole
sugya: once you have the verse for a **handle**, a **cover** follows *a fortiori* (*kal vachomer*), since a
cover is only more attached than a handle, not less. So every verse the Torah spends on a cover, or a *third*
verse on a handle, looks like one too many — and the Gemara's game becomes: **which verse is the spare, and
what does a spare verse teach?**

Four movements, in order:

1. **The hide as a handle** (118a). Continuing 117b: touch the back of a hide with an olive-bulk of flesh
   behind it and you are impure — the hide is the flesh's *handle*.
2. **Where handles are written** (118a). "For you" gives handles for food and for a carcass; the oven gives a
   third. Which of the three is superfluous, and why none can simply be dropped.
3. **The spare verse, spent twice** (118a–b). *Im eino inyan* — the carcass's cover verse, not needed for a
   cover, is cast onto a handle; and Rav Ḥaviva explains why it lands on a handle and not on a cover.
4. **Rav against R' Yochanan, twice** (118b). Is there a handle for *susceptibility* as well as impurity? And
   how little food behind a handle or cover still counts — an olive-bulk, a bean-bulk, or less?

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **יָד** *(yad)* | A **handle** — the part of a food you grasp but do not eat, like a stalk or a bone with meat on its tip. It imports and exports impurity, but does not join the food toward the requisite measure. |
| **שׁוֹמֵר** *(shomer)* | A **protective cover** — a husk, shell, or hide that guards the food. It does everything a handle does **and** joins the food to make up the measure. |
| **מַכְנִיס וּמוֹצִיא** *(machnis u'motzi)* | **Import and export.** A handle "brings impurity in" to the attached food, and "takes impurity out" from impure food to whatever else it touches. |
| **מִצְטָרֵף** *(mitztaref)* | **Joins** — combines with the food to reach the measure that transmits impurity (an egg-bulk for food-impurity). A cover joins; a handle does not. |
| **לָכֶם** *(lachem)* | "**For you**," i.e. for all your needs (Lev. 11:38–39). The word that folds handles into the law — anything you need for using the food is treated as the food. |
| **קַל וָחוֹמֶר** *(kal vachomer)* | An ***a fortiori*** inference. Here it carries the cover's rules for free from the handle's: if the lesser handle imports and exports, the greater cover surely does. |
| **דַּיּוֹ** *(dayo)* | "**It is enough.**" A conclusion drawn by inference is no stronger than its source — the reason the carcass's own handle-verse cannot be spared. |
| **טָרַח וְכָתַב לַהּ קְרָא** *(tarach v'katav lah kra)* | "**The verse takes the trouble to write it.**" Sometimes Scripture states outright what *kal vachomer* could have derived — one way to explain a "spare" verse. |
| **הֶכְשֵׁר** *(hechsher)* | **Rendering susceptible** — food becomes able to contract impurity only after contact with liquid. The question: does a handle work for this too, or only for impurity itself? |
| **מִקְרָא נִדְרָשׁ לְפָנָיו** *(mikra nidrash l'fanav)* | "**A verse is expounded from the phrase before it**" — and, for Rav, *only* the phrase before it, not the one two back. R' Yochanan expounds from both. |
| **כְּזַיִת / כְּפוֹל** *(kezayit / k'pol)* | An **olive-bulk / a bean-bulk**. Rav's minimums: a handle counts only behind an olive-bulk of food, a cover only behind a bean-bulk. |
| **פִּיטְמָא / נֵץ** *(pitma / netz)* | A pomegranate's **protrusion** (a cover, which joins) versus its **blossom** (a cover *over* a cover, which does not) — the objection that opens the last sugya. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Yehuda ben Nekosa** — a Tanna who transmits in the name of Rabbi Yaakov; his challenge closes the daf:
  how can two bones, each carrying only half an olive-bulk of flesh, ever join to a full olive-bulk?
- **Rabbi Yaakov** — the Tanna in whose name Yehuda ben Nekosa speaks. Several Tannaim bear the name; which
  one is meant here I would not state with confidence.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav** (Abba Arikha) — first-generation Amora, disciple of Rebbi and founder of the academy at Sura; holds
  there is no handle for *susceptibility*, and no handle for less than an olive-bulk.
- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation Amora of Meḥoza; declares the opening baraita defective
  (*chaseurei michasra*) and supplies the reading that makes it cohere. (**Rava** רבא — not **Rabbah** רבה,
  the earlier Pumbeditan.)
- **Rav Ḥiyya bar Ashi** — an early Amora and a leading transmitter of Rav's teachings; reports Rav's ruling
  that there is a handle for impurity but not for susceptibility. (**Rav Ḥiyya bar Ashi** — not **Rav Ashi**,
  the much later redactor, nor **Rabbi Ḥiyya** the Tanna.)
- **Rav Huna, son of Rav Yehoshua** — fourth-to-fifth-generation Amora, close colleague of Rav Papa; parries
  one objection by comparing produce not yet rendered susceptible to an oven not yet finished — neither is
  susceptible to impurity, so the leniency proves nothing unique.
- **Rav Ḥaviva** — a Babylonian Amora; explains that the carcass's spare cover-verse lands on a *handle*
  rather than a cover, since it does a handle's work (import/export) and not a cover's (joining). More than one
  Amora carries this name; I would not pin down which without more to go on.
- **Rav Yehuda bar Yishmael** — a Babylonian Amora; objects from the pomegranate and from the chapter's own
  opening mishna, forcing the three-verb derivation. A minor figure about whom I can say little with certainty.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Yoḥanan** (bar Nafcha) — second-generation Amora and head of the Tiberias academy; holds against Rav
  on both disputes — a handle *does* render food susceptible, and it counts even behind less than an olive-bulk.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 118a — The hide as the flesh's handle

**Continuing from 117b.** A baraita reads the verse *"בְּנִבְלָתָהּ"*, "its carcass" (Lev. 11:39), to teach
that the **hide** of a carcass with **less than an olive-bulk of flesh** on it does not convey carcass-impurity,
and the hide does not join to complete the olive-bulk. But then — **one might have thought** that even a hide
*with* an olive-bulk of flesh behind it does not make impure someone who touches only its outer, flesh-less
side. **Therefore the verse states *"יִטְמָא"*, "shall be impure":** the hide is a **handle** for the flesh, so
touching its back conveys impurity as though you had touched the flesh itself.

**The Okatzin mishna, restated.** *"תְּנַן הָתָם"* — the three categories are laid out. Whatever is a **handle
and not a cover** is *tamei u'metamei v'eino mitztaref* — imports impurity, exports it, but does **not join**.
Whatever is a **cover, even if not a handle**, imports, exports, **and joins**. Whatever is **neither handle
nor cover** (like hair) neither imports nor exports.

### 118a — Where are handles written?

**The two base verses.** *"יָדוֹת הֵיכָא כְּתִיבִי?"* — where are handles in the Torah? From *"לָכֶם"*, "for
you," said of food-impurity (Lev. 11:38): *for all your needs* includes the **handles** — a handle **imports**
impurity into the food. And from *"לָכֶם"* said of a carcass (Lev. 11:39): a handle **exports** impurity out
of a carcass to whoever touches it. So a handle both imports and exports.

**A cover needs no verse of its own** — *kal vachomer* from a handle: **if a handle, which does not even
protect the food, imports and exports, then a cover, which does protect, surely does.** So the verse the Torah
*does* spend on a cover must be teaching that a cover **joins** the food to the measure — *sh'ma minah l'tzaref.*

**The Gemara tests the assignment.** *"וְאֵימָא"* — say instead: a handle imports but does not export; a cover
imports and exports; but a handle does not export and a cover does not join. **Rejected:** *"הַשְׁתָּא עַיּוֹלֵי
מְעַיְּילָא, אַפּוֹקֵי מִיבַּעְיָא?!"* — now that a handle *imports*, need you even ask whether it *exports*? If
it can bring impurity in, all the more can it carry impurity out.

**A third handle-verse.** Try the reverse — a handle exports but does not import. This forces the Gemara to
note **an extra handle** written for **vessels**: *"תַּנּוּר וְכִירַיִם יוּתָּץ… לָכֶם"* (Lev. 11:35), "for
you" again including handles. So there are **three** derivations of a handle — food, oven, carcass.

### 118a — Which of the three is the spare?

**Each is protected by its own stringency.** *"הֵי מִינַּיְיהוּ מְיַיתַּר?"* — none of the three can be derived
from any single other, because each carries a *chumra* the others lack:

- **Seeds/food** — *טוּמְאָתָן מְרוּבָּה*, their impurity is greater (susceptible even to second-degree).
- **Oven/vessels** — *מְטַמֵּא מֵאֲוִירוֹ*, transmits through its **airspace**, not only by contact.
- **Carcass** — *מְטַמְּאָה אָדָם, וּבְמַשָּׂא, וְטוּמְאָה יוֹצְאָה מִגּוּפָהּ* — makes a **person** impure,
  makes one **carrying** it impure, and its impurity **emanates from its own body**.

**But one can be derived from the other two.** *"חֲדָא מֵחֲדָא לָא אָתְיָא, תֵּיתֵי חֲדָא מִתַּרְתֵּי"* — no one
from any single one, yet one from the *common ground* of the pair. Which one? Testing each shows the
**carcass** verse is the derivable — and therefore spare — one.

### 118a — The spare verse, spent (first pass)

**But the carcass's handle cannot simply be dropped — *dayo*.** *"דַּיּוֹ לַבָּא מִן הַדִּין לִהְיוֹת
כַּנִּדּוֹן"* — a rule inferred by the common-ground method is no stronger than its sources. Since the handles
of food and oven do **not** make a *person* impure, an inferred carcass-handle would not either — yet a
carcass's handle *does* reach a person. So the carcass **handle** verse is needed for itself.

**Then it is the carcass's *cover* that is spare.** A cover of a carcass doesn't join (we learned that from
"its carcass"), and its importing/exporting comes by *kal vachomer* from a handle — so its verse is free.
*"אִם אֵין עִנְיָן לְשׁוֹמֵר דִּנְבֵלָה, תְּנֵהוּ עִנְיָן לְיָד"* — **if it is not needed for a cover, apply it
to a handle** — first to the carcass's handle, and if not needed there, to *handles in general*, yielding **yad
l'hachnis, yad l'hotzi, shomer l'tzaref.**

### 118b — Why the spare lands on a handle, not a cover

**The objection.** *"וְאֵימָא"* — why cast the spare verse onto a **handle**? Cast it onto a **cover** in
general instead: one cover-verse to import, one to join — leaving **no source at all** that a handle imports.

**The first answer.** *"מֵעִיקָּרָא כִּי כְּתִיבָא יָד, אַהַכְנָסָה כְּתִיבָא"* — a handle's importing was never
resting on the spare verse. The *original* food-verse is written about impurity **entering** the food (the
sheretz falling on it), so a handle imports from there; the rest follows *a fortiori*.

**Then why the carcass's cover-verse at all?** *"לְגוּפֵיהּ"* — for itself, to teach that a cover applies to a
carcass in the first place — and *"מִילְּתָא דְּאָתְיָא בְּקַל וָחוֹמֶר טָרַח וְכָתַב לַהּ קְרָא"*, the Torah
sometimes writes out what *kal vachomer* could have supplied. But *"הֵיכָא דְּאִיכָּא לְמִידְרַשׁ, דָּרְשִׁינַן"*
— where a fresh teaching can be squeezed out, we prefer it to a merely redundant verse.

**Rav Ḥaviva's answer.** *"שָׁאנֵי שׁוֹמֵר דִּנְבֵלָה… אַיָּד שָׁדֵינַן לֵיהּ"* — the carcass's cover is
special: since it does a **handle's** work (imports and exports but does *not* join), we cast its spare verse
onto a **handle**, not onto a cover. The original derivation stands.

### 118b — Is there a handle for hechsher?

**Rav Yehuda bar Yishmael's objection, and the three verbs.** From *Okatzin*: a pomegranate's **protrusion**
joins but its **blossom** does not — yet neither is "sown" with the fruit, so how does even the protrusion
join? And the chapter's own mishna has hide, gravy, and bones joining, though none is sown. **The answer:**
three cognate words — *"עַל כׇּל זֶרַע זֵרוּעַ אֲשֶׁר יִזָּרֵעַ"* — teach a cover for **seeds**, for **trees**,
and for **meat, eggs, and fish**. Joining is not limited to what is literally sown.

**The first Rav–R' Yoḥanan dispute.** *"יֵשׁ יָד לְטוּמְאָה, וְאֵין יָד לְהֶכְשֵׁר"* — **Rav** (via Rav Ḥiyya
bar Ashi): a handle works to convey **impurity**, but not to render the food **susceptible** through liquid.
**R' Yoḥanan:** a handle works for **both**. Their split is either *sevara* — is *hechsher* the opening stage
of impurity or not — or *kra*: **Rav** holds *"מִקְרָא נִדְרָשׁ לְפָנָיו וְלֹא לִפְנֵי פָנָיו"* (expound only
from the phrase immediately before, where impurity sits, not two phrases back, where susceptibility sits);
**R' Yoḥanan** expounds from both. A baraita is taught **like R' Yoḥanan**.

### 118b — How little food is enough?

**The second dispute.** *"אֵין יָד לְפָחוֹת מִכְּזַיִת, וְאֵין שׁוֹמֵר לְפָחוֹת מִכְּפוֹל"* — **Rav:** no
handle for less than an **olive-bulk** of food, no cover for less than a **bean-bulk**. **R' Yoḥanan:** there
*is* a handle for less than an olive-bulk, and a cover for less than a bean-bulk.

**The objection that carries into 119a.** A Tosefta: **two bones, each with half an olive-bulk** of a corpse's
flesh; insert their bare ends into a house that overlies them, and the house is impure — the bones acting as
handles. **Yehuda ben Nekosa** in the name of **Rabbi Yaakov** asks: *"הֵיאַךְ שְׁנֵי עֲצָמוֹת מִצְטָרְפִין
לִכְזַיִת?"* — how can two bones, each under an olive-bulk, ever join to a full one? The knot is untied
tomorrow.

## The distinctions, side by side

This is the mishna the whole daf is sourcing — the three kinds of attachment, tested by the three things an
attachment might do to impurity. Every verse-hunt above is just an effort to fill one of these cells.

| Attachment | Imports impurity in (*machnis*) | Exports impurity out (*motzi*) | Joins to the measure (*mitztaref*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Handle** (*yad*) — held, does not protect | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** |
| **Cover** (*shomer*) — protects, even if not held | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| **Neither** — e.g. hair | No | No | No |

---

## One line to carry with you

> The Torah needed only one verse for a handle — a cover always follows *a fortiori*, because a cover is a
> handle that also guards. So every extra verse is a gift the Gemara must spend, and the daf is the ledger of
> where each one goes.
