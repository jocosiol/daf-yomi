## The big picture (read this first)

The chapter's mishna listed the appendages that **join** meat toward the egg-bulk of food-impurity. Today
two of them — *alal* and the horns — get defined, and then the mishna's closing clause opens the daf's
real subject: an animal that has been **slaughtered but is still twitching** (*mefarkeset*).

Such an animal sits on a knife-edge. It is no longer a living animal, but it is not yet a carcass. The
Gemara's whole effort is to say *which* of the two it is — and the answer turns out to be: **neither, and
both.**

> A twitching animal *"יָצְתָה מִכְּלַל חַיָּה, וְלִכְלַל מֵתָה לֹא בָּאת"* — has **left the category of the
> living but not entered that of the dead.** So the law treats it as **dead** for one question and as
> **living** for the next, and stringently on both.

Four movements, in order:

1. **What is *alal*?** (121a). A **sinew** (R' Yochanan) or **scraps of flesh** the knife left on the hide
   (Reish Lakish) — proved from a verse in Job, from a mishna, and from when the scraps count as food.
2. **"Mere wood"** (121a). A beak, a talon, a horn — inedible in themselves; they join only at the soft,
   living base where blood still flows.
3. **The twitching animal** (121a–b). It imparts food-impurity but not carcass-impurity — and needs
   *intent* and *contact with liquid* to do even that. Why?
4. **Alive or dead?** (121b). Chizkiya against R' Yochanan, the "left the living, not yet the dead"
   resolution, and a cascade of cases where the twitching animal is read now as dead, now as living.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **אָלָל** *(alal)* | A disputed appendage in the mishna. R' Yochanan: a **sinew** (the nuchal ligament); Reish Lakish: **scraps of flesh** the flaying knife left on the hide. |
| **מַרְטְקָא** *(marteka)* | The **nuchal ligament** — the tough neck-sinew. R' Yochanan's reading of *alal*; being inedible "wood," it joins the meat but is never food on its own. |
| **בָּשָׂר שֶׁפְּלָטַתּוּ סַכִּין** *(basar shepaltatu sakin)* | **Flesh the knife left behind** on the hide during flaying. Reish Lakish's reading of *alal* — real flesh, so it can be food when one means to eat it. |
| **וְהוּא שֶׁכִּנְּסוֹ** *(v'hu shekineso)* | "**Only if he gathered it.**" Rav Huna: the scraps impart carcass-impurity only once a person collects an olive-bulk in one place — the gathering shows he counts it as food. |
| **עֵץ בְּעָלְמָא** *(etz b'alma)* | "**Mere wood**" — inedible matter. A beak, talon, or horn is *etz b'alma* except at its fleshy base, so only the base joins the meat. |
| **מְפַרְכֶּסֶת** *(mefarkeset)* | **Twitching** — an animal after a valid cut of the *simanim* but before its soul departs. The daf's central, liminal state. |
| **הֶכְשֵׁר** *(hechsher)* | **Rendering susceptible** — food contracts impurity only after contact with liquid; here the liquid must come "from another place," since the slaughter-blood does not count. |
| **מַחְשָׁבָה** *(machshava)* | **Intent** — designating the flesh as food. The twitching animal (never meant for a Jew's table) needs this before it can bear food-impurity. |
| **סוֹפוֹ לְטַמֵּא טוּמְאָה חֲמוּרָה** *(sofo l'tamei tumah chamura)* | "**Its end is to impart severe impurity.**" Food destined to become carcass-impure needs no *hechsher* — the question is whether a twitching animal qualifies. |
| **אֵבֶר מִן הַחַי** *(ever min hachai)* | A **limb from a living animal** — forbidden to all humanity (the Noahide laws). A limb severed from a twitching animal counts as such. |
| **יָצָא מִכְּלַל חַיָּה** *(yatza miklal chaya)* | "**It left the category of the living**" — but did not enter that of the dead. The formula for the twitching animal's in-between status. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Yosei** (ben Ḥalafta) — fourth-generation Tanna; his baraita explains why a kosher-bird carcass
  needs intent but no liquid — it will impart severe impurity in the throat.
- **The school of Rabbi Yishmael** (*d'vei R' Yishmael*) — its derasha on "if water is put on seed"
  (Lev. 11:38) fixes the rule that only food never destined for severe impurity needs *hechsher*.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Yoḥanan** (bar Nafcha) — head of Tiberias; *alal* is a sinew, and a twitching animal is still
  **living** with regard to its limbs.
- **Reish Lakish** (Shimon ben Lakish) — his disputant; *alal* is scraps of flesh left by the knife.
- **Rabbi Elazar** (ben Pedat) — third-generation Amora of Tiberias; identifies the fleshy part of a beak
  and talon, and raises the dilemma of a flawed slaughter (interrupting or pressing the knife).
- **Rabbi Asi** — Eretz-Yisrael Amora, colleague of Rabbi Ami; requires both intent and liquid for the
  twitching animal. (**Rabbi Asi** — not **Rav Ashi**, the much later redactor of the Talmud.)
- **Ḥizkiyya** — early Amora (son of Rabbi Ḥiyya); a twitching animal is like **dead** as to its limbs, so
  a Noahide may eat them. Disputes R' Yoḥanan.
- **Rabbi Zeira** — third-generation Amora who moved from Bavel to Eretz Yisrael; resolves the paradox
  with "it left the living but did not enter the dead."
- **Rabbi Yirmeya** — his student; presses the objection from Ḥizkiyya's own view.
- **Rabbi Avin and Rabbi Meyasha** — Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim; two answers on when the knife-scraps count as
  food. Minor figures about whom I can say little with confidence.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav Huna** — second-generation head of Sura; the scraps count only once a person gathers them, and a
  hide nullifies two half-olives of flesh upon it.
- **Rav Pappa** — fifth-generation Amora of Naresh; locates the joining part of a beak (a membrane) and a
  horn (its bloody base).
- **Rav Oshaya** — teaches the Tosefta that decides the *mefarkeset* dispute in R' Yoḥanan's favor. (More
  than one Sage bears this name; which is meant here I would not state with confidence.)
- **Rav Sheshet** — third-generation Amora, famed for mastery of baraitot; rules the twitching animal does
  not save swallowed items.
- **Abaye** (bar Kaylil) — head of Pumbedita; rules stringently on both sides — it does not save, yet
  bestiality with it is punishable.
- **Rav Idi bar Avin** and **Rav Yitzḥak bar Ashyan**, and **Rav Shmuel bar Yitzḥak** — Amoraim cited on
  cutting meat before death, and on what "valid slaughter" requires (examining the knife).

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 121a — What is *alal*?

**The dispute.** *"מַאי אָלָל?"* — **R' Yochanan:** *marteka*, the **nuchal ligament** (a sinew).
**Reish Lakish:** *basar shepaltatu sakin*, **flesh the knife left** on the hide.

**Three tests.** (1) From Job — *"רֹפְאֵי אֱלִל"*, "physicians of no value": fits a sinew, which cannot be
healed. Resolved: in the *verse* all agree it is a sinew; they dispute only the *mishna*'s *alal*. (2) From
R' Yehuda: *alal hamechunas*, if an olive-bulk is collected in one place one is liable — and **Rav Huna:**
*"וְהוּא שֶׁכִּנְּסוֹ"*, only if a person gathered it. Collecting shows he treats it as food — which fits
**flesh**, not wood. Resolved: by R' Yehuda all agree it is flesh; the dispute is by the Rabbis. (3) *Basar
shepaltatu sakin* — how is it food? If he means to eat it, it is susceptible on its own; if not, he has
nullified it! **R' Avin and R' Meyasha:** one says he means to eat *part* (uncertain which); the other says
*part* was severed by an animal (food) and part by the knife (nullified).

### 121a — "Mere wood"

**Beak and talons.** A mishna: a beak and talons become impure, transmit, and join. But a beak is *etz
b'alma*! **R' Elazar:** the *lower* beak. Still wood! **Rav Pappa:** the membrane inside, at the base of
the upper beak. **Talons** — R' Elazar: the fleshy place where they enter the flesh. **Horns** — Rav
Pappa: the base *"בִּמְקוֹם שֶׁחוֹתְכִין וְיוֹצֵא מֵהֶן דָּם"*, where they are cut and blood flows.

### 121a–b — The twitching animal

**The mishna's case.** A Jew who slaughters a **non-kosher** animal for a gentile, or a gentile who
slaughters a **kosher** animal for a Jew — while it is *mefarkeset* it imparts **food-impurity but not
carcass-impurity**.

**Rabbi Asi:** to bear food-impurity it needs *machshava* (intent that the flesh be food) **and** *hechsher*
— contact with liquid **from another place** (the slaughter-blood does not count). **The objection:** its
*end* is to impart severe (carcass) impurity, and *sofo l'tamei tumah chamura* needs no *hechsher*! The
school of R' Yishmael's derasha grounds the rule; and R' Yosei's baraita explains a kosher-bird carcass the
same way — its end is severe impurity in the throat, so it needs intent but not liquid.

**Ḥizkiyya's reason (121b).** *"הוֹאִיל וְיָכוֹל לְגוֹרְרָהּ"* — one *can* chop the animal into pieces each
under an olive-bulk, so it is **not certain** to become carcass-impure; hence it still needs *hechsher*.

### 121b — Alive or dead?

**The challenge to Ḥizkiyya.** R' Yirmeya to R' Zeira: but Ḥizkiyya holds that after a valid cut of the
*simanim*, while twitching, *"אֵינָהּ לְאֵבָרִים"* — the animal is **dead** (so a Noahide may eat its
limbs), against **R' Yochanan**, *"יֶשְׁנָהּ לְאֵבָרִים"*, it is **living**. If Ḥizkiyya calls it dead, it
should already bear carcass-impurity! **R' Zeira's resolution:** *"יָצְתָה מִכְּלַל חַיָּה, וְלִכְלַל מֵתָה
לֹא בָּאת"* — it left the living without entering the dead.

**The Tosefta (Rav Oshaya), like R' Yochanan.** A limb that separates from it is *k'poresh min hachai* —
imparts carcass-impurity and is forbidden to Noahides even after death; flesh that separates does not. Cut
only *one* siman — no food-impurity; merely stabbed — no impurity at all.

**Eating before death.** One may cut an olive-bulk from the slaughter-site, salt and rinse it well, wait
for the soul to depart, and eat — permitted to Jew and gentile alike (supporting Rav Idi bar Avin in the
name of Rav Yitzḥak bar Ashyan: this is what one does "to be healthy").

**Two more rulings.** R' Elazar's dilemma — if one *interrupted* or *pressed* the knife: the slaughter must
be valid in every respect (Rav Shmuel bar Yitzḥak: including examining the knife). And R' Zeira to Rav
Sheshet — does it **save** swallowed items from tent-impurity? **Abaye:** treat it stringently both ways —
it does **not** save (as dead), yet bestiality with it is **punishable** (as living).

## The distinctions, side by side

The whole second half turns on one paradox — a twitching animal is judged as **dead** for some laws and
**living** for others, and stringently on each. Every row traces to a ruling above.

| The question | Read as… | The ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Imparting **food-impurity** | dead — it is food | Yes, with intent and *hechsher* |
| Imparting **carcass-impurity** | living | No |
| A **limb** that separates from it | as from the living | Carcass-impurity; forbidden to Noahides |
| **Saving** swallowed items under a tent | stringently, as dead | It does **not** save them |
| **Bestiality** with it | stringently, as living | The offender is liable |

---

## One line to carry with you

> A slaughtered animal still twitching has left the world of the living and not yet reached the world of the
> dead — so the law lets it belong to whichever gives the stricter answer, dead here and living there.

- q: What is the dispute between R' Yoḥanan and Reish Lakish over the meaning of "alal"?
  opts:
    - Whether it is a sinew (the nuchal ligament) or scraps of flesh the knife left on the hide.
    - Whether it joins the meat to the measure or is instead susceptible to impurity on its own.
    - Whether it is the marrow within the bone or the gravy that congeals around the meat.
    - Whether it imparts food-impurity or the more severe impurity of an animal carcass.
  correct: a
  why: R' Yoḥanan — marteka, a sinew; Reish Lakish — basar shepaltatu sakin, flesh left by the flaying knife.
- q: How does the verse in Job ("physicians of no value") bear on the meaning of alal?
  opts:
    - It proves the mishna's alal is flesh, since only flesh can be described as healed.
    - It fits a sinew, which cannot be healed — so in the verse all agree alal is a sinew.
    - It shows alal must be collected to matter, as a physician gathers his remedies.
    - It settles nothing, since Job speaks of people and the mishna speaks of an animal.
  correct: b
  why: The phrase "physicians of no value" suits an unhealable sinew; the verse's alal is agreed, only the mishna's is disputed.
- q: What does Rav Huna add with "only if he gathered it" (v'hu shekineso)?
  opts:
    - That the scraps impart impurity only after they have come into contact with liquid.
    - That the scraps join the meat only if they still cling to the hide when flayed.
    - That the scraps count as food only once a person collects an olive-bulk in one place.
    - That the scraps are nullified unless an animal, not a knife, severed them first.
  correct: c
  why: Gathering an olive-bulk in one place shows he treats the scraps as food — achshava by collection.
- q: Why is a beak or a horn called "mere wood" (etz b'alma), and when does it nonetheless join the meat?
  opts:
    - Because it is inedible; it joins only at its soft, fleshy base where blood still flows.
    - Because it is detached from the body; it joins only once reattached to an olive-bulk.
    - Because it is a handle and not a cover; it joins only when it protects the flesh within.
    - Because it belongs to a bird; it joins only when the bird was properly slaughtered.
  correct: a
  why: The hard beak/horn is inedible wood; only the fleshy, bloody base (membrane; cut-line of the horn) joins.
- q: What does a twitching (mefarkeset) animal impart, and what does Rabbi Asi require for it?
  opts:
    - Carcass-impurity but not food-impurity, requiring only that it be stabbed rather than slaughtered.
    - Food-impurity but not carcass-impurity, requiring intent and contact with liquid from elsewhere.
    - Both food- and carcass-impurity, requiring nothing further once the simanim are cut.
    - Neither impurity at all, until its soul has fully departed and it becomes a carcass.
  correct: b
  why: Mefarkeset imparts tumat ochlin, not tumat neveilot; R' Asi requires machshava and hechsher from another place.
- q: Why might one think the twitching animal should need no hechsher, and how does Ḥizkiyya answer?
  opts:
    - Because it is already a carcass; Ḥizkiyya answers that a carcass still requires liquid.
    - Because the slaughter-blood is itself a liquid; Ḥizkiyya answers that such blood does not count.
    - Because its end is severe impurity; Ḥizkiyya answers one can chop it below an olive-bulk, so its end is uncertain.
    - Because intent alone suffices for food; Ḥizkiyya answers that intent must be joined by liquid.
  correct: c
  why: Sofo l'tumah chamura would exempt it, but one can cut it into sub-olive-bulk pieces, so severe impurity is not certain.
- q: How does R' Zeira resolve the paradox that a twitching animal is neither clearly living nor dead?
  opts:
    - It has left the category of the living but has not yet entered that of the dead.
    - It is fully alive until the moment its soul departs, and only then a carcass.
    - It is fully dead from the moment the simanim are cut, and never again living.
    - It is living for Torah law but dead for the Noahide laws, and never both at once.
  correct: a
  why: Yatza miklal chaya ul'klal meta lo ba — the liminal status that lets each law take the stricter side.
- q: A limb that separates from a twitching animal is treated how?
  opts:
    - Like flesh from a carcass — it imparts food-impurity but is permitted to gentiles.
    - Like a limb from a living animal — it imparts carcass-impurity and is forbidden to Noahides.
    - Like an ordinary olive-bulk of meat — it joins the measure but conveys no impurity.
    - Like a sinew — it is mere wood, so it neither imparts impurity nor is forbidden.
  correct: b
  why: K'poresh min hachai — carcass-impurity and forbidden to Noahides as ever min hachai, even after death.
- q: How does Abaye rule on whether the twitching animal saves swallowed items and on bestiality with it?
  opts:
    - It saves the items and bestiality is exempt, treating it leniently as still living throughout.
    - It does not save the items and bestiality is exempt, treating it as dead for both questions.
    - It saves the items and bestiality is punishable, treating it as living for both questions.
    - It does not save the items yet bestiality is punishable, treating it stringently on each.
  correct: d
  why: Stringent both ways — as dead it does not save, and as living one who has relations with it is liable.
