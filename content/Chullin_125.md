## The big picture (read this first)

Yesterday's mishna laid out the **thigh bone** (*kulit*) — the marrow-filled femur — of four different
sources, and asked a simple-looking pair of questions of each: **sealed or perforated?** and **by which
route does it defile?** Today's daf takes the one clause that looks easiest — a **corpse's** thigh bone, which
defiles by touch whether sealed or open — and shows it is the hardest.

The engine of the whole page is a single inference and the trouble it makes:

> The mishna says one who **touches** a corpse-bone is impure. *Touches* — yes. *Overlies* (is under the
> same roof) — **no**. But a corpse's bone should defile in a tent! So what exactly is in this bone that it
> defiles by contact but not overhead?

Peeling that back forces a chain of narrowings — no flesh on it, no olive-bulk of marrow in it — until the
Gemara reaches a genuine question: **does the marrow inside a bone "heal" the flesh outside it**, making the
bone a living limb? Three great answers follow, and they disagree about the deepest thing on the page:
**Rav Yehuda son of R' Chiyya** says marrow does *not* heal, **Abaye** says it does but the bone here was
*scraped*, and **R' Yochanan** says it heals, the bone *does* defile overhead, and the mishna's word
"touching" **quietly includes overlying**.

That last move — reading *nogea* (touch) to mean *ma'ahil* (overlie) — is what the rest of the daf tests. Is
touch-impurity and tent-impurity **one concept** that can combine to an olive-bulk, or two? *Which* tanna
ever calls a tent "touching"? (Answer: **R' Yosei**.) And when you drape a net over a corpse, does the
impurity trapped beneath it **break through and rise** even where there's no hole?

## The big picture — the mishna behind the daf

To follow the Gemara you need the mishna it is chewing on. The *kulit* of four sources:

- **A corpse** (*met*): touch it — impure, **sealed or perforated** alike.
- **A sacrificial animal** whose meat became *piggul* or *notar*: touch it — impure, **sealed or perforated**
  alike (a rabbinic decree).
- **A carcass** (*neveila*) and **a creeping thing** (*sheretz*): touch it **sealed** — *pure*; **perforated
  at all** — impure by **contact**, and (from a verse) by **carrying** too.

The daf never disputes the rulings. It asks *why each one is what it is* — and the answers turn out to pull
against one another, which is what generates the sugya.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **קוּלִית** *(kulit)* | The **thigh bone** — the marrow-filled femur. The whole mishna is a survey of whose *kulit* defiles, when sealed and when perforated. |
| **סָתוּם / נָקוּב** *(satum / nakuv)* | **Sealed** (no access to the marrow) versus **perforated** (the marrow can be reached). The mishna's two-way switch for every bone on the list. |
| **מוֹחַ** *(moach)* | The **marrow** inside the bone. Whether an olive-bulk of it sits there — and whether it is loose or bonded — drives every objection. |
| **מַאֲהִיל** *(ma'ahil)* | One who **overlies**, or a roof that overlies — *tent*-impurity. The daf's central question is whether the mishna's "touch" secretly includes this. |
| **טוּמְאָה בּוֹקַעַת וְעוֹלָה** *(tum'a boka'at ve-ola)* | Impurity that **breaks through and ascends** — an olive-bulk of corpse-marrow in a sealed bone still rises past it to defile overhead. |
| **מַעֲלֶה אֲרוּכָה** *(ma'aleh arucha)* | To **heal** — literally "raise a scar." Does marrow *inside* the bone regenerate flesh *outside* it, making the bone a living limb? The hinge of the three readings. |
| **שִׁפָּהּ** *(shipah)* | **Scraped** the bone bare. Abaye's key: a scraped bone can no longer heal, so it is not a proper limb — lengthwise it can still heal, widthwise it cannot (*siman*: a palm tree). |
| **שִׁימּוּשׁ נוֹתָר** *(shimush notar)* | **Serving as** *notar* — a sacrificial bone that held over-time meat becomes a *base for a forbidden thing* and defiles the hands like the *notar* itself. |
| **מִתְקַשְׁקֵשׁ** *(mitkashkesh)* | **Rattling** — R' Binyamin bar Giddel's case: marrow gone loose inside the bone. It won't heal, so a carcass-bone needs to be perforated to defile. |
| **חַד שְׁמָא / תְּרֵי שְׁמֵי** *(chad shma / trei shmei)* | **One concept / two concepts** — are touch-impurity and tent-impurity the same category (so two half-olives combine) or distinct (so they don't)? |
| **מְלֹא תַרְוָוד רָקָב** *(melo tarvad rakav)* | A **ladleful of corpse-dust** — R' Yosei's case, which defiles by contact, carrying *and* tent, and proves he calls a tent "touching." |
| **טוּמְאָה טְמוּנָה** *(tum'a temuna)* | **Hidden impurity** — sealed off beneath a covering. R' Yosei holds it does **not** break through and ascend. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Yosei** (ben Ḥalafta) — fourth-generation Tanna of Tzippori, student of R' Akiva; the tanna who
  calls a *tent* "touching," and who holds that *hidden* impurity does not break through. Nearly the whole
  second half of the daf is an exposition of his positions.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Yoḥanan** (bar Nafcha) — head of the Tiberias academy, the dominant voice of Eretz-Yisrael halakha;
  reads the mishna as having an olive-bulk of marrow that *does* heal, so the corpse-bone defiles in a tent —
  and "touching" in the mishna **means overlying**.
- **Rabbi Elazar** (ben Pedat) — second-generation Amora of Tiberias, R' Yoḥanan's foremost colleague; a
  thigh bone scraped **lengthwise** stays impure (it can still heal), scraped **widthwise** is pure — mnemonic,
  a **palm tree**. (The Amora "Rabbi Elazar" unqualified — not the Tanna R' Elazar ben Shammua.)
- **Rav Yehuda, son of Rabbi Ḥiyya** — first-generation Amora of Eretz Yisrael, son of R' Ḥiyya and son-in-law
  of R' Yannai; infers from the mishna that marrow inside a bone does **not** heal the flesh outside.
- **Rabbi Yitzḥak** — Eretz-Yisrael Amora (reported here by Mari bar Avuh); sacrificial bones that served as
  *notar* defile the hands, having become a base for a forbidden thing.
- **Rabbi Zeira** — third-generation Amora who went up from Bavel to Tiberias; rescues the *Oholot* mishna for
  R' Yoḥanan by reading it as *pressed* impurity between two chests, where everything counts as contact.
- **Rabbi Binyamin bar Giddel** — an Eretz-Yisrael Amora transmitting R' Yoḥanan; explains the carcass-bone by
  a case of **rattling** marrow. A minor figure about whom I can say little more with confidence.
- **Rabbi Avin** (Ravin) — third–fourth-generation Amora who carried Eretz-Yisrael teaching to Bavel; brings
  the *Oholot* mishna as support that "touching" can mean overlying. "**Some say Rabbi Yosei bar Avin**" — a
  later Amora — said it instead; the Gemara leaves the attribution open.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Abaye** — fourth-generation head of Pumbedita; reads the mishna as a **scraped** bone (marrow does heal in
  general), and distinguishes a tent below a handbreadth ("touching") from one above it ("merely a tent").
- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation head of Maḥoza; against Abaye, holds *even* a tent above a
  handbreadth is called "touching," and brings the netting-over-a-corpse baraita to prove it. (**Rava** —
  *not* **Rabba**; no Rabbah appears on this daf.)
- **Mari bar Avuh** — a Babylonian Amora; transmits R' Yitzḥak's ruling on *notar*-bearing bones. A minor
  figure; I would not add a biography I cannot vouch for.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 125a — "Touches, yes; overlies, no"

**The inference.** The Gemara opens on the corpse-bone clause: *"נוֹגֵעַ – אִין, אֲבָל מַאֲהִיל – לָא"* — one
who **touches** is impure, but one who **overlies** it (is under the same roof) is **not**. That is startling:
a corpse's own bone should defile in a tent.

**The narrowing.** So *what case* is this? A chain of *heichi dami* objections strips it down:

- **If there's an olive-bulk of flesh** on the bone — *"בְּאֹהֶל נָמֵי לִיטַמֵּא"*, it should defile in a tent
  too. → So there is **no** olive-bulk of flesh.
- **If there's an olive-bulk of marrow inside** — *"טוּמְאָה בּוֹקַעַת וְעוֹלָה"*, the impurity breaks through
  the bone and rises, so it should defile in a tent. → So there is **no** olive-bulk of marrow.
- **If the marrow inside heals the flesh outside** (*ma'aleh arucha*), the bone is *"אֵבֶר מְעַלְּיָא"*, a
  proper limb, and any bit of a limb defiles in a tent. → **Rav Yehuda son of R' Ḥiyya:** *"זֹאת אוֹמֶרֶת"* —
  this proves that **marrow inside does not heal the flesh outside**.

### 125a — One mishna, several teachings

**The pushback.** If you've pinned the mishna to a case with *no* olive-bulk, then why does the **sacrificial**
bone defile? And why do the **carcass** and **creeping-thing** bones defile when perforated?

**The resolution — *milei milei*.** *"רֵישָׁא דְּלֵיכָּא כְּזַיִת, סֵיפָא דְּאִיכָּא כְּזַיִת"* — the **opening**
clause (the corpse) has no olive-bulk; the **later** clauses (sacrifice, carcass, creeping thing) do. The
tanna is teaching **several separate matters**:

- **The corpse clause** teaches that marrow inside does not heal flesh outside.
- **The sacrifice clause** teaches *shimush notar* — as **Mari bar Avuh said R' Yitzḥak said:**
  *"עַצְמוֹת קָדָשִׁים שֶׁשִּׁימְּשׁוּ נוֹתָר, מְטַמְּאִין אֶת הַיָּדַיִם"*, bones of sacrifices that served as
  *notar* defile the hands, *"הוֹאִיל וְנַעֲשָׂה בָּסִיס לְדָבָר הָאָסוּר"* — since they became a **base for a
  forbidden thing**.
- **The carcass clause** teaches that even with an olive-bulk of marrow, perforated — yes, sealed — no.

### 125a — Abaye: the bone was scraped

**A different reading.** *"אַבָּיֵי אָמַר: לְעוֹלָם מוֹחַ מִבִּפְנִים מַעֲלֶה אֲרוּכָה מִבַּחוּץ"* — actually,
marrow inside *does* heal flesh outside. Then why doesn't the corpse-bone defile in a tent? *"בְּשֶׁשִּׁפָּהּ"*
— because **it was scraped**; a scraped bone can no longer heal, so it is not a proper limb.

**And in line with R' Elazar.** *"קוּלִית שֶׁשִּׁפָּהּ לְאׇרְכָּהּ – טְמֵאָה, לְרׇחְבָּהּ – טְהוֹרָה"* — a bone
scraped **lengthwise** stays impure (it can still heal), scraped **widthwise** is pure. *"וְסִימָנָיךְ –
דִּיקְלָא"* — your mnemonic is a **palm tree**: strip it lengthwise and it regrows; strip it around its girth
and the sap is cut off and it dies.

### 125a — R' Yoḥanan: "touching" means overlying

**The boldest reading.** *"לְעוֹלָם דְּאִיכָּא כְּזַיִת, וּמוֹחַ מִבִּפְנִים מַעֲלֶה אֲרוּכָה מִבַּחוּץ"* —
there *is* an olive-bulk of marrow, and it *does* heal. So the bone **does** defile in a tent — and
*"מַאי נוֹגֵעַ דְּקָתָנֵי – מַאֲהִיל"*, the "touching" the mishna teaches **is overlying**.

**The obvious objection.** If marrow heals, why are the sealed carcass and creeping-thing bones **pure**?
**R' Binyamin bar Giddel said R' Yoḥanan said:** the case is *"כְּזַיִת מוֹחַ הַמִּתְקַשְׁקֵשׁ"* — an olive-bulk
of **rattling**, detached marrow. For a **corpse**, impurity breaks through and rises regardless; for a
**carcass**, since the marrow is loose it will never heal, so the bone must be **perforated** to defile.

### 125a — Do contact and tent combine? R' Avin's support

**Two half-olives.** *"אַף אֲנַן נָמֵי תְּנֵינָא"* — **R' Avin (or R' Yosei bar Avin)** brings a mishna
(*Oholot* 3:1): one who **touches** half an olive-bulk of a corpse **and overlies** another half — or has half
overlie *him* — is **impure**. That only works if touch and tent are *"חַד שְׁמָא"*, **one concept**, so the
two halves **combine**; the mishna's own rule is *"מִשֵּׁם אֶחָד – מִצְטָרֵף וְטָמֵא, מִשְּׁנֵי שֵׁמוֹת – טָהוֹר"*.
So overlying is a species of touching — supporting R' Yoḥanan.

**But the next clause.** *"אֲבָל הַנּוֹגֵעַ בְּכַחֲצִי זַיִת, וְדָבָר אַחֵר מַאֲהִיל עָלָיו וְעַל כַּחֲצִי זַיִת –
טָהוֹר"* — if one **touches** half and a **separate item** overlies both him and another half, he is **pure**.
If touch and tent were one concept, why pure? So the *opening* clause is now the problem.

**R' Zeira's rescue.** *"בְּטוּמְאָה רְצוּצָה בֵּין שְׁנֵי מִגְדָּלִים עָסְקִינַן"* — the first clause is
**pressed** impurity, wedged between two chests with no handbreadth of space, so the overlying counts as
*"כּוּלַּהּ נְגִיעָה"*, all touching. No proof for R' Yoḥanan after all.

### 125b — Which tanna calls a tent "touching"?

**R' Yosei.** *"מַאן תַּנָּא דְּקָרֵי לְאֹהֶל נוֹגֵעַ? רַבִּי יוֹסֵי הִיא"* — the baraita:
*"מְלֹא תַרְוָוד רָקָב מְטַמֵּא בְּמַגָּע, וּבְמַשָּׂא, וּבְאֹהֶל"*, a **ladleful of corpse-dust** defiles by
contact, carrying, and tent. Carrying and tent are clear — you carry all of it, you overlie all of it. But
**contact**? You can't touch *all* the loose dust at once. *"אֶלָּא לָאו שְׁמַע מִינַּהּ: מַאי נוֹגֵעַ –
מַאֲהִיל"* — so "touching" here must mean **overlying**.

**But he lists both.** The baraita names *nogea* **and** *ma'ahil* separately — so they can't be the same.
**Abaye:** two kinds of overlying — *"לְמַטָּה מִטֶּפַח – אֹהֶל נְגִיעָה, לְמַעְלָה מִטֶּפַח – אֹהֶל גְּרֵידָא"*,
a tent **below a handbreadth** is "touching," a tent **above a handbreadth** is a *mere* tent. **Rava:** no —
*even* above a handbreadth it is "touching"; a *mere* tent is only *"בְּהַמְשָׁכָה"*, where the roof **spreads**
impurity from source to person.

### 125b — Rava's proof: the net over the corpse

**The baraita (R' Yosei).** Bed-bundles and window-grilles laid as a ceiling **screen** (*chotzetzin*)
impurity between floors. But spread them like a net over a corpse in the open: *"הַנּוֹגֵעַ כְּנֶגֶד הַנֶּקֶב –
טָמֵא, שֶׁלֹּא כְּנֶגֶד הַנֶּקֶב – טָהוֹר"* — overlie **opposite a hole**, impure; **not opposite a hole**, pure.

**Rava reads it.** If the net is *below* a handbreadth, then not-opposite-a-hole should *still* be impure — it
is *"מֵת בִּכְסוּתוֹ"*, a corpse in its clothing, and clothing is no barrier. So it must be **above** a
handbreadth — and yet R' Yosei calls overlying it **"touching."** That is Rava's proof.

**Abaye holds his ground.** No — it *is* below a handbreadth; *"מֵת בִּכְסוּתוֹ מְבַטֵּל לֵיהּ, הַאי לָא מְבַטֵּל
לֵיהּ"* — a corpse's clothing is *nullified* to it and so is no barrier, but a net you mean to lift again is
**not** nullified, so it *does* screen.

**The last turn — hidden impurity.** Then let the trapped impurity **break through and rise** anyway
(*boka'at ve-ola*), defiling even not-opposite-a-hole! *"קָסָבַר רַבִּי יוֹסֵי: טוּמְאָה טְמוּנָה אֵינָהּ
בּוֹקַעַת"* — R' Yosei holds **hidden impurity does not break through**. The proof is the *Oholot* 4:2 mishna
of a **chest-compartment** (*teivat ha-migdal*): *"דֶּרֶךְ טוּמְאָה לָצֵאת, וְאֵין דֶּרֶךְ טוּמְאָה לִיכָּנֵס"* —
impurity's way is to *leave* a space, not to *enter* one — with R' Yosei ruling the house **pure** because one
could remove the impurity piecemeal or burn it in place. That argument is where the daf breaks off, to resume
tomorrow.

## The distinctions, side by side

The corpse-bone clause gets **three readings**, and they part company on the deepest question — does marrow
heal, and what does "touching" mean? Every cell traces to the walkthrough above.

| Reading | Is there an olive-bulk of marrow? | Does marrow inside heal the flesh outside? | What does "touching" (*nogea*) mean? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rav Yehuda b'R' Ḥiyya** | No olive-bulk | **No** — this is exactly what the clause teaches | Touching, literally — the bone does not defile in a tent |
| **Abaye** | — (the bone was **scraped**) | Yes, in general — but a scraped bone cannot | Touching, literally |
| **R' Yoḥanan** | Yes, an olive-bulk | **Yes** — so the bone **does** defile in a tent | **Overlying too** — "touch" includes tent-impurity |

## One line to carry with you

> The mishna said one small word — "touches" — and the whole daf is an argument over whether it also, silently,
> means "stands under the same roof." A word's reach is decided not by the word but by the thing it names.
