## The big picture (read this first)

The daf opens still inside yesterday's question — **when does a partial break end a thing's identity, given
it might be put back together?** — and closes the file on it before turning to a wholly new subject. Three
movements, cleanly separated:

**First, the oven.** R' Yirmeya had pressed Reish Lakish from the mishna of dismantling an impure oven.
Rava dismantles the objection instead: don't argue from R' Meir — *argue from the Rabbis*, who require
scraping the plaster **until the oven rests on the ground**, and who therefore agree with Reish Lakish that
only a total, irreparable break purifies. Then Rava rereads the whole mishna so that R' Meir need not
disagree either. A pair of *Kelim* contradictions about an oven's measures — three parts, four handbreadths,
or the majority — get sorted along the way (a horizontal cut behaves differently from a vertical one; a
nine-handbreadth oven differently from a seven).

**Then the same dispute, one more time.** Over a torn garment that leaves a scarf-sized remnant, Reish
Lakish and R' Yochanan clash again — now the word is not *chalim* (repairable) but **חָשִׁיב** (*significant*):

> A hide reduced below five handbreadths for straps is pure. Reish Lakish: that is only a *garment* — a
> **hide's** remnant is significant and stays impure. R' Yochanan: even a hide is not significant. And the
> whole thing turns on *what you mean to do with it* — a scrap too small for a *zav*'s seat is significant
> to no one.

**Then a fresh mishna, and the rest of the daf.** A **hide with flesh clinging to it**. Touch a strand of
that flesh, or a hair backing it, and you are impure — the strand *is* flesh, the hair *guards* it. But
split the olive-bulk into **two half-olives** and the tannaim divide: R' Yishmael says the hide imparts
impurity by **carrying** but not by **contact**; R' Akiva says **neither** — *the hide nullifies them*. The
Gemara then spends the day on the fault-lines this opens: does it matter whether a **beast** or a **knife**
severed the flesh (Ulla says yes, and Rav Nachman refuses to believe it); do two sub-minimum **contacts add
up**; and must a carcass's impurity be **carriable all at once**, without a utensil doing the lifting?

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **חָשִׁיב** *(chashiv)* | **Significant** — Reish Lakish's category for a hide's remnant: too meaningful to disregard, so it stays impure. R' Yochanan: even a hide is not significant. The re-run of yesterday's *chalim*. |
| **גִּיסְטְרָא** *(gistera)* | A **shard** — an oven cut vertically, so the larger piece still stands on its own; contrasted with a horizontal cut (*tzalkei mitzlak*) where nothing stands. |
| **כְּזַיִת בָּשָׂר** *(kezayit basar)* | An **olive-bulk of flesh** — the minimum of carcass-flesh that conveys impurity. The mishna's hide carries exactly this. |
| **צִיב** *(tziv)* | A **strand** of flesh emerging from the piece. It has the status of the flesh itself, so touching it defiles. |
| **שַׂעֲרָה שֶׁכְּנֶגְדּוֹ** *(sa'ara sheknegdo)* | The **hair opposite** the flesh — on the far side of the hide. A *protector* (*shomer*) of the flesh, so it too conveys impurity. |
| **בְּמַגָּע / בְּמַשָּׂא** *(be-maga / be-masa)* | Impurity **by contact** / **by carrying**. The mishna's two half-olives split the tannaim along exactly this line. |
| **מְבַטְּלָן** *(mevatlan)* | "**Nullifies them**" — R' Akiva's reason the hide with two half-olives defiles by neither route: the hide cancels the pieces' significance. |
| **פְּלָטַתּוּ חַיָּה / סַכִּין** *(pelatatu chaya / sakin)* | Severed **by a beast** (e.g. a dog's bite) versus **by a knife**. Ulla: the mishna's rule holds only for a beast; a knife-cut *nullifies* the flesh into the hide. |
| **נוֹגֵעַ וְחוֹזֵר וְנוֹגֵעַ** *(noge'a ve-chozer ve-noge'a)* | "**Touch and touch again**" — whether two contacts, each with less than an olive-bulk, add up to defiling contact. Bar Padda: yes; R' Yochanan: no. |
| **מְרוּדָּד** *(mrudad)* | A **thin, spread-out** smear of flesh — flat against the hide. Even a large volume of it is insignificant and nullified; and it is what joins two half-olives so they lift together. |
| **נִישָּׂא בְּבַת אַחַת** *(nissa be-vat achat)* | **Carried all at once** — Ulla's demand from the verse: impurity by carrying requires the olive-bulk lift as one, without a utensil bearing it. |
| **קוּלִית סְתוּמָה** *(kulit setuma)* | A **sealed thigh bone** — intact, no access to the marrow. Rav Avya's question: does it defile by carrying for R' Yishmael? — met with a flying raven. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Meir** — fourth-generation Tanna, R' Akiva's student; on the oven, one need not scrape the plaster
  to the ground, only reduce it below four handbreadths from within. Rava rereads the mishna so even he can
  agree with Reish Lakish.
- **Rabbi Yishmael** (ben Elisha) — third-generation Tanna, the great disputant of R' Akiva; a hide with
  two half-olives defiles by carrying but not by contact. (*Not* R' Yishmael b'R' Yosei, below.)
- **Rabbi Akiva** (ben Yosef) — third-generation Tanna of Bnei Brak; the hide defiles by neither contact nor
  carrying, because *the hide nullifies* the pieces — yet he concedes when they are skewered on a wood chip.
- **Rabbi Yehuda** (bar Ilai) — fourth-generation Tanna; a *midras*-impure hide meant for straps turns pure
  the moment a scalpel touches it, against the Rabbis who require reducing it below five handbreadths.
- **Rabbi Dosa ben Harkinas** — early Tanna; two sub-olive corpse-pieces under one roof do not join for
  tent-impurity (*Oholot* 3:1). R' Yochanan pairs him with R' Yishmael as holding "the same thing."
- **Rabbi Eliezer** (ben Hyrcanus) — second-generation Tanna; in the baraita, adds that one who *carries* is
  impure — read by the Gemara as requiring the pieces be carriable at once.
- **Rabbi Yishmael b'Rabbi Yosei** (ben Ḥalafta) — fifth-generation Tanna; via Rav Huna, even a scarf-sized
  untorn remnant leaves a garment pure (the *lishna achrina*). (*Not* R' Yishmael ben Elisha above.)
- **Joshua son of Nun** — invoked only rhetorically: R' Ami would not accept the *reisha* version even from him.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Reish Lakish** (Shimon ben Lakish) — second-generation Amora of Tiberias; a hide's remnant is

- **Rabbi Yoḥanan** (bar Nafcha) — head of the Tiberias academy, Reish Lakish's colleague; even a hide is
  not significant. He also holds that two contacts do **not** combine. (The Amora — distinct from R'
  Yishmael's Tanna generation.)
- **Ulla** (bar Yishmael) — second–third-generation Amora who carried Eretz-Yisrael teaching to Bavel;

- **Rabbi Ami** — third-generation head of Tiberias after R' Yochanan; twice defends R' Yochanan's teaching

- **Rav Oshaya** — Amora who "ascended" to Eretz Yisrael and relayed the Babylonian give-and-take to R' Ami.
- **Bar Padda** — an Eretz-Yisrael Amora; touching the flesh from *inside* the hide, two contacts combine.
  A minor figure about whom I can say little more with confidence.
- **The school of Rabbi Yannai** — defines "any size" for a small oven as one handbreadth, the size of a

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation head of Maḥoza; rereads the oven mishna to save Reish

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 124a — Finishing the oven: the objection turned into support

**Where we stand.** R' Yirmeya had objected to Reish Lakish from the oven mishna: R' Meir purifies an oven
by reducing it below four handbreadths *from within the plaster* — *"מְמַעֲטוֹ מִבִּפְנִים מֵאַרְבָּעָה
טְפָחִים"* — yet the plaster still holds it, so *"הָא חֲלִים וְקָאֵי"*, it "exists in a repairable state."
Why is it pure, if Reish Lakish counts the repairable as still connected?

**Rava's first move.** Don't argue *against* Reish Lakish from R' Meir — argue *for* him **from the Rabbis**:
they require *"גּוֹרֵר אֶת הַטְּפֵילָה עַד שֶׁיְּהֵא בָּאָרֶץ"*, scraping the plaster until the oven rests on
the bare ground, i.e. a total, irreparable break. That is Reish Lakish's very principle.

**Rava's rereading.** *"אֶלָּא אָמַר רָבָא"* — better still, R' Meir need not disagree at all. Read the
mishna as two questions. **To purify** an already-impure oven — *everyone*, R' Meir included, divides it into
three and scrapes to the ground. **To keep** an oven from ever becoming susceptible — *there* R' Meir is
lenient: no need to scrape, just reduce it below four handbreadths from within.

### 124a — The oven's measures: three, four, or the majority

**"Divides into three."** *"אָמַר מָר: חוֹלְקוֹ לִשְׁלֹשָׁה"* — so that no single piece holds the majority. The
Gemara contradicts it from *Kelim* 5:1: an oven's original state to become impure is **four** handbreadths,
and its **remains** stay impure at **four** — R' Meir; the Rabbis distinguish a large oven from a small one,
whose remains stay impure only *"בְּרוּבּוֹ"*, in its majority. And "any size" for a small oven? The school
of R' Yannai: *"טֶפַח, שֶׁכֵּן עוֹשִׂים תַּנּוּרֵי בָּנוֹת טֶפַח"* — a handbreadth, the size of a child's toy
oven.

**The two contradictions, resolved.** The inference — impure only if a four-handbreadth piece remains, so
otherwise pure even with a majority — clashes with the earlier "majority" ruling. *Answer:*
*"הָתָם, דְּצַלְקֵיהּ מִצְלָק; הָכָא, דְּעַבְדֵיהּ גִּיסְטְרָא"* — *there* the oven was cut **horizontally**, so
no piece stands stably; *here* it was made a **shard** (*gistera*), cut vertically, so a majority piece can
stand on its own. And the "majority of a handbreadth" that R' Yannai's toy oven would leave — what use is it?
**Abaye:** the "majority" rule speaks of a *large* oven; the "four" rule and the "majority" rule sit
*"הָא בְּתַנּוּרָא בַּר תִּשְׁעָה, הָא בְּתַנּוּרָא בַּר שִׁבְעָה"* — one about a **nine**-handbreadth oven, the
other a **seven**.

### 124a — A hide reduced for straps: "significant" restated

**The *lishna achrina*.** Rav Huna, in R' Yishmael b'R' Yosei's name: a torn garment is pure *even* if a
scarf-sized piece is left untorn. On this, **Reish Lakish:** *"לֹא שָׁנוּ אֶלָּא טַלִּית, אֲבָל עוֹר —
חֲשִׁיב"* — that is only a **garment**; a **hide's** remnant is *significant* and stays impure. **R'
Yochanan:** *"אֲפִילּוּ עוֹר נָמֵי לָא חֲשִׁיב"*, even a hide is not significant. (Yesterday's *chalim* /
"repairable," now cast as *chashiv* / "significant.")

**R' Yochanan's objection, and its answer.** From *Kelim* 26:9: a *midras*-impure hide meant for straps and
sandals turns pure once a scalpel touches it (R' Yehuda), or — per the Rabbis —
*"עַד שֶׁיַּמְעִיטֶנּוּ מֵחֲמִשָּׁה טְפָחִים"*, once reduced below five handbreadths. Reduced, it is pure —
why, if it should be significant? **Reish Lakish:** *"דְּקָא בָעֵי לֵיהּ לְמוֹשַׁב זָב"* — the case is one who
wants the hide for a **zav's seat**; a scrap under five handbreadths is useless as a seat, so *there* it is
not significant. Significance, in other words, is measured against the purpose in hand.

### 124a — The new mishna: a hide with flesh on it

*"עוֹר שֶׁיֵּשׁ עָלָיו כְּזַיִת בָּשָׂר"* — a carcass-hide bearing an olive-bulk of flesh. **One who touches a
strand emerging from it** (*tziv*) **or a hair on the opposite side** (*sa'ara sheknegdo*) is impure: the
strand *is* flesh, and the hair *protects* it. **Two half-olives** on the hide, though, split the tannaim:

- **R' Yishmael:** *"מְטַמֵּא בְּמַשָּׂא וְלֹא בְּמַגָּע"* — impure by **carrying** (you lift them together) but
  not by **contact** (you touch each separately).
- **R' Akiva:** *"לֹא בְּמַגָּע וְלֹא בְּמַשָּׂא"* — **neither**. But he *concedes* that two half-olives
  **skewered on a wood chip** and moved *are* impure. Why does he purify with the hide? *"מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהָעוֹר
  מְבַטְּלָן"* — because the **hide nullifies** them.

### 124a — Gemara: teeth or knife? and Rav Nachman's outburst

**Ulla in R' Yochanan's name.** The mishna's flesh keeps its status *"אֶלָּא פְּלָטַתּוּ חַיָּה"* — only where
a **beast** tore it off (a dog's bite); *"אֲבָל פְּלָטַתּוּ סַכִּין — בָּטֵיל"*, but a **knife**-cut nullifies
the flesh into the hide, since a person's deliberate act cancels it.

**Rav Nachman's disbelief.** Even a piece the size of a *tarta* (a quarter-*kav*)? *Yes.* Even a *sifter*
(*nafya*)? *Yes.* *"הָאֱלֹהִים! אִם אָמַר לִי רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן מִפּוּמֵּיהּ — לָא צָיֵיתְנָא לֵיהּ"* — By God,
even from R' Yochanan's own mouth I would not accept it. When Rav Oshaya carried this to R' Ami, R' Ami
bristled: because Rav Nachman married into the Exilarch's house, he **demeans** R' Yochanan's teaching?

**But which clause?** Later Rav Oshaya found R' Ami teaching the rule on the *seifa* (the two half-olives) —
and there R' Ami himself would not accept even the *reisha* (a full olive-bulk) version, "even from Joshua
son of Nun." When **Ravin and the descenders** came, though, they placed R' Yochanan's rule on the *reisha*.
The objection — that a knife should then nullify even a *tarta*, absurdly — is deferred to Rav Pappa.

### 124b — *Mrudad*: a thin smear of flesh

**Rav Pappa's key.** *"בִּמְרוּדָּד"* — R' Yochanan spoke of a **thin, spread-out** layer of flesh flat
against the hide. Even a large *volume* of such flesh, severed with the hide, is trivial and nullified — so
there is no absurdity in a *tarta* being nullified.

### 124b — Do two contacts combine? Bar Padda vs R' Yochanan

**Bar Padda.** R' Yishmael denies impurity by *contact* only for one who touches the hide *from the outside*
(*me'acharav*). Touch the flesh directly *from inside* (*milfanav*), though, and *"יֵשׁ נוֹגֵעַ וְחוֹזֵר
וְנוֹגֵעַ"* — two contacts, each under an olive-bulk, **join** to make defiling contact.

**R' Yochanan.** *"אֵין נוֹגֵעַ וְחוֹזֵר וְנוֹגֵעַ"* — they do **not** join; and he is consistent, for he holds
*"רַבִּי יִשְׁמָעֵאל וְרַבִּי דּוֹסָא בֶּן הַרְכִּינָס אָמְרוּ דָּבָר אֶחָד"* — R' Yishmael and R' Dosa ben
Harkinas said one thing. R' Dosa (*Oholot* 3:1) holds two sub-olive corpse-pieces under one roof do not
combine for tent-impurity — no *"מַאֲהִיל וְחוֹזֵר וּמַאֲהִיל."*

**The pushback.** If R' Dosa aligns with R' Yishmael, then the Rabbis who oppose R' Dosa align with R'
Akiva — yet R' Akiva is *lenient* (purifies), while those Rabbis are *stringent*! *Answer:* R' Akiva purifies
**only** because of the hide's nullification; *in general* he holds pieces combine — as the mishna's own
*seifa* shows (the wood-chip skewer defiles).

### 124b — The verse: contact and carrying locked together

**Rav Ukva bar Ḥama's objection to Bar Padda.** The baraita on *"בְּנִבְלָתָם"* (Lev 11:24–25) — "and *not*
a hide bearing two half-olives" — teaches: one might think it defiles not even by carrying, so the verse adds
*"וְהַנּוֹשֵׂא יִטְמָא"* (R' Yishmael). R' Akiva reads the two verbs as locked: what enters the category of
**contact** enters **carrying**, and what does not enter contact does not enter carrying. But on Bar Padda's
view it *does* enter contact — from inside! **Rava resolves R' Akiva:** *"אֶת שֶׁבָּא לִכְלַל מַגָּע בְּכׇל
צַד — בָּא לִכְלַל מַשָּׂא"* — what enters contact in **every** manner (even touching the hide's outside)
enters carrying; what does not, does not.

### 124b — The sealed thigh bone, and the flying raven

**Rav Avya's question.** A **sealed thigh bone** (*kulit setuma*) — marrow within, no flesh without, so it
does not defile by contact in any manner. For R' Yishmael, does it defile by *carrying*? It turns on whether
R' Yishmael truly holds "no contact anywhere → no carrying." **Rabba bar Rav Huna's answer:**
*"עוֹרְבָא פָּרַח"* — "a raven flies!", a deflection. His son Rava protested: isn't this the great Rav Avya of
Pumbedita you praise? — and Rabba admitted *"סַמְּכוּנִי בָּאֲשִׁישׁוֹת"*, "hold me up with cakes," I was
spent, and he asked a matter that needs reasoning.

### 124b — Carried all at once

**Ulla.** Two half-olives skewered on a wood chip — *"אֲפִילּוּ מוֹלִיךְ וּמֵבִיא כׇּל הַיּוֹם כּוּלּוֹ —
טָהוֹר"*, even carried back and forth all day, one stays pure. Why? The verse's *venisa*/*nosei* teaches
*"בָּעֵינַן נוֹשֵׂא וְהוּא דְּנִישָּׂא בְּבַת אַחַת"* — carrying requires the olive-bulk be **carriable all at
once**, without a utensil bearing it. Objections from the mishna (R' Yishmael) and from R' Akiva's concession
— why do *those* two-half-olive cases defile by carrying? — are met, both times, with **Rav Pappa's
*bimrudad***: a thin strip of flesh connects the pieces so they lift as one. Finally the whole rule is cast
as a tannaic dispute: R' Eliezer's "even one who carries" is reread to mean *only* where the pieces are
carriable at once.

## The distinctions, side by side

The mishna's engine is a single hide bearing flesh, and how the impurity travels depends on **how the flesh
sits** and **who is looking**. Every cell traces to the walkthrough above.

| The flesh on the hide | R' Yishmael | R' Akiva |
|---|---|---|
| **A whole olive-bulk** (touch the *strand* or the *hair*) | Impure — by contact and by carrying | Impure |
| **Two half-olives** lying on the hide | Carrying **yes**, contact **no** | **Neither** — the hide nullifies them |
| **Two half-olives skewered on a wood chip**, moved | Impure | Impure — he **concedes** here |

---

## One line to carry with you

> The same flesh, the same hide — but split it in two, or let a knife rather than a beast take it off, and
> the impurity may simply cease to travel. Significance is not in the thing; it is in what it can still do.

  why: The Rabbis' total scraping supports Reish Lakish; Rava then rereads the mishna as purify-vs-prevent so R' Meir need not disagree.
- q: How does the Gemara reconcile the oven that is pure only when cut into three with the one pure only without a four-handbreadth remnant?
  opts:
    - One mishna speaks of a clay oven and the other one speaks entirely of a metal oven.
    - One was cut horizontally so nothing stands; the other a vertical shard that stands.
    - One follows R' Meir and the other the Rabbis, left as an unresolved standing dispute.
    - One measures the oven before it was fired and the other one measures it afterward.
  correct: b
  why: Tzalkei mitzlak (horizontal, nothing stable) versus gistera (a vertical shard that stands) — different cuts, different rules.
- q: In the "straps" case, why is a hide reduced below five handbreadths pure even for Reish Lakish?
  opts:
    - The case concerns a hide wanted for a zav's seat — a scrap that small can't serve as one.
    - A hide, unlike a garment, is never susceptible to midras impurity in the very first place.
    - A scalpel-cut, per R' Yehuda, always purifies a hide whatever the measure that remains.
    - Below five handbreadths a hide counts as torn in a circle, not cut along a straight line.
  correct: a
  why: Bo'ei leih le-moshav zav — significance is measured by purpose; a piece too small for a seat is significant to no one.
- q: In the new mishna, why is one who touches a hair on the far side of the hide impure?
  opts:
    - The hair itself is reckoned an olive-bulk of flesh once counted together with the strand.
    - Touching any part of a carcass-hide, hair or not, always transmits impurity by Torah law.
    - The hair carries blood out from the flesh through to the outer surface of the whole hide.
    - The hair is a shomer (protector) of the flesh, and a protector conveys its impurity.
  correct: d
  why: The strand is flesh and the opposite hair is a shomer guarding it, so touching either defiles.
- q: On what do R' Yishmael and R' Akiva disagree about a hide bearing two half-olives of flesh?
  opts:
    - R' Yishmael — impure by carrying not contact; R' Akiva — neither, the hide nullifies them.
    - R' Yishmael — impure by contact but not carrying; R' Akiva — by both contact and carrying.
    - Both hold it impure by carrying, differing only over the strand's size joining the pieces.
    - R' Yishmael — pure in every single case; R' Akiva — impure only where a beast severed it.
  correct: a
  why: R' Yishmael — masa yes, maga no; R' Akiva — neither, mipnei she-ha'or mevatlan, but he concedes with the wood-chip skewer.
- q: According to Ulla in R' Yochanan's name, what determines whether the flesh on the hide is nullified?
  opts:
    - Whether the piece of flesh is larger or smaller than a tarta, which is a quarter of a kav.
    - Whether the hide was already impure with midras before the flesh ever came to be attached.
    - Whether a beast severed it (not nullified) or a knife did (nullified by his own act).
    - Whether the flesh lies on the hair side of the hide or the flesh side that faces inward.
  correct: c
  why: Pelatatu chaya — a beast's tearing keeps it flesh; pelatatu sakin — a knife-cut nullifies it into the hide.
- q: What was Rav Nachman's reaction to the report that even a tarta-sized piece is nullified by a knife-cut?
  opts:
    - He accepted it at once, since it followed plainly from R' Yochanan's own stated reasoning.
    - He said he would not accept it even if R' Yochanan told it to him from his own mouth.
    - He objected that only a beast, and never a knife, can sever flesh from a carcass-hide.
    - He ruled the piece is nullified only if it is a thin, spread-out layer of flesh on a hide.
  correct: b
  why: Ha-Elohim — by God, even from R' Yochanan's mouth he would not listen; R' Ami saw it as demeaning R' Yochanan.
- q: What is Rav Pappa's resolution — "bimrudad" — and where does the Gemara apply it?
  opts:
    - The flesh is folded double, so a single handbreadth counts as two for the impurity measure.
    - The hide was cut in a circle, so it cannot be repaired and therefore it turns pure at once.
    - The oven was made a vertical shard, so its majority piece stands alone and so stays impure.
    - A thin, spread-out layer of flesh, so a large volume is nullified and pieces lift as one.
  correct: d
  why: A thin (mrudad) layer explains both why a large severed piece is nullified and why two half-olives can be carried together.
- q: How does R' Yochanan defend pairing R' Yishmael with R' Dosa ben Harkinas, given that the Rabbis opposing R' Dosa are stringent while R' Akiva is lenient?
  opts:
    - He concedes the pairing fails and withdraws the comparison between the two tannaim entirely.
    - R' Akiva is lenient only because the hide nullifies; in general he too holds pieces combine.
    - He argues that R' Dosa in fact rules stringently, just like the Rabbis who oppose his view.
    - Tent-impurity and contact-impurity follow wholly unrelated rules that cannot be compared.
  correct: b
  why: R' Akiva's leniency is the hide's nullification alone; the seifa's wood-chip skewer shows he holds pieces combine in general.
- q: What does Ulla derive from the verse's venisa/nosei about impurity by carrying?
  opts:
    - That carrying defiles only by day, since the verse speaks of impurity lasting until evening.
    - That carrying and contact are wholly independent categories that never depend on one another.
    - That the olive-bulk must be carriable all at once, with no utensil bearing its own weight.
    - That a beast's severing, unlike a person's, leaves flesh able to defile by carrying alone.
  correct: c
  why: Ba'einan nosei ve-hu de-nissa be-vat achat — the impurity must lift as one; a wood-chip skewer, doing the lifting, leaves one pure.
