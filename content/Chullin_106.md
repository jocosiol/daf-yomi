## The big picture (read this first)

Yesterday graded the three waters — first, middle, and last — and ended among Abaye's table customs.
Today asks the harder question underneath all of it: *why does any of this matter?* The daf answers
first with a warning, then with law.

> The warning comes from **Rav Dimi** and **Ravin**, two travelers who carried Eretz-Yisrael teachings
> to Babylonia in slightly different words: skip the **first waters** and you may be **fed forbidden
> meat**; skip the **last waters** and a **marriage may be destroyed**. Then the law: which water is fit
> to wash with, *why* the Sages ever obligated washing before ordinary food, whether washing before
> *fruit* is required or merely fussy, and exactly **how far up the hand** the water must reach.

Four movements:

1. **What is at stake** (106a) — Rav Dimi and Ravin on the two catastrophes that followed neglected
   washing, with Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak's mnemonic for who said which.
2. **Which water qualifies** (106a) — fire-heated water and the hot springs of Tiberias, Chizkiyya
   against Rabbi Yochanan, resolved by Rav Pappa into a dispute only about water drawn through a ditch.
3. **Why wash at all** (106a) — washing before bread as a guard on *teruma*, and as a *mitzva*; then
   washing before fruit as cleanliness, optional, or plain arrogance.
4. **How far, and for how long** (106a–106b) — up to which joint the water must reach for ordinary
   food, for *teruma*, and for the Temple service, and Rav's leave to wash once in the morning.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **מַיִם רִאשׁוֹנִים / אַחֲרוֹנִים** *(mayim rishonim / acharonim)* | The first waters (before bread) and last waters (before Grace); neglecting them, the daf warns, once led to forbidden meat and a ruined marriage. |
| **חַמֵּי הָאוּר** *(chamei ha'ur)* | Water heated over fire — Chizkiyya bars it for washing the hands, Rabbi Yochanan permits it. |
| **חַמֵּי טְבֶרְיָא** *(chamei Teveria)* | The natural hot springs of Tiberias — fit for immersion, disputed for washing the hands. |
| **בַּת בִּירְתָּא** *(bat birta)* | Water drawn off through a ditch or channel — the one case in which the Tiberias dispute actually turns. |
| **גָּזְרִינַן / לָא גָּזְרִינַן** *(gazrinan / la gazrinan)* | "We decree / we do not decree" — whether ditch water is banned lest one come to use a vessel. |
| **חוֹצֵץ** *(chotzetz)* | An interposition between skin and water that spoils immersion — and equally spoils the washing of the hands. |
| **סֶרֶךְ תְּרוּמָה** *(serech teruma)* | "A guard on *teruma*" — the reason the Sages had everyone wash before ordinary food: to keep priests from growing lax with the priestly portion. |
| **עַד הַפֶּרֶק** *(ad haperek)* | "Up to the joint" — how far along the fingers the water must reach; the joint's exact place is itself disputed. |
| **מִגַּסֵּי הָרוּחַ** *(migasei haruach)* | "Of the arrogant" — Rav Nachman's verdict on one who insists on washing before eating fruit. |
| **רְשׁוּת** *(reshut)* | Optional — Rava's grade for washing before fruit: neither obligation nor mitzva. |

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 106a — The stakes, then the water

**The two catastrophes.** The daf opens with a warning delivered in two versions. **Rav Dimi** taught
that neglecting the **first waters** once had a Jew **fed pig meat** — a shopkeeper, seeing his customer
eat without washing, took him for a gentile and served him accordingly; and neglecting the **last
waters** once had a **wife divorced**, when thieves used the lentils left on a man's mustache (proof he
had not washed) to fake his instruction that she return their money. **Ravin** told it more darkly:
first waters, **carcass meat** (*neveila*); last waters, the enraged husband **killed his wife**.

> **אֲתָא רַב דִּימִי אַפְּקַהּ, אֲתָא רָבִין קַטְלַהּ.**
> Rav Dimi came and *divorced* her; Ravin came and *killed* her.

That is **Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak's mnemonic** for keeping the two versions straight. **Rabbi Abba**
would teach one detail from each — the pig meat from Rav Dimi, the killing from Ravin — always choosing
the **more severe** telling.

**Fire-heated water.** A dispute on whether heated water is fit for washing the hands: **Chizkiyya**
says one **may not** wash with it, **Rabbi Yochanan** says one **may** — and he backs it with testimony,
having asked **Rabban Gamliel son of Rebbi**, who ate only in ritual purity, and been told that **all
the great men of the Galilee** wash in heated water.

**The hot springs of Tiberias.** The same two disagree again, now over *chamei Teveria*. Chizkiyya: one
may not wash the hands from them, **but may immerse the hands** directly in them. Rabbi Yochanan: one may
immerse the **whole body** in them to become pure, **but not** the face, hands, and feet.

**The objection.** If the whole body may be immersed, surely the hands, all the more so? **Rav Pappa's
answer** dissolves the dispute into agreement on the extremes and a fight only in the middle:

> **בִּמְקוֹמָן – דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא לָא פְּלִיגִי דִּשְׁרֵי … מִשְׁקַל מִינַּיְיהוּ בְּמָנָא – דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא לָא פְּלִיגִי דַּאֲסִיר.**
> When the water stands **in place**, all agree it is permitted; **taken up in a vessel**, all agree it
> is forbidden. They disagree only when it is **drawn through a ditch** (*bat birta*): one holds we
> **decree** against ditch water lest one come to use a vessel, and one holds we do **not** decree.

**Like a tannaitic dispute.** The Gemara anchors this in a baraita about water no longer fit even for an
animal to drink: **in vessels** it is unfit, **in the ground** it is fit as a *mikveh*. **Rabbi Shimon
ben Elazar** adds: even in the ground one may immerse the whole body but not face, hands, and feet — the
same *kal vachomer*, and so the same reading: they too split over **ditch water** and whether to decree.

**Why wash before ordinary food at all?** **Rav Idi bar Avin** in the name of **Rav Yitzchak bar
Ashian**: washing before non-sacred food (*chullin*) exists as a **guard on *teruma*** (*serech
teruma*) — hands are presumed impure and would defile *teruma*, so the Sages had everyone wash always,
lest priests grow lax. **And also because of a mitzva.** Which mitzva? **Abaye**: the mitzva to **obey
the Sages**. **Rava**: the mitzva to heed **Rabbi Elazar ben Arakh**, who read the *zav* verse — "and
his hands he has not rinsed in water" (Vayikra 15:11) — as a Torah hint for washing the hands. When
**Rava** pressed **Rav Nachman** on the inference, he re-read it: *another* who has not rinsed his hands
is treated as impure.

**Washing before fruit.** **Rabbi Elazar** said **Rabbi Oshaya**: washing before **fruit** is only for
**cleanliness** (*nekiyut*). The listeners inferred: no obligation, but still a *mitzva*. **Rava**
corrected them — **neither obligation nor mitzva, but optional** (*reshut*) — and even that clashes with
**Rav Nachman**, who held that one who washes before fruit **is merely of the arrogant** (*migasei
haruach*). **Rabba bar bar Chana** clinched it from life: standing before **Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi**,
he watched them eat a basket of fruit **without washing**, give him nothing, and each bless
**separately** — three rulings at once: no washing for fruit, no *zimmun* on fruit, and two who ate
should split (each blessing alone). A baraita agrees on the split — unless one is an ignoramus, in which
case the scholar blesses and the ignoramus is discharged by listening.

### 106b — How far the water must reach

**Up to which joint.** A baraita measures the pour: for **chullin**, up to the **joint** of the fingers;
for **teruma**, up to the joint; for **sanctifying hands and feet in the Temple**, up to the joint where
the palm meets the wrist. And whatever **interposes** (*chotzetz*) in the body's immersion likewise
interposes for washing the hands and for the Temple sanctification.

**Three readings of "the joint."** **Rav**: *here* (the second joint) for *chullin*, *there* (the third,
at the palm) for *teruma*. **Shmuel**: the same, strict line for **both**. **Rav Sheshet**: the same,
lenient line for **both**. **Bar Hedya** reports Rabbi Ami ruling strictly for both — and heads off the
suspicion that it was only because Rabbi Ami is a **priest**, by noting that **Rabbi Meyasha**, a Levite
and grandson of **Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi**, ruled the same way.

**Wash once, rely all day.** **Rav**: a person may wash both hands in the morning and **stipulate** to
rely on that washing the whole day — provided he guards them from dirt and impurity. The application, to
the water-starved valley of Aravot, spills over into tomorrow's daf.

## The distinctions, side by side

The daf's legal core is two heated waters, judged by two sages — before Rav Pappa shows the gap is
narrower than it looks:

| The water | Chizkiyya | Rabbi Yochanan |
|---|---|---|
| **Fire-heated** (*chamei ha'ur*) | May **not** wash the hands with it | **May** wash — as the great men of the Galilee do |
| **Hot springs of Tiberias** (*chamei Teveria*) | No washing, but may **immerse the hands** | May immerse the **whole body** — but not face, hands, feet |

*Rav Pappa's reading of Tiberias:* standing water — all permit; water in a vessel — all forbid; only
water **drawn through a ditch** is disputed, and only over whether to **decree** against it.

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar** — fifth-generation Tanna, a student of Rabbi Meir; supplies the baraita
  about water unfit for animals that mirrors the Tiberias dispute.
- **Rabbi Elazar ben Arakh** — the celebrated Tanna, foremost student of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai;
  cited by Rava as the source who anchored hand-washing in the Torah's *zav* verse. (The attribution of
  a hand-washing derivation to so early a figure is discussed by the commentators; I would not build
  more on it than the Gemara does.)
- **Rabban Gamliel son of Rebbi** — Rabban Gamliel III, son of Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, who succeeded him
  as Nasi; Rabbi Yochanan cites his practice on heated water.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Chizkiyya** — son of Rabbi Chiyya, a first-generation figure straddling the Tannaitic and Amoraic
  eras; here the stringent voice on both heated water and the Tiberias springs.
- **Rabbi Yochanan** (bar Nappacha) — second generation, head of the academy in Tiberias; the lenient
  voice, backed by the practice of the Galilee's great men.
- **Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi** — first-generation Amora of Lod; named as the grandfather of the Levite
  Rabbi Meyasha.
- **Rabbi Meyasha** — grandson of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, a Levite; his ruling shows the strict view
  was not merely priestly caution.
- **Rabbi Oshaya** (Rabba) — first-generation Amora, a master of baraitot; source (via Rabbi Elazar)
  that washing before fruit is only for cleanliness.
- **Rabbi Elazar** (ben Pedat) — third-generation Amora, student of Rabbi Yochanan; transmits Rabbi
  Oshaya's ruling. **Not** Rabbi Elazar ben Arakh, the Tanna above.
- **Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi** — third-generation colleagues, leading sages of Tiberias; Rabba bar bar
  Chana learns three rulings from watching them eat fruit.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav Dimi** and **Ravin** — fourth-generation *nachutei*, sages who ferried Eretz-Yisrael teachings
  down to Babylonia; they give the two versions of the washing catastrophes.
- **Rabbi Abba** — a Babylonian-born sage who settled in Eretz Yisrael (third generation); teaches the
  severe detail from each version.
- **Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak** — fourth generation, later head of Pumbedita; author of the mnemonic.
- **Rav Idi bar Avin** and **Rav Yitzchak bar Ashian** — the pair behind the *serech teruma* reason;
  both are minor tradents, and I would not press their biographies further.
- **Abaye** — fourth generation, head of Pumbedita; "the mitzva to obey the Sages."
- **Rava** — fourth generation, head of Machoza; "neither obligation nor mitzva, but optional," and the
  reader of Rabbi Elazar ben Arakh's verse.
- **Rav Nachman** (bar Yaakov) — third generation, Nehardea; washing before fruit is "of the arrogant."
- **Rabba bar bar Chana** — third generation, a Babylonian who studied under Rabbi Yochanan in Eretz
  Yisrael; the eyewitness to Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi.
- **Rav** (Abba Arikha) — first generation, founder of Sura; the two-joint reading, and leave to wash
  once in the morning.
- **Shmuel** — first generation, head of Nehardea; the strict single line for both.
- **Rav Sheshet** — third generation, a blind master of tradition at Nehardea and Mechoza; the lenient
  single line for both.
- **Rav Pappa** — fifth generation, founder of the academy at Naresh; dissolves the Tiberias dispute.
- **Bar Hedya** — a contemporary of Abaye and Rava (better known as a dream-interpreter); reports Rabbi
  Ami's practice.

*Look-alikes worth flagging:*
- **Rav Nachman** (bar Yaakov) and **Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak** are two different sages — and both
  appear on this daf.
- **Rabbi Elazar** (ben Pedat, the Amora) is **not** **Rabbi Elazar ben Arakh** (the Tanna) — again,
  both are on this daf.
- **Rabbi Ami and Rabbi Asi** (the Eretz-Yisrael pair) are neither **Rav Asi** the Babylonian nor **Rav
  Ashi** the redactor.
- **Rav Dimi** and **Ravin** are distinct travelers who often report the same tradition differently, as
  here.
