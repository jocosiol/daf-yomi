## The big picture (read this first)

Yesterday ended mid-argument. **Rav** had ruled there is no handle behind less than an olive-bulk of food
and no cover behind less than a bean-bulk; **R' Yochanan** said even less counts. Today the Gemara works
that dispute all the way through a stack of baraitot and mishnayot — and then opens two more questions on
the same theme, each about the *edge* of what still counts as an attachment.

The chapter's grammar has not changed. A part joined to food is a **handle** (*yad*), a **cover**
(*shomer*), or neither; a handle imports and exports impurity, a cover does that *and* joins to the
measure. Today's page presses on the word "joins" from two directions:

> **How little food** behind an attachment is still enough for it to count? And **how little of a thing**
> can itself serve as an attachment — down to a single hair?

Three movements, in order:

1. **The femur, and the measure dispute** (119a). A marrow-bone (*kulit*) with only a bean-bulk of flesh
   still draws the whole bone into impurity. Rav and R' Yochanan each square this with their view — and
   then **R' Chanina** and **R' Yochanan** split over whether "bean-bulk" is a genuine minimum or merely
   the number the baraita happened to echo. Three proofs are brought against the strict view and each is
   turned aside.
2. **Rav Oshaya's dilemma** (119a–b). A new question: if one food's protective cover is *divided* into
   sections, do the sections **join** to make up the measure? The onion-peel mishna is set beside it, and
   the same two proofs return — resolved the same way.
3. **Is a hair a handle?** (119b). Back to the two-bones Tosefta, and then the sharpest case: **Reish
   Lakish** says only a bone is a handle for flesh, not a hair; **R' Yochanan** says even a hair is. The
   mishna about touching a hair on a hide (124a) is thrown at the dispute, and a detour into how we write
   tefillin on a perforated hide.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **קוּלִית** *(kulit)* | A **femur** — a marrow-bone. Because it holds marrow, it counts as a **cover** (*shomer*) for the food inside, not merely a handle. Rava reads the baraita's word "*kulit*" as proof the case is about a cover. |
| **כְּפוֹל** *(k'pol)* | A **bean-bulk** — Acherim's minimum of flesh on a femur. The dispute: is this a real floor, or just a round figure echoing the first *tanna*'s olive-bulk? |
| **זֶהוּ שִׁיעוּר** *(zehu shiur)* | "**This is the measure**" — R' Chanina's view that a bean-bulk is the true minimum for a cover to join. R' Yochanan: *ein zeh shiur*, it is **not** a fixed measure; even less joins. |
| **בְּרִיָּה** *(beriya)* | A **distinct entity** — a whole small item (a single grain in its shell). Its cover joins even below a bean-bulk, so proofs from grains cannot settle the general rule. |
| **קוּלְחָא** *(kulcha)* | The **stalk** to which pods are attached. The Gemara's escape hatch: many proofs are really about a stalk serving as a **handle** for many pods, not a cover for one. |
| **שְׁנֵי שׁוֹמְרִין** *(shnei shomrin)* | **Two covers** — Rav Oshaya's case of a single food whose protective cover is split into separate sections. Do they *join* to the measure? |
| **שׁוֹמֵר עַל גַּבֵּי שׁוֹמֵר** *(shomer al gabei shomer)* | A **cover on top of a cover** — e.g. an onion's outer peel over its inner peel. As a rule, the outer one does not count; this is *not* Rav Oshaya's case. |
| **נִימָא** *(nima)* | A single **hair**. The thinnest possible attachment: Reish Lakish says it is no handle for flesh, R' Yochanan says it is. |
| **צִיב** *(tziv)* | A **strand of flesh** protruding from an olive-bulk on a hide. Touching it (or the hair opposite) conveys impurity — the mishna (124a) hurled at Reish Lakish. |
| **חַלְחוּלֵי מְחַלְחֵל** *(chalchulei mechalchel)* | "**It perforates through**" — the hair pierces the hide to touch the flesh directly, so it can be a *cover* even though the hide already covers the flesh. |
| **כְּתִיבָה תַּמָּה** *(ktiva tama)* | "**Perfect writing**" required for tefillin — the objection that a hide perforated by hair-roots could not be written on. Answered: ink covering a hole leaves it no hole. |
| **מְלַאי שֶׁבֵּין הַמְּלָאִין** *(melai shebein hamelaim)* | "**An awn among many awns**" — R' Ela's rule that one thin strand is a handle only when bundled with others you can grip. Applied here to a *hair among hairs*. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Yehuda ben Nekosa** — a Tanna who transmits in the name of Rabbi Yaakov; his challenge from yesterday
  reappears as the *gufa*: how can two bones, each with half an olive-bulk of flesh, join to a full one?
- **Rabbi Yaakov** — the Tanna in whose name Yehuda ben Nekosa speaks; several Tannaim bear the name and
  I would not pin down which is meant.
- **Acherim** — literally "Others," the anonymous voice that rules a bean-bulk of flesh suffices; the
  Talmud elsewhere identifies "Acherim" as **Rabbi Meir**.
- **The school of Rabbi Yishmael** (*d'vei R' Yishmael*) — the Tannaitic academy whose *beraita* on
  "any sowing seed" (Lev. 11:37) teaches that shells of wheat, barley, and lentils count as covers.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav** (Abba Arikha) — first-generation Amora, disciple of Rebbi and founder of Sura; holds no cover
  for less than a bean-bulk, the strict side carried through the daf.
- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation Amora of Meḥoza; reads the baraita's very word
  "*kulit*" as proof the case is about a **cover**, since a femur holds marrow. (**Rava** רבא — not
  **Rabbah** רבה.)
- **Rav Oshaya** — the Amora who raises the dilemma of two divided covers and later teaches a supporting
  baraita. More than one Sage carries this name (there is an early Eretz-Yisrael *Rav Oshaya Rabba*, the
  great compiler of baraitot); which is meant here I would not state with confidence.
- **Rav Acha, son of Rava** (*brei d'Rava*) — a later Amora; supplies the recurring escape — that the
  proof-texts speak of a *stalk* serving as a handle, and that "using [*bemishmishan*]" means "carrying."
- **Rav Acha bar Yaakov** — third-to-fourth-generation Amora; objects that hair perforating a hide would
  make it unfit for writing tefillin.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Yochanan** (bar Nafcha) — second-generation Amora and head of Tiberias; the expansive voice on
  both disputes — even below a bean-bulk a cover joins, and even a hair is a handle.
- **Reish Lakish** (Shimon ben Lakish) — R' Yochanan's brother-in-law and great disputant in Tiberias;
  holds only a bone is a handle for flesh, not a hair.
- **Rabbi Chanina** (bar Ḥama) — first-generation Eretz-Yisrael Amora, a senior figure in Tzippori; holds
  the bean-bulk is a genuine minimum (*zehu shiur*).
- **Rabbi Ela** (also spelled **Ilai** in the sugya) — an Eretz-Yisrael Amora; his rule about "an awn
  among many awns" is borrowed to explain how a single hair can be a handle.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 119a — Reconciling the femur baraita

**Squaring Rav with the earlier baraita.** *"וְרַב הַאי בְּמַאי מוֹקֵים לַהּ?"* — how does Rav read the
baraita (with Yehuda ben Nekosa) given his view? If it speaks of a **handle**, the opening clause is hard;
if a **cover**, the closing clause is. The Gemara answers *"אִיבָּעֵית אֵימָא"* — read it either way: as a
handle, Rav rules like Yehuda ben Nekosa; as a cover, like the first *tanna*. **R' Yochanan** reads the
whole baraita as **handle**, like the first *tanna*.

**The femur (*kulit*).** *"תָּא שְׁמַע"* — R' Yehuda: a femur with an **olive-bulk** of flesh draws the
whole bone into impurity. **Acherim:** even a **bean-bulk** does. The Gemara asks how Rav reads this too —
and again offers handle-or-cover. **R' Yochanan** reads it all as **cover**, like Acherim. **Objection:**
Acherim said *bean-bulk*, but R' Yochanan holds even *less* counts! **Answer:** *"אַיְּידֵי דְּקָאָמַר
תַּנָּא קַמָּא שִׁיעוּרָא"* — since the first *tanna* named a measure (olive-bulk), Acherim named one too
(bean-bulk), though he really holds even less suffices.

**Rava reads the word.** *"אָמַר רָבָא: דַּיְקָא נָמֵי דִּבְשׁוֹמֵר עָסְקִינַן, דְּקָתָנֵי קוּלִית"* — the
baraita's own wording proves it is about a **cover**: it says *kulit*, a marrow-bone, which protects the
food inside. Had it meant a handle it would have said "any bone." *Sh'ma minah.*

### 119a — Is the bean-bulk a real minimum?

**The dispute.** *"אִיתְּמַר"* — **R' Chanina:** *zehu shiur*, the bean-bulk is the genuine floor for a
cover to join. **R' Yochanan:** *ein zeh shiur*, it is no fixed measure — even less than a bean-bulk joins.
(R' Yochanan explains the baraita's "bean-bulk" the same way as before: it merely echoes the first
*tanna*'s number.)

**Three proofs against the strict view — all turned aside.**

- **R' Elazar ben Azarya's pods.** He rules a pod of **beans** pure (large beans need no protection) but a
  pod of small **legumes** impure — even one grain attached — "because one desires the use of the pod."
  This looks like a cover counting below a bean-bulk. **Rejected** (Rav Acha, son of Rava): the case is
  really a **stalk** touched by the creature — a *handle*, not a cover — and "*bemishmishan*" (desires its
  use) means "*betashmishan*," carrying the legumes by the stalk.
- **The school of R' Yishmael.** "Any sowing seed" (Lev. 11:37): wheat, barley, and lentils *in their
  shells* — all under a bean-bulk — so shells (covers) count below a bean-bulk. **Rejected:** *"בְּרִיָּה
  שָׁאנֵי"* — a whole grain is a **distinct entity**; the cover-law applies to it even when tiny, so it
  proves nothing about the general rule.

### 119a–b — Rav Oshaya's dilemma: two divided covers

**The question.** *"בָּעֵי רַב אוֹשַׁעְיָא: שְׁנֵי שׁוֹמְרִין מַהוּ שֶׁיִּצְטָרְפוּ?"* — do **two covers**
join with the food to reach the egg-bulk?

**What it is *not*.** Not a cover *on top of* a cover — the onion mishna (R' Yehuda: three peels; the
outer never joins) already settles that a *shomer al gabei shomer* does not count. Rather, it is **one
food whose single cover is split into sections**: this section guards this part, that section that part.
Since each guards its own part and the parts are one food, do the covers join — or not, since neither
guards the whole?

**Proofs and rejections.** The same two texts (REBA's pods; R' Yishmael's grains) are offered as
resolutions and turned aside the same way — *b'kulcha, mishum yad*. For the grains a fresh reading is
added: *"בְּשִׁדְרָה, וּמִשּׁוּם שׁוֹמֵר"* — the grains sit on one **stem** and all protect each other (if
one falls the rest fall), so it is not Rav Oshaya's case of covers each guarding only a part. And a single
large grain — *chitei d'Shimon ben Shatach* — can itself reach an egg-bulk. The dilemma is left unresolved.

### 119b — The two bones, and the hair

**The *gufa*.** *"שְׁנֵי עֲצָמוֹת וַעֲלֵיהֶן שְׁנֵי חֲצָאֵי זֵיתִים"* — two bones, each with half an
olive-bulk of a corpse's flesh; insert their bare ends into a house that overlies them, and the house is
impure. **Yehuda ben Nekosa** in the name of **R' Yaakov:** *"הֵיאַךְ שְׁנֵי עֲצָמוֹת מִצְטָרְפִין
לִכְזַיִת?"* — how do two half-olives on two bones ever join to a whole?

**The hair dispute.** *"אָמַר רֵישׁ לָקִישׁ: לֹא שָׁנוּ אֶלָּא עֶצֶם, דְּהָוֵי יָד, אֲבָל נִימָא לָא הָוְיָא
יָד"* — **Reish Lakish:** only a **bone** is a handle for flesh; a single **hair** attached to an
olive-bulk is not, so it does not carry impurity into a house. **R' Yochanan:** even a **hair** is a
handle.

**The objection from the hide (124a).** One who touches a **strand of flesh** (*tziv*) emerging from an
olive-bulk on a hide, or the **hair** opposite it, is impure. *"מַאי לָאו מִשּׁוּם יָד?"* — is that not
because the hair is a **handle**, against Reish Lakish? **Answer:** no — *mishum shomer*, the hair is a
**cover**. **But** — a cover over a cover (hide already covers the flesh)? *"חַלְחוּלֵי מְחַלְחֵל"* — the
hair **perforates through** the hide and touches the flesh directly.

**The tefillin detour.** *"מַתְקֵיף לַהּ רַב אַחָא בַּר יַעֲקֹב"* — if hairs perforate the hide, how do we
write tefillin, which need *ktiva tama*, perfect writing with no holes? **Answer** (from *the West*):
*"כׇּל נֶקֶב שֶׁהַדְּיוֹ עוֹבֵר עָלָיו אֵינוֹ נֶקֶב"* — any perforation the ink passes over is not a
disqualifying perforation.

**The other resolution.** *"וְאִיבָּעֵית אֵימָא, כּוּלֵּיהּ מִשּׁוּם יָד"* — the whole mishna is about a
**handle** after all, and does *not* refute Reish Lakish, because (as R' Ela taught of an awn) it speaks of
*a hair among many hairs* (*nima shebein hanimin*), which you can grip together with the flesh. A single
hair remains no handle. A second version (*lishana achrina*) recasts the whole dispute as being about
*several* hairs, not one — carrying into tomorrow.

## The distinctions, side by side

Today's page runs on a single theme — **the edge of what still counts** — argued through three named
disputes. In each, one Sage draws the line tight and R' Yochanan draws it wide. Every cell traces to a
line above.

| Disputed point | The stricter view | R' Yochanan's view |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum flesh for a **cover** to join (Acherim's "bean-bulk") | **R' Chanina:** the bean-bulk is the real floor (*zehu shiur*) | **No floor** — even less than a bean-bulk joins |
| Whether a single **hair** is a handle for flesh | **Reish Lakish:** only a bone is a handle, not a hair | Even a **hair** is a handle |
| Why touching a hair on a hide (124a) conveys impurity | **Reish Lakish:** the hair is a **cover** (it perforates to the flesh) | The hair is a **handle** |

---

## One line to carry with you

> The law of attachment has two edges — how little food behind it, and how little of it there is — and
> the whole daf is R' Yochanan pushing both edges outward while others hold the line: even a bean-bulk is
> not a floor, and even a single hair is a handle.
