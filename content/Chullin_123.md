## The big picture (read this first)

The daf opens by tidying up yesterday's four-*mil* measure. The tradition that Reish Lakish attached three
laws to it was doubly wrong: it was **Aivu** who said it, not R' Abbahu — a listener confused the two names —
and there were **four** laws, not three. The fourth is the mishna's own *kdei avoda*: skins trodden for the
time it takes to walk four *mil* are pure. A short bridge follows — the impurity a marauding **legion**
leaves in a house, because soldiers carry human scalps for witchcraft — and that talk of human skin ushers
in a new mishna about hides.

The new mishna is about **flaying** (*hamafshit*), and its real question is this:

> While you are peeling a hide off a carcass, at what point does the part you have already flayed stop being
> "flesh" — stop carrying and receiving the impurity of a carcass? It depends on **why** you are flaying:
> for a carpet, the answer is one measure; for a jug, another; flaying up from the legs, another still.

Underneath the mishna's measures runs the deeper current that occupies the whole daf: **when does a partial
severance end a thing's halakhic identity — given that it might still be put back together?** A hide half
peeled, a garment half torn, an oven half dismantled — is the changed part already "gone," or does the fact
that it *could* be restored keep it what it was? That is the question behind Rav vs R' Asi on the flayed
handbreadth, and behind the great Reish Lakish vs R' Yochanan dispute over whether a hide is **repairable**.

Four movements, in order:

1. **Closing the four-*mil* measure** (123a). Aivu, not R' Abbahu; four laws, not three — and the legion's
   scalps.
2. **The mishna of flaying** (123a). Carpet, jug, from the legs, and the neck-hide.
3. **Where does the flayed part turn pure?** (123a–b). Rav vs R' Asi on the handbreadth next to the flesh,
   the size of "the measure of grasping," and the torn-garment analogy.
4. **Is a hide "repairable"?** (123b). Reish Lakish vs R' Yochanan, R' Avin's "each piece is fallen," and
   the chain of objections that carries into tomorrow's oven.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **הַמַּפְשִׁיט** *(hamafshit)* | **One who flays** — peels the hide off a carcass. The mishna asks how much of the peeled hide still counts as flesh for impurity. |
| **כְּדֵי אֲחִיזָה** *(kdei achiza)* | The **measure of grasping** — flaying for a carpet, the hide stays "flesh" until this much is peeled. A handbreadth, and per Abaye a **folded** handbreadth (*tefach kaful*). |
| **לְשָׁטִיחַ / לְחֵמֶת** *(l'shatiach / l'chemet)* | Flaying **for a carpet** (cut head-to-tail, peeled from both sides) versus **for a jug** (cut a circle at the neck, peeled downward) — two methods, two measures. |
| **הַמַּרְגִּיל** *(hamargil)* | One who flays **starting from the legs** upward for a jug — here the whole hide is a connection until it is peeled off entirely. |
| **חִבּוּר** *(chibbur)* | A **connection** — hide still joined to the flesh so that impurity passes through it, both to receive impurity and to impart it. |
| **יָד** *(yad)* | A **handle** — the strip of hide one grips while flaying. It serves the flesh, so it conveys impurity. R' Asi's reason the near handbreadth stays impure. |
| **שׁוֹמֵר** *(shomer)* | A **protector** — a covering that guards the flesh; like a handle, it conveys impurity. Abaye's frame for the neck-hide dispute. |
| **טֶפַח הַסָּמוּךְ לַבָּשָׂר** *(tefach hasamuch labasar)* | The **handbreadth next to the flesh** — pure for Rav (already flayed), impure for R' Asi (still gripped as a handle). |
| **חָלִים** *(chalim)* | **Repairable** — Reish Lakish's category: a torn hide can be reattached, so it keeps its impurity; R' Yochanan holds even a hide is not repairable. |
| **רִאשׁוֹן רִאשׁוֹן עוֹשֶׂה נִיפּוּל** *(rishon rishon oseh nipul)* | "**Each piece, as flayed, is rendered fallen**" — R' Avin: a flayed strip is permanently detached and never rejoins, unlike a torn garment that can be sewn. |
| **טְבוּלַת יוֹם** *(tevulat yom)* | A garment **immersed that day**, still awaiting nightfall — the only case where tearing the majority (not all) of it purifies it. |
| **כְּדֵי מַעְפּוֹרֶת** *(kdei ma'aporet)* | The **measure of a scarf** — an untorn remnant this size keeps a torn garment a connection, and still impure. |

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 123a — Closing the four-*mil* measure

**The correction.** Yesterday's line — that Reish Lakish attached *three* laws to the four-*mil* measure, and
that R' Abbahu transmitted it — is set right. *"אַיְבוּ אַמְרַהּ… וְאַרְבְּעֵי אֲמַר בַּהּ"* — it was **Aivu**
who said it in Reish Lakish's name, a listener having confused Aivu with Abbahu, and there were **four**
laws, not three. The fourth is the mishna's own *kdei avoda*: skins trodden for the time it takes to walk
four *mil* are pure.

**How far must one go — and turn back?** **R' Yosei b'R' Ḥanina:** the four-*mil* rule for finding a synagogue
or water was taught only if it lies **ahead** of you; if it is **behind** you, *"אֲפִילּוּ מִיל אֶחָד אֵינוֹ
חוֹזֵר"* — you need not turn back even one *mil*. **Rav Aḥa bar Yaakov** infers the fine print: it is a full
*mil* one need not return, but for **less than a *mil*** behind you, you must go back.

**The legion's scalps.** A bridge to the coming mishna's talk of human skin: a **legion** (*ligyon*) on the
march, one soldier of which enters a house, renders the house impure — *"שֶׁאֵין לָךְ כׇּל לִיגְיוֹן… שֶׁאֵין
לוֹ כַּמָּה קַרְקִפְלִין"*, for every legion carries several human **scalps** for witchcraft. And do not be
surprised — the scalp of **R' Yishmael** was set on the heads of kings.

### 123a — The mishna: how far you flay, and why

**The cases.** *"הַמַּפְשִׁיט בַּבְּהֵמָה וּבַחַיָּה"* — one flaying any animal, kosher or not, small or large:

- **For a carpet** (*l'shatiach*) — cutting head-to-tail and peeling from both sides — the hide keeps its
  flesh-status *"כְּדֵי אֲחִיזָה"*, **until the measure of grasping** is flayed.
- **For a jug** (*l'chemet*) — cutting a circle at the neck and peeling downward — until he flays **the
  breast** (*chazeh*), the hardest part to peel.
- **Flaying up from the legs** (*hamargil*) for a jug — the **whole** hide is a connection, to receive and
  impart impurity, until it is peeled off entirely.
- **The neck-hide**, once all else is flayed — **R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri:** *"אֵינוֹ חִבּוּר"*, not a connection;
  **the Rabbis:** a connection *"עַד שֶׁיַּפְשִׁיט אֶת כּוּלּוֹ"*, until the whole is flayed.

### 123a — Where does the flayed part turn pure?

**Rav vs R' Asi.** The Gemara asks: once you have flayed *more* than the measure of grasping —
*"מִכָּאן וְאֵילָךְ מַאי?"* **Rav:** *"טָהוֹר הַמּוּפְשָׁט"*, the flayed part is **pure** (it no longer serves
the flesh as a handle). **R' Asi:** *"טֶפַח הַסָּמוּךְ לַבָּשָׂר טָמֵא"*, the **handbreadth next to the flesh**
is impure — because that is where the flayer grips, so it is still a **handle** (*yad*).

**Three challenges to R' Asi.** (1) A baraita: once flayed this much, one who touches the flayed hide is
pure — even, seemingly, the near handbreadth. *Answer:* it means everything **except** that handbreadth.
(2) A baraita distinguishing "hide opposite the flesh" (impure) from the near handbreadth (pure). *Answer:*
the *tanna* called the whole near handbreadth "the hide opposite the flesh." (3) The *Tosefta* flatly says
the near handbreadth is pure. *Answer:* that is the **first** handbreadth (*tefach rishon*) flayed past the
measure of grasping — where it is easier to grip the hide's *edge*, or to cut with a knife holding nothing;
only once **three** handbreadths are off does one grip the near handbreadth and it becomes a handle.

**How big is the measure of grasping?** One baraita: a handbreadth. Another: two! **Abaye:** a **folded**
handbreadth (*tefach kaful*) — and a *Tosefta* teaches exactly that.

### 123a–b — The torn garment, and Rabba against Rabba bar Avuh

**The garment rule.** *"טַלִּית שֶׁהִתְחִיל בָּהּ לְקוֹרְעָהּ"* (*Kelim* 28:8) — an impure garment one begins
to tear becomes pure once its **majority** is torn: it is no longer a usable garment. **Rav Naḥman in Rabba
bar Avuh's name:** this was taught only of a garment **immersed that day** (*tevulat yom*). Since its owner
already showed he did not spare it — he immersed it, which harms it — he will not spare it now, and will tear
the majority. A garment *not* immersed that day needs to be torn **entirely**, lest one tear only half and
call it a majority.

**Rabba's two refutations.** (1) The decree should apply even to a *tevul yom* garment — *"שֶׁמָּא יֹאמְרוּ
טְבִילָה בַּת יוֹמָא עוֹלָה"*, lest onlookers think same-day immersion suffices to use it before nightfall.
(2) On the very logic of "lest one tear only half," a **bird burnt-offering** according to **R' Elazar b'R'
Shimon** — who requires pinching only a *majority* of the two *simanim* — should likewise be decreed against,
lest one pinch only half.

**Rav Yosef's answers.** (1) *"קִרְעָהּ מוֹכִיחַ עָלֶיהָ"* — its **tear proves** it: no one will think an
immersed garment may be used before sunset, since the tear plainly explains its purity. (2)
*"כֹּהֲנִים זְרִיזִים הֵן"* — **the priests are vigilant**, so no decree is needed for the offering.

### 123b — Testing Rabba bar Avuh, and a *tereifa* that imparts impurity

**From our mishna.** More than the measure of grasping is **pure** — why not decree it impure, lest one
complete *only* the measure of grasping, touch a source of impurity, and be wrongly deemed pure? *Answer:*
the mishna's impurity here is only **rabbinic**, so no decree. *Objection:* a **pure** person flaying an
**impure** carcass is Torah-law impurity! *Answer:* it is a **tereifa**. *And can a tereifa impart impurity?*
Yes — per **Shmuel's father**: a slaughtered *tereifa* imparts impurity when it is a **consecrated** animal.

### 123b — Is a hide "repairable"? Reish Lakish vs R' Yochanan

**The dispute.** After Rav Huna limits the garment rule (an untorn **scarf's measure** left behind keeps it a
connection), the core dispute lands. **Reish Lakish:** the garment rule holds *"אֶלָּא טַלִּית, אֲבָל עוֹר —
חֲלִים"*, only for a **garment**; but a torn **hide** stays impure, because it is **repairable** (*chalim*) —
it can be reattached. **R' Yoḥanan:** *"אֲפִילּוּ עוֹר נָמֵי לָא חֲלִים"*, even a hide is **not** repairable.

**Four objections to Reish Lakish, and their answers:**

- **From straps (R' Yoḥanan):** a *midras*-impure hide, once reduced below five handbreadths, is pure — why,
  if it is repairable? *Reish Lakish:* "repairable" was said of a **straight** cut; here the hide is cut **in
  a circle** (*mekatze'a uva lo derech sevivotav*), which cannot be repaired.
- **From the flaying mishna (R' Yirmeya):** more than the measure of grasping is pure — why, if repairable?
  ***R' Avin* resolves it:** *"רִאשׁוֹן רִאשׁוֹן עוֹשֶׂה נִיפּוּל"* — **each piece, as it is flayed, is
  rendered fallen** and will never rejoin the flesh; unlike a torn garment, it is truly detached.
- **From the neck-hide (Rav Yosef):** R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri calls it *not* a connection — yet it was never
  even moved, so surely it "exists repaired"! **Abaye:** do not object from R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri — read the
  Rabbis' clause instead, which supports Reish Lakish. *Rather,* the mishna's dispute is about a
  **protector prone to detach by itself** (*shomer he'asuy linatek me'elav*): the Rabbis count it a
  protector, R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri does not — a different axis entirely.
- **From the oven (R' Yirmeya):** an impure oven is purified by dividing it in three and scraping off its
  plaster — the objection that opens, and runs into tomorrow's daf.

## The distinctions, side by side

The mishna sets a single question — *how far must you flay before the peeled hide stops being "flesh"?* — and
answers it case by case. Every row traces to the walkthrough above.

| Flaying case | Stays "flesh" (a connection) until… | The refinement or dispute |
|---|---|---|
| **For a carpet** (*shatiach*) | the **measure of grasping** is flayed | past it: flayed part pure (Rav) / near handbreadth impure as a handle (R' Asi) |
| **For a jug** (*chemet*) | the **breast** (*chazeh*) is flayed | the breast is hardest to peel, so it marks the point |
| **From the legs** (*hamargil*) | the **whole** hide is flayed off | the entire hide is a connection meanwhile |
| **The neck-hide** (all else flayed) | — | R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri: *not* a connection; the Rabbis: a connection until all is flayed |
