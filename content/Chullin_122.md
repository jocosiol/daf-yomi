## The big picture (read this first)

The daf opens by closing an argument left over from yesterday. Rav Huna had said the flesh-scraps on a
flayed hide impart carcass-impurity **only if a person gathered them** (*v'hu shekineso*). Whose view was
he following? The Gemara runs the statement past both R' Yishmael and R' Akiva, and settles it: Rav Huna
speaks for **R' Akiva** — and the whole reason a hide clears the flesh clinging to it is that **the hide
nullifies it**, no matter how the flesh came loose.

Then a **new mishna** opens the chapter's next theme:

> Most skin is not flesh — you cannot eat it, and it does not carry the impurity of a carcass. But **some
> skins are so soft they still count as flesh** — a young camel's hump, a young calf's head, the skin under
> a lamb's tail, the four soft *sheratzim*. And one skin is flesh for a reason all its own — **human skin**.

The Gemara's work is to fix the edges of that list: *when* is a camel "young," *when* is a calf "young,"
*why* is human skin impure at all, and *how long* is the "tanning-period" that finally strips a soft skin
of its flesh-status — a measure that turns out to govern prayer and hand-washing too.

Four movements, in order:

1. **Whose view was Rav Huna's?** (122a). Not R' Yishmael but **R' Akiva** — the hide nullifies the flesh
   whether an animal or a knife loosened it.
2. **The mishna** (122a). The catalogue of skins that count as flesh — and the exception of human skin.
3. **Defining the borderlines** (122a–b). Ulla on human skin; how "young" a camel and a calf must be;
   Rav and R' Chanina on the "hooves"; the derivation for the creeping animals.
4. **The four-mil measure** (122b). How long tanning takes — and the same span for reaching a synagogue
   or water.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **עוֹרוֹתֵיהֶן כִּבְשָׂרָן** *(oroteihen kivsaran)* | "**Their skins are like their flesh**" — the chapter's principle. A skin soft enough to be eaten carries impurity exactly as flesh does. |
| **מְבַטֵּל עוֹר** *(mevatel or)* | "**The hide nullifies**" — a hide of a certain size cancels the flesh-scraps clinging to it, so they impart no carcass-impurity. R' Akiva's reason. |
| **פְּלָטַתּוּ חַיָּה / סַכִּין** *(paltatu chaya / sakin)* | Flesh loosened from the hide **by an animal** tearing it, versus **by a knife** flaying it — the two ways scraps end up on a hide. |
| **חֲזִיר הַבָּר** *(chazir habar)* | The **wild boar**, whose hide is tougher than the domesticated pig's — hence the dispute over whether its skin counts as flesh. |
| **חֲטֶרֶת שֶׁל גָּמָל** *(chatéret shel gamal)* | The **hump of a camel** — soft, and flesh-like, only while the camel is still "young." |
| **גָּמָל הָרַכָּה** *(gamal harakah)* | A **young (soft) camel** — defined as one that "has not yet carried a burden." |
| **עֵגֶל הָרַךְ** *(egel harach)* | A **young calf** — "young" until its first year is out (Ulla) or as long as it suckles (R' Yoḥanan). |
| **גִּישְׁתָּא** *(gishta)* | The **texture** of a skin — R' Yehuda's criterion for whether a skin counts as flesh, rather than a verse. |
| **בְּלָשׁוֹן יָחִיד** *(b'lashon yachid)* | Teaching a mishna "**in the singular**" — as one individual sage's view, so the *halakha* need not follow it. |
| **כְּדֵי עֲבוֹדָה** *(kdei avoda)* | The **tanning-period** — treading a spread skin for this long strips it of flesh-status; fixed at four *mil*. |
| **אַרְבַּעַת מִילִין** *(arba'at milin)* | **Four *mil*** — the walking-time that measures tanning, and also how far one must go for a synagogue or water. |
| **שְׁמוֹנָה שְׁרָצִים** *(shemona sheratzim)* | The **eight creeping animals** of Leviticus 11; four of them (gecko, monitor, lizard, skink) have soft, flesh-like skin. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Yishmael** and **Rabbi Akiva** — the great third-generation disputants; here, whether a hide
  nullifies the flesh upon it (R' Yishmael — not; R' Akiva — yes). (**R' Yishmael** the Tanna, *not*
  **R' Yishmael bar Abba** the Amora who appears below.)
- **Rabbi Yehuda** (bar Ilai) — fourth-generation Tanna; the wild boar's skin counts too, and the lizard's
  does not, because he goes by **texture**.
- **Rabbi Ḥiyya** — a Tanna of the transitional generation; his baraita on a donkey's ear sewn into a
  basket presses the meaning of the mishna's "trod upon." (**R' Ḥiyya**, not **R' Ḥiyya bar Abba**.)
- **Elazar ben Yehuda of Aveilum** (in the name of **R' Yaakov**) and **R' Shimon ben Yehuda of Kefar
  Ikom** (in the name of **R' Shimon** [bar Yoḥai]) — the individual view that *all* the listed skins have
  flesh-status; the mishna is taught to match it. The two tradents are minor figures I can say little about
  with confidence.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Ulla** — second-generation Amora who carried Eretz-Yisrael teachings to Bavel; human skin is pure by
  Torah law, and a calf is "young" in its first year.
- **Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi** — first-generation Amora of Lod; a camel is "young" as long as it has not
  carried a burden.
- **Rabbi Yoḥanan** (bar Nafcha) — head of Tiberias; a calf is young as long as it suckles, and he rules
  that a young calf's head-skin does *not* in fact impart impurity — the mishna is taught "in the singular."
- **Reish Lakish** (Shimon ben Lakish) — his colleague and brother-in-law; asks after the young camel and
  honors the one who answers him.
- **Rabbi Zeira** — third-generation Amora who moved from Bavel to Eretz Yisrael; asks the same question
  and answers his tradent sharply — the foil in the daf's aggadic aside.
- **Rabbi Yirmeya** — fourth-generation Amora, sharp questioner; raises the dilemma of a camel whose time
  to carry has come but has not carried.
- **Rabbi Ḥanina** — first-generation Amora of Tzippori; the "hooves" means the knee sold with the head.
- **Rabbi Yishmael bar Abba** and **Ravin bar Ḥinnana** — the tradents who answer Reish Lakish and R'
  Zeira on the young camel. Minor figures.
- **Rabbi Abbahu** — third-generation Amora of Caesarea; transmits the four-*mil* rulings for the kneader,
  for prayer, and for washing the hands, in the name of Reish Lakish.
- **Rabbi Yannai** — first-generation Amora; fixes the tanning-period at four *mil*.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav Huna** — second-generation head of Sura; the scraps count only when gathered, and (via R' Yannai)
  the tanning-period is four *mil*.
- **Rav** (Abba Arikha) — founder of Sura; "hooves" means literally the hooves, and he reads "after its
  kinds" as interrupting the verse. (**Rav**, not **Rava**.)
- **Rabbi Yirmeya** and **Abaye** (head of Pumbedita) — each raises an unresolved dilemma on the young
  camel (*teiku*).
- **Rav Shmuel bar Yitzḥak** — third-generation Amora; Rav has the standing of a Tanna and teaches the
  chameleon too.
- **Rav Sheshet son of Rav Idi** — later Amora; our mishna's tanna follows R' Yehuda, going by texture.
- **Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak** — head of Pumbedita; his statement opens as the daf ends (it runs into 123a).

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 122a — Whose view was Rav Huna's?

**The question.** *"אַלִּיבָּא דְּמַאן?"* — Rav Huna said the flesh-scraps impart carcass-impurity only
*v'hu shekineso*, once gathered. Whose view is that? The mishna (124a) has a hide bearing two half-olives
of flesh — **R' Yishmael:** it imparts impurity by *carrying* but not by *contact* (one touches them
apart, carries them together); **R' Akiva:** it imparts by neither, for the hide nullifies the flesh.

**The squeeze.** If Rav Huna follows **R' Yishmael** — but R' Yishmael says the hide does *not* nullify, so
the scraps should count without gathering. If he follows **R' Akiva** — it is obvious, since R' Akiva says
the hide nullifies outright.

**A first answer.** He follows R' Yishmael; and R' Yishmael's "hide does not nullify" is only where **an
animal** tore the flesh loose (*paltatu chaya*), whereas a **knife** (*paltatu sakin*) does nullify.

**The refutation.** From R' Yehuda's *alal hamechunas* (yesterday's line) with Rav Huna's gloss
*v'hu shekineso*: that must be knife-flayed flesh that was gathered, yet it still imparts impurity — so a
knife does *not* nullify for R' Yishmael, and Rav Huna cannot be squaring the two cases.

**The settled reading.** *"רַב הוּנָא דְּאָמַר כְּרַבִּי עֲקִיבָא"* — Rav Huna follows **R' Akiva**. And it
is not obvious, *lest you say* R' Akiva nullifies only knife-flayed flesh; Rav Huna teaches that R' Akiva's
reason is simply *"מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהָעוֹר מְבַטְּלָן"*, **because the hide nullifies them** — animal-torn or
knife-flayed alike.

### 122a — The mishna: skins that count as flesh

**The list.** *"אֵלּוּ שֶׁעוֹרוֹתֵיהֶן כִּבְשָׂרָן"* — the skin of a human corpse; the skin of a
**domesticated pig** (soft, and eaten by gentiles); the hump-skin of a **young camel**; the head-skin of a
**young calf**; the hide of the **hooves**; the skin of the **womb**; the skin of a **fetus**; the skin
**beneath the tail**; and the skin of the four soft *sheratzim* — the **gecko, desert monitor, lizard, and
skink**. **R' Yehuda:** add the **wild boar**; but the **lizard** is like the **weasel** — not flesh.

**The closing clause.** All of these, once **tanned** or **trodden** for the tanning-period, are pure —
*except human skin*. **R' Yoḥanan ben Nuri:** all *eight* creeping animals have (non-flesh) skins.

### 122a — Human skin, and how "young" is young

**Ulla on human skin.** *"דְּבַר תּוֹרָה עוֹר אָדָם טָהוֹר"* — by Torah law human skin is pure; the Sages
declared it impure only as a **decree, lest a person make mats from the skins of his father and mother.**
Some attach this to the *reisha* (untanned skin), some to the *seifa* (tanned) — and whoever says it of the
reisha says it all the more of the seifa; whoever says it only of the seifa holds untanned human skin
impure by Torah law.

**The pig.** First tanna and R' Yehuda argue only over **texture** — is the wild boar's skin tough
(*ashun*) or soft (*rakhikh*)?

**The young camel.** *"וְכַמָּה גָּמָל הָרַכָּה?"* — Ulla in R' Yehoshua ben Levi's name:
*"כׇּל זְמַן שֶׁלֹּא טָעֲנָה"*, **as long as it has not carried a burden.** R' Yirmeya asks: its time to
carry came but it did not carry — what then? Abaye asks the mirror case — its time had not come but it
carried anyway. Both *teiku*.

**The aggadic aside.** Reish Lakish and R' Zeira each raised the same question and were each given the same
answer. Reish Lakish — famously **harsh** — *honored* R' Yishmael bar Abba ("sit opposite me"); R' Zeira —
one of the **saintly** of Bavel — snapped at Ravin bar Ḥinnana ("do you have only one *halakha* to say?").
*"מָה בֵּין תַּקִּיפֵי אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל לַחֲסִידֵי דְבָבֶל"* — see the difference between the harsh scholars
of Eretz Yisrael and the saintly ones of Bavel.

**The young calf.** *"וְכַמָּה עֵגֶל הָרַךְ?"* — **Ulla:** in its first year (*ben shnato*). **R' Yoḥanan:**
as long as it suckles.

### 122b — Reading the calf-dispute, and R' Yoḥanan's own ruling

**Resolving the terms.** The two are reconciled: Ulla means both first-year *and* suckling; R' Yoḥanan
means suckling even past the first year — proved from his bare phrase "as long as it suckles," with no
added "and provided it suckles."

**R' Yoḥanan's surprise.** Reish Lakish asks him whether a young calf's head-skin actually imparts
impurity; R' Yoḥanan: *"אֵינוֹ מְטַמֵּא"*, **it does not.** But you taught us the mishna that lists it!
*"אַל תַּקְנִיטֵנִי, בִּלְשׁוֹן יָחִיד אֲנִי שׁוֹנֶה אוֹתָהּ"* — do not provoke me; **I teach it in the
singular**, as an individual view. The proof: a baraita where only the **Rabbis** and the individual
**Elazar ben Yehuda** dispute how far the flesh-status of these skins reaches.

### 122b — The hooves, the creeping animals, the tanning-period

**The "hooves."** **Rav:** literally the hooves. **R' Ḥanina:** the **knee** (*rechuva*) sold together with
the head.

**The creeping animals.** A baraita reads *"הַטְּמֵאִים"* (Lev. 11) as **including their skins** as flesh;
*"אֵלֶּה"* ("these") limits it to some. But "these" is written over all eight! **Rav:** *"לְמִינֵהוּ"*
("after its kinds") **interrupts** the passage, so only the latter animals are included. Then let the
**chameleon** count too — **Rav Shmuel bar Yitzḥak:** Rav has the standing of a **Tanna** and does teach
the chameleon. And our mishna, which omits it, follows **R' Yehuda**, who goes by **texture** (*gishta*) —
the dispute over the lizard is a dispute over how soft *its* skin is.

**"Trod upon."** The mishna implies treading is required; but R' Ḥiyya taught that a donkey's ear **sewn**
into a basket is pure without treading! Answer: **sewing is itself a nullifying act**; a spread skin needs
treading.

**The four-*mil* measure.** *"כַּמָּה כְּדֵי עִבּוּד?"* — Rav Huna in R' Yannai's name: **four *mil*** (the
time to walk them). And R' Abbahu in Reish Lakish's name extends the span: a **kneader** guarding purity, a
traveler seeking a **synagogue** to pray, and one seeking **water** to wash before eating — each must go up
to four *mil*. Rav Naḥman bar Yitzḥak's qualification opens as the daf ends.

## The distinctions, side by side

The mishna's catalogue is really a set of borderline calls — each skin counts as flesh, but only under a
condition or against a dissent. Every row traces to a line above.

| Skin | Counts as flesh? (first tanna) | The dissent or the limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Human corpse** | Yes — impure | Ulla — pure by Torah law; impure only by decree (mats from parents) |
| **Domesticated pig** | Yes — the skin is soft | R' Yehuda — the wild boar too; they argue only over texture |
| **Young camel's hump** | Yes, while "young" | "Young" — as long as it has not carried a burden |
| **Young calf's head** | Yes, while "young" | Ulla — first year; R' Yoḥanan — while suckling, and it does *not* in fact impart |
| **Lizard** (*leta'a*) | Yes — soft texture | R' Yehuda — like the weasel; its skin is *not* flesh |

## One line to carry with you

> A skin becomes flesh only where it is soft enough to be eaten — so the whole chapter is spent asking, of a
> young calf, a young camel, a lizard, exactly how soft is soft enough, and for how long.
