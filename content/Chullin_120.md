## The big picture (read this first)

The chapter's opening mishna lists eight things that **join** with meat to make up the egg-bulk of
food-impurity: the hide, the gravy, the spices, the meat-residue, the bones, the tendons, the horns, and
the hooves. Yesterday the Gemara fought over whether a *hair* joins; today it turns to the mishna's own
vocabulary — and one gloss (*rotev* = **congealed** fat) opens the door to the daf's real theme:

> **When a solid food is melted or its liquid squeezed out, does the liquid still carry the solid's
> law?** For impurity, for a food-prohibition, for a tithe — is the juice treated like its source?

The answer runs on a single word. Three times the Torah adds **"soul" (*nefesh*)** to a prohibition, and
Reish Lakish reads each as *l'rabot et hashoteh* — **to include one who drinks** the forbidden food after
melting it down. Once that principle is fixed, the Gemara stress-tests it, then chases the same
"liquid-follows-solid" question through creeping animals, tithed produce, and consecrated food.

Four movements, in order:

1. **A hair as a cover** (120a). The tail of yesterday's dispute — Reish Lakish and R' Yochanan on
   whether a *hair* joins the meat as a cover — with the tefillin detour.
2. **What are *rotev* and *kifa*?** (120a). Gravy is **congealed fat** (only a solid joins, since liquid
   and solid share no common measure); spices are **spices**.
3. **"Soul" includes the drinker** (120a). Melt forbidden fat and swallow it, dissolve leavened bread and
   drink it, liquefy a bird carcass and drink it — all forbidden, from *nefesh*. And why the Torah needed
   the word three separate times.
4. **Liquid follows solid, everywhere** (120a–b). The juice of creeping animals, then the liquids of
   tithed and consecrated produce — derived through first-fruits and *teruma* — closing on R' Eliezer
   against R' Yehoshua over how much a derivation carries with it.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **רוֹטֶב** *(rotev)* | **Gravy** — glossed by Abaye as **congealed fat** (*chelev dikrish*) that oozed from meat. Inedible itself, so not susceptible on its own, yet it **joins** the meat toward the egg-bulk. |
| **קֵיפֶה** *(kifa)* | Literally a settled hash; the mishna's *kifa* is glossed by Rav Pappa as **spices** — not eaten themselves, but joining the meat to the measure. |
| **חֵלֶב דִּקְרִישׁ** *(chelev dikrish)* | **Congealed fat.** Only when solid does the fat join the meat, because impurity needs a shared measure — and liquid and solid do not share one. |
| **יִשּׁוּב הַדַּעַת** *(yishuv hadaat)* | "**Settling the mind**" — the standard for Yom Kippur liability: any amount that settles a person's mind makes him liable, so even *liquid* brine counts there. |
| **אִיצְטְרוֹפֵי** *(itztarufei)* | **Joining** for impurity — which requires a **common measure**. Since liquids and solids have different measures, they do not join; hence only *congealed* fat counts. |
| **אַחְשְׁבֵיהּ** *(achshevei)* | "**He ascribed significance to it.**" By taking the trouble to congeal blood and eat it, a person treats it as food — so he is liable though blood is not normally eaten that way. |
| **נֶפֶשׁ לְרַבּוֹת הַשּׁוֹתֶה** *(nefesh l'rabot hashoteh)* | "**'Soul' — to include one who drinks.**" The extra word *nefesh* extends a prohibition written as "eating" to one who **drinks** the food melted down. |
| **מַשְׁקִין הַיּוֹצְאִין מֵהֶן כְּמוֹתָן** *(mashkin hayotzin mehen k'motan)* | "**The liquids that come out of them are like them**" — the juice of a forbidden or tithed solid carries the same law as the solid. |
| **טוּמְאָה בְּמַשֶּׁהוּ** *(tumah b'mashehu)* | Creeping animals impart impurity in **any amount** — a stringency unique to them, which blocks deriving other laws from theirs. |
| **דּוּן מִינַּהּ וּמִינַּהּ** *(dun minah umina)* | "**Infer from it, and again from it**" — R' Eliezer: carry *all* the source's details to the derived case (even other species). |
| **דּוּן מִינַּהּ וְאוֹקֵי בְּאַתְרַהּ** *(dun minah v'oki b'atrah)* | "**Infer from it but keep it in its own place**" — R' Yehoshua: take only the principle, and otherwise judge the new case by its own rules (wine and oil only). |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Yosei** (ben Ḥalafta) — fourth-generation Tanna; derives from "*pri*" that first-fruits are
  brought as fruit, and from "*you shall bring*" that pressed wine counts too — the hinge for "liquid
  follows solid."
- **Rabbi Eliezer** (ben Hyrcanus) — early Tanna of Lod; holds *dun minah umina* — even the juice of
  other *teruma* species obligates payment.
- **Rabbi Yehoshua** (ben Ḥananya) — his contemporary and frequent disputant; holds *dun minah v'oki
  b'atrah* — only wine and oil, so the juice of other species is exempt. Two further mishnayot are shown
  to follow him.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rava** (bar Yosef bar Ḥama) — fourth-generation Amora of Meḥoza; first glosses *rotev* as floating
  fat. (**Rava** רבא — not **Rabbah** רבה.)
- **Abaye** (bar Kaylil) — Rava's lifelong study-partner, head of Pumbedita; corrects both glosses —
  floating fat and hash are edible, so *rotev* must be **congealed** fat.
- **Rabbah** (bar Naḥmani) — third-generation head of Pumbedita; glosses *kifa* as a congealed hash.
  (**Rabbah** רבה — the earlier Pumbeditan, not **Rava** רבא.)
- **Rav Pappa** — fifth-generation Amora, founder of the academy at Naresh; gives the accepted gloss,
  *kifa* = **spices**.
- **Rav Aḥa** — an Amora who raises the tefillin objection to the "hair perforates the hide" answer.
  Several Sages bear this name; which is meant I would not pin down.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Reish Lakish** (Shimon ben Lakish) — second-generation Amora of Tiberias; the engine of the daf — a
  hair is not a cover; brine joins on Yom Kippur; and the "*nefesh*" derashot that include the drinker.
- **Rabbi Yoḥanan** (bar Nafcha) — head of Tiberias and Reish Lakish's disputant; holds a hair *is* a
  cover for the meat.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 120a — A hair as a cover (finishing 119)

The daf opens mid-argument, now on the **chapter's own mishna** (hide, gravy, and the rest all join).
**Reish Lakish:** only a bone and the like join as a **cover** — a hair does not. **R' Yochanan:** even a
**hair** is a cover. **Reish Lakish's challenge:** a cover on top of a cover? — the hide already covers the
flesh. **R' Yochanan:** *"חַלְחוּלֵי מְחַלְחֵל"* — the hair perforates the hide to the flesh. **The
tefillin detour** returns (Rav Aḥa: perforations would ruin *ktiva tama*; answer: ink over a hole is no
hole), and the hide-mishna about touching a hair is read as a **handle** — *a hair among many hairs*.

### 120a — What are *rotev* and *kifa*?

**Gravy.** *"מַאי רוֹטֶב? אָמַר רָבָא: שׁוּמְנָא"* — Rava: floating fat. **Abaye:** that is edible — it
would be susceptible on its own! Rather, *chelev dikrish* — **congealed** fat that oozed out and is not
eaten, yet joins the meat.

**Why *congealed*?** *"מַאי אִירְיָא קְרִישׁ?"* — even liquid fat should join, for **Reish Lakish** said
brine on a vegetable combines with it to a large-date-bulk (*kakotevet*) for Yom Kippur liability!
**Answer:** the two laws run on different standards. Yom Kippur is about *yishuv daat* — any amount that
settles the mind makes one liable, liquid included. Impurity is about *itztarufei* — **joining needs a
common measure**, and liquids and solids share none. So only *congealed* (solid) fat joins.

**Spices.** *"מַאי קֵיפֶה? אָמַר רַבָּה: פִּירְמָא"* — Rabbah: a hash. **Abaye:** edible, so susceptible on
its own. **Rav Pappa:** *tavlin* — **spices**, not eaten themselves but joining the meat.

### 120a — "Soul" includes the drinker

**The baraita.** One who **congealed blood and ate it**, or **melted forbidden fat and swallowed it**, is
liable. Blood: *"אַחְשׁוֹבֵי אַחְשְׁבֵיהּ"* — by congealing it he ascribed food-status to it. But
forbidden fat — the Torah wrote *eating*, and swallowing a liquid is not eating! **Reish Lakish:**
*"אָמַר קְרָא נֶפֶשׁ – לְרַבּוֹת אֶת הַשּׁוֹתֶה"* — the word "soul" **includes the drinker**.

**The same word, twice more.** For **leavened bread** dissolved and drunk on Pesach (karet), and for a
**kosher-bird carcass** liquefied in fire and drunk (impurity) — each verse's *nefesh* includes the
drinker. (A carcass melted in the *sun* is pure — it rots first and is unfit.)

### 120a — Why "soul" is written three times (tzerichuta)

*"וּצְרִיכִי"* — each *nefesh* is needed, because no case can be derived from another: each carries a
stringency the others lack (see the grid below). And the reverse — deriving one from the *other two* —
also fails on every attempt. So all three are necessary.

### 120a–b — Liquid follows solid, everywhere

**Creeping animals.** *"הַטְּמֵאִים"* (Lev. 11:31) forbids their juice (*tzir*), gravy, and sediment. Why
a verse — derive it from the three above? Because of *dayo*: an inference is no stronger than its source,
so one would require a *kezayit*; the verse teaches liability even for a **lentil-bulk**, like the
creature itself. **120b:** and the reverse — write only creeping animals and derive the rest? No: their
impurity is *b'mashehu*, in **any amount**, a stringency the others lack.

**Tithed and consecrated produce.** A baraita: for *tevel*, the new crop, consecrated produce, Sabbatical
produce, and diverse-kinds — *mashkin hayotzin mehen k'motan*, the liquid is forbidden like the solid.
Source? The self-developing prohibitions come from the set; **consecrated** produce (which needs a human
act) is derived from **first-fruits** — R' Yosei: "*pri*," you bring fruit, and "*you shall bring*"
includes pressed wine. That is refuted (first-fruits need recitation and placement), so it is derived from
***teruma*** instead (linked to first-fruits by "*the offering of your hand*"). *Teruma* too is refuted
(death and a fifth), so consecrated produce is derived from **teruma plus one of the others**.

### 120b — Dun minah: R' Eliezer against R' Yehoshua

**The mishna** (*Terumot*): one who ate date-honey, apple-wine, or teruma-grape vinegar — **R' Eliezer**
obligates principal-and-a-fifth; **R' Yehoshua** exempts. **The root of it:** *"דּוּן מִינַּהּ וּמִינַּהּ"*
versus *"דּוּן מִינַּהּ וְאוֹקֵי בְּאַתְרַהּ."* **R' Eliezer** carries *every* detail from first-fruits to
*teruma* — even the juice of *other* species is like the produce. **R' Yehoshua** takes only the principle
and keeps *teruma* in its own frame — wine and oil only, so other juices are exempt. Two further mishnayot
(first-fruits brought only from olives and grapes; *orla* lashes only for their juice) are shown to follow
**R' Yehoshua**.

## The distinctions, side by side

The heart of the daf is the *tzerichuta*: why the Torah wrote "soul" three times rather than deriving one
melted-and-drunk prohibition from the others. Each case carries a stringency the others lack, so none can
be spared — every cell traces to the Gemara's own back-and-forth.

| Case (melted, then drunk) | Punishable by *karet* | Had a period of fitness | Ever partly permitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forbidden fat** (*chelev*) | **Yes** | No | **Yes** — fat of undomesticated animals |
| **Leavened bread** (*chametz*) | **Yes** | **Yes** — before Pesach | No |
| **Kosher-bird carcass** (*neveila*) | No | **Yes** | No |

---

## One line to carry with you

> Melt a forbidden food to a liquid and you have not escaped it — one added word, "soul," follows it down
> the throat. What is true of the solid is true of what pours out of it.
