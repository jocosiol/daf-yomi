## The big picture (read this first)

Two rulings from yesterday are still standing, and the whole of today is the Gemara throwing
proof-texts at them, one after another, to see if they buckle.

The first is **Rav Ḥisda's**: one who damages the priestly gifts, or eats them, owes the kohen
**nothing**. Behind it lies a sharp idea — the gifts are *money that has no claimant*. There is
no one who can drag you to court over them, and where there is no plaintiff there is no suit. The
top of 131a hurls three cases at this (a seized threshing floor, a sale of innards, a list of "the
priest's property"), and each time the Gemara shows the case turns on something else — benefit,
intactness, or a technical use of the word "property" — not on a kohen's power to sue.

The second ruling is **Rav's**, and it takes over the daf. A Levite kept snatching the gifts, and
Rav's reaction reveals a doubt he never resolved: **are Levites included in "the people"** (*am*)
of the verse "this shall be the priests' due *from the people*"? If they are, the gifts can be
forced out of them; if not, the Torah exempted them. Rav simply does not know — so he leaves the
Levite alone, because the burden of proof is on whoever would take.

> The engine of the daf is a single word — *am*. Every objection that follows is really asking:
> does this baraita quietly assume Levites are "the people" or that they are not? And every answer
> is Rav's escape hatch — reread the case so it never rides on that assumption at all.

In the middle of all this sits a self-contained gem: a baraita mapping **every gift left to the
poor** — gleanings, the forgotten sheaf, the corner, fallen grapes, defective clusters — against
the poor man's tithe, and asking of each whether the owner may choose *whom* to give it to. The
answer turns on a single Hebrew verb in each verse: *leave* versus *give*.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| מַתְּנוֹת כְּהוּנָּה — *mattenot kehuna* | The priestly gifts; in this chapter the three cuts of a non-sacred animal owed to the kohen. |
| הַזְּרוֹעַ וְהַלְּחָיַיִם וְהַקֵּבָה — *hazeroa vehalechayayim vehakeiva* | The foreleg, the jaw (with cheeks and tongue), and the maw — the three gifts themselves. |
| אִיקְּרוֹ "עַם" — *ikru "am"* | "Are they called 'the people'?" — the unresolved question of whether Levites fall under "from the people" and so must give the gifts. |
| טוֹבַת הֲנָאָה — *tovat hana'a* | The benefit of discretion — the owner's right to choose which poor person or kohen receives the gift. |
| מִשְׁתָּרְשֵׁי לֵיהּ — *mishtarshei leih* | "It works out as gain for him" — he is spared money he would otherwise have paid, so the obligation is about his benefit, not a claimant's suit. |
| אַנְפָּרוּת — *anparot* | A seizure for no reason (as opposed to seizure in payment of a debt). |
| נִכְסֵי כֹהֵן — *nichsei kohen* | "The property of a priest" — a legal label meaning he may trade with them and creditors may collect them, not that a court hands them over. |
| עֲזִיבָה / נְתִינָה — *aziva / netina* | "Leaving" versus "giving" — the two Torah verbs that decide whether a gift carries the benefit of discretion. |
| מַעֲשֵׂר רִאשׁוֹן — *maaser rishon* | First tithe, ordinarily the Levite's portion. |
| רֵאשִׁית הַגֵּז — *reishit hagez* | The first shearing of wool, owed to the kohen, with no "from the people" attached to its verse. |
| קַנְסִינְהוּ עֶזְרָא — *kansinhu Ezra* | "Ezra penalized them" — his decree stripping the Levites of the first tithe after they failed to return from Babylon. |
| תְּיוּבְתָּא — *teyuvta* | A conclusive refutation — an objection the Gemara cannot answer, that overturns a position. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Babylonian Amoraim**
- **Rav** — first-generation Amora, disciple of Rebbi in Eretz Yisrael, founder of the academy at Sura. The whole "are Levites *am*?" thread is his unresolved doubt.
- **Rav Ḥisda** — second/third-generation Amora of Sura, student of Rav. Author of the "no payment for damaging the gifts" ruling under fire here.
- **Rav Pappa** — fifth-generation Amora, student of Abaye and Rava, headed the academy at Naresh. Sits transmitting Rav's teaching when Rav Idi objects.
- **Rav Idi bar Avin** — fourth/fifth-generation Amora, contemporary of Rav Pappa; presses the objection from the poor-gifts baraita.
- **Mareimar** — late Babylonian Amora of the Rav Ashi era; issues the closing halakhic ruling on both disputes.
- **Rava** — fourth-generation Amora of Meḥoza. Raises the objection to Ulla. (Not to be confused with **Rabbah** רבה; here the text is **Rava** רבא.)

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**
- **Ulla** (bar Yishmael) — third-generation Amora who traveled between Eretz Yisrael and Babylon; his practice of giving the gifts to a female priest closes the daf.
- **Rabbi Ile'a** (Ela) — Eretz-Yisrael Amora; supplies the *gezeira shava* obligating even a poor owner in the poor man's tithe.
- **Rabbi Levi** — Eretz-Yisrael Amora and noted aggadist; reads "after you" as forgotten produce. (If this is a different R' Levi I would not want to overstate; the identification as the aggadist is the usual one.)

**Tannaim**
- **Rabbi Akiva** — third-generation Tanna of Bnei Brak; holds first tithe goes to the Levite.
- **Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya** — second/third-generation Tanna; holds first tithe may go to the kohen as well.
- **The school of Rabbi Yishmael** — the study-hall of the third-generation Tanna R' Yishmael; reads "you shall not go over the boughs" as "do not strip away all its splendor."

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 131a — three cases thrown at Rav Ḥisda

**Picking up from 130b.** A baraita had a kohen who receives produce while it is still *tevel*
(untithed) and acquires it; the daf explains this holds that **gifts not yet separated are treated
as if already separated** (*mattanot shelo hurmu k'mi shehurmu*), so the priest's portion already
has the status of *teruma*.

**First objection.** *Ta shema* — the king's household seized a man's threshing floor. **If for a
debt** he owed the crown, he must tithe replacement grain (the seizure is like a sale of untithed
produce); **if for no reason** (*anparot*), he is exempt. That the debt-case obliges him seems to
prove the tithe is "money with claimants" — against Rav Ḥisda.

**The answer.** It is different there, **because it simply gains him money** (*mishtarshei leih*):
were he exempt, he would pocket the value of grain he was already bound to tithe. The obligation is
about *his* windfall, not a kohen's right to sue.

**Second objection.** *Ta shema* from the mishna (132a): "Sell me a cow's innards," and the **maw**
(a gift) was among them — the buyer must give it to the kohen and the seller may not deduct its
value. **Why?** Let it count as damaging or eating the gifts, which Rav Ḥisda exempts! **The
answer:** it is different there, **because the gifts are intact** (*b'einaihu*) — a distinct object
sitting in front of you, which of course must be handed over. Rav Ḥisda spoke of gifts no longer
identifiable as such.

**Third objection.** *Ta shema* — a baraita lists **nine items that are "the property of a priest"**
(*nichsei kohen*): teruma, teruma of the tithe, ḥalla, the first shearing, the gifts, demai, first
fruits, and the principal and the added fifth. Is "property" not about **extracting them through
judges**? **No** — it is the sense of *Bikkurim* 3:12: a kohen may buy slaves, land, or a
non-kosher animal with them, a creditor collects them for a debt, a wife for her marriage contract,
and he may buy a Torah scroll. "Property" describes what he can *do* with them, not a court order.

**The snatching Levite, and Rav's doubt.** A certain Levite kept grabbing the gifts from children
carrying them to the priests. Rav's retort — "isn't it enough that we don't take the gifts *from
him*, and he snatches too?!" — implies there might be grounds to take them. The Gemara sharpens it:
**if Levites are "the people," take the gifts from them; if not, the Torah exempted them** — either
way there is no in-between. **The resolution:** Rav is genuinely **uncertain** (*mesapka leih*), and
under doubt the one who would take bears the burden of proof, so the Levite is left alone.

**Rav Idi's objection — the gifts to the poor.** Rav Pappa was transmitting Rav's doubt when Rav
Idi bar Avin objected from a baraita that catalogues the poor's gifts: **four in a vineyard** —
fallen grapes (*peret*), defective clusters (*olelot*), the forgotten cluster, and the corner
(*pe'a*); **three in grain** — gleanings (*leket*), the forgotten sheaf, and *pe'a*; **two on a
tree** — the forgotten fruit and *pe'a*. For all of these the owner has **no benefit of discretion**,
and even a poor Israelite who owns the field is forced by the court to leave them. The daf then
lays out the verse for each (Leviticus 19:10; the "after you" of Deuteronomy 24:20–21; the school
of R' Yishmael on the olive) — the objection itself waits for 131b.

### 131b — the objection lands, and Rav answers "it's a machloket"

**Why no discretion, and why the tithe is different.** For the field-gifts the verse writes
**"leaving"** (*aziva*) — you abandon them where they lie — so no choosing. But the **poor man's
tithe** split at home writes **"giving"** (*netina*) — "and you shall give it" — an act the owner
controls, so it *does* carry the benefit of discretion. And a poor owner is still bound by both, by
the "for the poor" of the gleaning verse and R' Ile'a's *gezeira shava* on "to the stranger."

**Now the objection bites.** The baraita ended: the gifts "are not taken from a Levite for a
Levite." By inference, from a Levite **for a kohen** they *are* — so Levites *are* "the people," and
Rav should not be in doubt!

**Rav's escape — "like the foreleg but not the foreleg."** The baraita's "gift" is not the actual
foreleg-jaw-maw but **first tithe** (*maaser rishon*). — But first tithe is the *Levite's*! — It
follows **Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya**, who says first tithe may go to the kohen. — But he said "*as
well*," not "to the kohen and not the Levite"! — Yes: **after Ezra penalized** the Levites for not
returning from Babylon. — But Ezra only decreed *don't give* it to them, not *take* it *from* them!
— **Rather**, the case is **reishit hagez**, the first shearing, whose verse never says "from the
people," so even a Levite owes it. Each retreat keeps the ruling off the word *am*.

**Two more *ta shema*'s, same move.** A baraita's principle — items **of sanctity** (teruma, ḥalla)
are taken from a Levite, items **without sanctity** (foreleg, jaw, maw) are not — seems to prove
Levites are *not* "the people"; answered again as first tithe after Ezra. Then: "one who slaughters
**for a kohen or a gentile** is exempt from the gifts," and an explicit parallel baraita reads "for
a **Levite or an Israelite** — obligated." That one the Gemara cannot deflect: **a conclusive
refutation of Rav** (*teyuvta*).

**Rav's last word — it is a tannaitic dispute.** The Yom Kippur atonement verse (Leviticus 16:33)
is expounded clause by clause; one baraita reads a spare "he shall atone" as **the Levites**,
another reads it as **Canaanite slaves**. The difference: does the first tanna already fold Levites
into "the people of the assembly" (yes — *am*), leaving the spare word for slaves; or not (no —
*am*), needing the spare word for Levites? Rav is **uncertain which tanna** the halakha follows —
so his doubt stands even against the "refutation."

**The closing ruling and Ulla.** **Mareimar** rules: the halakha is like **Rav** (the Levites'
status stays in doubt, so the gifts are not forced from them) **and** like **Rav Ḥisda** (no payment
for damaging the gifts). Finally, **Ulla** would give the gifts to a **female priest** (*kohenet*) —
the daughter of a kohen, even married to an Israelite. **Rava objected:** the remainder of *her*
meal-offering is eaten, but a kohen's is wholly burnt — so "kohen" excludes his daughter; how then
can she receive the gifts? Ulla's reply — "*My teacher…*" — spills over into 132a.

## The distinctions, side by side

The poor-gifts baraita is the daf's cleanest grid: three classes of gift, each tested for whether
the owner may choose the recipient, whether a court forces even a poor owner, and which verb the
Torah used.

| Gift | Owner's discretion? | Court forces even a poor owner? | Deciding word / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Field-gifts to the poor** — *leket, shichecha, pe'a, peret, olelot* | No — first-come acquires | Yes | **"leaving"** (*aziva*) is written |
| **Poor man's tithe** (*maaser ani*), split at home | Yes | Yes | **"giving"** (*netina*) is written |
| **Priestly gifts** — foreleg, jaw, maw | (owner gives whom he wishes) | Not taken kohen↔kohen or Levi↔Levi | rests on the doubt — is a Levite "*am*"? |

## One line to carry with you

> When you cannot prove who owes, you cannot make them pay: Rav leaves the Levite's gifts and
> Rav Ḥisda leaves the damager's debt for the same reason — no claimant with standing means no
> claim the court will hear.

```yaml
- q: What idea underlies Rav Ḥisda's ruling that one who damages or eats the priestly gifts pays nothing?
  opts:
    - The gifts lose every bit of their value the instant they are separated from the slaughtered animal.
    - The gifts are money that has no claimant — with no one able to sue, there is simply no case.
    - The owner never fully acquired the slaughtered animal, so its cuts were never really his to owe on.
    - A kohen is always presumed to have forgiven his portion of the animal well in advance of any claim.
  correct: b
  why: Rav Ḥisda treats the gifts as "money with no one to demand it," so damaging them creates no debt a court will enforce.

- q: Why does the seized threshing floor (taken for a debt) fail to refute Rav Ḥisda?
  opts:
    - Because grain seized outright by force never had any gift-obligation attach to it in the first place.
    - Because the king's household is entirely exempt from the laws of tithing and priestly gifts.
    - Because the obligation is about his own gain (mishtarshei leih) — he is spared money he owed.
    - Because a forced seizure legally counts as an ordinary sale for every purpose of the law.
  correct: c
  why: He must tithe replacement grain only so he does not pocket the value of grain he was already bound to tithe — a benefit issue, not a claimant issue.

- q: Why does the sale of innards with the maw inside fail to refute Rav Ḥisda?
  opts:
    - Because it was in fact the buyer, and not the seller, who actually ended up eating the priestly gift.
    - Because a cow's innards are never subject to the priestly gifts in the very first place.
    - Because the entire sale was legally void, so nothing at all ever changed hands between them.
    - Because there the gift is intact (b'einaihu) — a distinct object that plainly must be handed over.
  correct: d
  why: Rav Ḥisda spoke of gifts no longer identifiable; an intact maw is a recognizable item, not comparable.

- q: When the baraita calls teruma, ḥalla, and the rest "the property of a priest," what does that phrase mean?
  opts:
    - That a court extracts them by force from anyone who wrongfully withholds them from a priest.
    - That the priest may trade with them and a creditor or wife may collect them for a debt or ketuba.
    - That they may simply be redeemed for money in just the way that second tithe is able to be redeemed.
    - That they belong collectively to the whole priestly watch rather than to any single kohen.
  correct: b
  why: Per Bikkurim 3:12, "property" describes what he may do with them and who may collect them — not a court order handing them over.

- q: On what grounds did Rav decline to force the gifts out of the snatching Levite and Levites generally?
  opts:
    - He held that the Levites are quite certainly exempt, since the verse explicitly names only "the people."
    - He held that the snatched gifts belonged to the children carrying them and not to the priests.
    - He was unsure whether Levites count as "the people," so the burden falls on whoever would take.
    - He held that the Levites had already been penalized by Ezra and therefore owed nothing further.
  correct: c
  why: Rav's doubt about "am" means the one who would extract the gifts must prove his claim — and he cannot.

- q: Why do the field-gifts to the poor (leket, shichecha, pe'a) carry no benefit of discretion?
  opts:
    - Because the poor are far too numerous for any owner to choose fairly among all of them.
    - Because the monetary value of these gifts is too small to be worth assigning to anyone.
    - Because a kohen, rather than the field's own owner, is the one charged with distributing them to the poor.
    - Because the Torah writes "leaving" (aziva) — the owner abandons them rather than hands them over.
  correct: d
  why: '"Leave them for the poor" makes them ownerless; the first poor person to take them acquires them, so there is no one to favor.'

- q: Why does the poor man's tithe, split at home, carry the benefit of discretion?
  opts:
    - Because it is separated indoors at home, well away from the very field where the poor would gather.
    - Because the Torah writes "giving" (netina) — "and you shall give it" — an act the owner controls.
    - Because it is simply larger in quantity than any one of the field-gifts left to the poor.
    - Because only the owner is able to judge which of the poor recipients are truly deserving.
  correct: b
  why: 'And you shall give it to the Levite, the stranger… — an act of giving, so the owner chooses the recipient.'

- q: The baraita says the gifts are not taken "from a Levite for a Levite," implying they ARE taken for a kohen. How does the Gemara keep this from refuting Rav?
  opts:
    - It rereads the case as first tithe or first shearing — "like the foreleg but not the foreleg."
    - It concedes the point entirely and admits that Levites really are called "the people."
    - It dismisses the baraita outright as nothing more than a mistaken, garbled transmission.
    - It restricts the whole of the rule to the days of the Temple, when the gifts were still actively given.
  correct: a
  why: By making the "gift" maaser rishon or reishit hagez, the case no longer rides on whether a Levite is "am."

- q: Why does the Gemara invoke "after Ezra penalized them" to explain taking first tithe from a Levite for a kohen?
  opts:
    - Because Ezra decreed the abolition of the institution of first tithe altogether and for good.
    - Because before Ezra's decree the first tithe had been given to the poor in place of Levites.
    - Because only after the penalty is first tithe assigned to priests rather than to the Levites.
    - Because only a court convened by Ezra himself ever held the power to extract the gifts by force.
  correct: c
  why: R' Elazar ben Azarya allows first tithe to a kohen; the penalty on the Levites is what makes it go to the kohen here.

- q: What did Mareimar rule as the final halakha on these two disputes?
  opts:
    - Against both Rav and Rav Ḥisda — gifts are forced from Levites, and those who damage them must pay.
    - Like Rav Ḥisda alone — gifts may be extracted in court, though the Levites themselves stay exempt.
    - Like Rav Idi bar Avin's refutation, settling once and for all that Levites really are "the people."
    - Like Rav (the Levites stay in doubt, so gifts are not forced) and like Rav Ḥisda (no payment).
  correct: d
  why: Mareimar upholds both rulings — Rav's unresolved doubt about Levites and Rav Ḥisda's exemption from payment.
```
