## The big picture (read this first)

Yesterday closed on a new prohibition — **אֵבֶר מִן הַחַי**, the limb of a living animal — and the
observation that its two camps read one and the same verse. Today the whole daf is a survey of that
one prohibition, walking its boundary in four directions at once: **which species** it covers,
**how much** you must eat to be flogged, **whose intention** counts, and **which verse** it comes
from.

> The prohibition of eating from the living is not one law but a small family of them: a *limb*
> torn from the living, *flesh* torn from the living, and flesh of a *tereifa*. Each amud asks where
> one ends and the next begins — and the answers keep turning on a single strange question about
> the animal itself: while it is still alive, does it already stand **divided into its limbs**?

Four movements:

1. **Which species** (102a) — non-kosher or only kosher, domesticated or also bird; and the crucial
   split between what binds a Jew and what binds a **descendant of Noah**.
2. **How much** (102a–102b) — Rav's rule that a limb, too, needs an olive-bulk, and the mishnah that
   seems to forbid *any* amount, rescued by counting sinew and bone.
3. **Whose intention** (102b) — Rava's twin *im timtzi lomar*: could a person's private thought
   about how he means to eat change whether he is liable?
4. **Which verse** (102b) — Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish divide two verses between limb, flesh,
   and *tereifa*, and it decides how many sets of lashes you earn.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **אֵבֶר מִן הַחַי** *(ever min hachai)* | A limb — flesh, sinew and bone together — severed from a living animal and eaten. The prohibition surveyed all daf. |
| **כֹּל שֶׁאַתָּה מְצֻוֶּה עַל דָּמוֹ** *(kol she'atah metzuveh al damo)* | "Any animal whose blood you are commanded about": Rabbi Elazar's reading, extending *ever min hachai* to non-kosher species. |
| **בָּשָׂר לְחוֹדֵיהּ** *(basar lechudeih)* | "The flesh alone": the Rabbis' reading, confining the prohibition to species whose flesh can become permitted. |
| **אִיסּוּרוֹ נוֹהֵג בִּבְנֵי נֹחַ** *(issuro noheg bivnei Noach)* | The prohibition binds descendants of Noah too — its added stringency, and the one point on which all agree it covers non-kosher species. |
| **בְּחַיֶּיהָ לְאֵבָרִים עוֹמֶדֶת** *(bechayeha le'evarim omedet)* | "In its lifetime it stands divided into limbs" — the pivot that decides whether eating a whole live creature counts as eating a limb. |
| **כְּזַיִת** *(kezayit)* | An olive-bulk, the measure "eating" implies; Rav requires it even for a limb. |
| **בְּמַשֶּׁהוּ בָּשָׂר גִּידִין וַעֲצָמוֹת** *(bemashehu basar gidin va'atzamot)* | An olive-bulk made of a trace of meat plus sinews and bones — how a limb reaches the shiur without an olive-bulk of edible flesh. |
| **קְלָנִיתָא** *(kelanita)* | A tiny, scrawny bird — the case of a body with no olive-bulk of meat but a limb that reaches one with sinew and bone. |
| **מַחְשֶׁבֶת אֳכָלִין שְׁמָהּ מַחְשָׁבָה** *(machsheves ochlin shemah machshavah)* | "Thought about food is [significant] thought" — the premise of Rava's twin cases. |
| **זֶה לְפִי מַחְשַׁבְתּוֹ** *(zeh lefi machshavto)* | "Each according to his own thought" — the same act can be liable for one person and not another. |
| **בָּשָׂר מִן הַחַי / בְּשַׂר טְרֵפָה** *(basar min hachai / besar tereifa)* | Flesh (less than a whole limb) torn from the living / flesh of a *tereifa* — the two neighbours whose verses Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish divide differently. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Yehuda** (bar Ilai) — fourth generation, student of Rabbi Akiva. Holds *issur chal al
  issur*, so he needs no verse to extend *ever min hachai* to non-kosher species.
- **Rabbi Elazar** (ben Shammua) — fourth generation, student of Rabbi Akiva. The one for whom the
  verse is truly needed; the daf reassigns the derivation to him. Not the Amora Rabbi Elazar ben
  Pedat, and not Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Shimon below.
- **Rabbi Meir** — fourth generation, student of Rabbi Akiva. Limits *ever min hachai* to a kosher
  domesticated animal.
- **The Rabbis** (*chachamim*) — confine the prohibition to kosher species, including birds and wild
  animals.
- **Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi** (Rebbi) — fifth generation, redactor of the Mishnah. Exempts for a whole
  live bird under an olive-bulk: in life it is *not* yet divided into limbs.
- **Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Shimon** — fifth generation, son of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. Holds one
  liable by *kal vachomer*: in life it already stands divided into limbs. (Not Rabbi Elazar ben
  Shammua above.)

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Yochanan** (bar Nappacha) — second generation, head of Tiberias. Assigns *ever min hachai*
  and *tereifa*/flesh to two separate verses.
- **Reish Lakish** (Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish) — second generation, Tiberias; Rabbi Yochanan's
  brother-in-law and great disputant. Divides the same two verses the other way.
- **Rabbi Mani bar Pattish** — a later Eretz-Yisrael figure; resolves the *Teharot* mishnah as Jew
  versus Noahide. Beyond that I would not press his biography.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav** (Abba Arikha) — first generation, founder of Sura. Requires an olive-bulk for a limb, and
  the source of the graded shiurim for kosher and non-kosher birds.
- **Rav Giddel** — second/third generation, a faithful transmitter of Rav's rulings. Reports that
  all agree a descendant of Noah is bound regarding non-kosher species.
- **Rabba bar Shmuel / bar Sheila / bar Shimi** — the uncertain name (hence the mnemonic) behind
  Rabbi Meir's reason; reported from Rav Chisda or Rav Yosef.
- **Rav Chisda** — third generation, Sura. **Rav Yosef** — third generation, Pumbedita. Cited as the
  two possible sources of that same teaching.
- **Rav Amram** — third generation, Nehardea/Pumbedita; a student of Rav Sheshet. Objects that the
  *Teharot* mishnah already implies an olive-bulk.
- **Rav Nachman** (bar Yaakov) — third generation, Nehardea. Supplies "a trace of meat with sinews
  and bones."
- **Rav Sheizvi** and **Rav Sherevya** — later Babylonian *amoraim* of the school; the first brings
  the *Teharot* proof, the second the *kelanita*. Both comparatively minor; I would not guess
  further at their generations.
- **Rava** — fourth generation, Machoza. The twin *im timtzi lomar* on thought and food.
- **Abaye** — fourth generation, head of Pumbedita; Rava's lifelong disputant. Presses the "same act,
  different person" objection both times.

*Look-alikes worth flagging:* three men named **Elazar** are in play — Rabbi Elazar **ben Shammua**
(Tanna, paired with Rabbi Yehuda), Rabbi Elazar **bar Rabbi Shimon** (Tanna, the *kal vachomer*), and
the Amora Rabbi Elazar **ben Pedat** (not on this daf). **Rabba bar Shmuel/Sheila/Shimi** is a
transmitter, not **Rabbah** the head of Pumbedita. And **Rabbi Yehuda** the Tanna is not **Rav
Yehuda** the Amora.

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 102a — Whose blood, whose flesh: the two readings

The dispute from yesterday's last line is laid out in full. Everyone builds on **״לֹא תֹאכַל הַנֶּפֶשׁ
עִם הַבָּשָׂר״** (Devarim 12:23), but they cut it in opposite places.

**Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Elazar.**

> **כֹּל שֶׁאַתָּה מְצֻוֶּה עַל דָּמוֹ — אַתָּה מְצֻוֶּה עַל אֵבָרָיו.**
> "With regard to any animal whose blood you are commanded about, you are commanded about its
> limbs."

Since even a non-kosher animal's blood is forbidden, its severed limb is forbidden too. *Ever min
hachai* reaches every species.

**The Rabbis.** Read the verse the other way — *"…the life with the flesh," but the **flesh** on its
own.* Only a species whose flesh becomes permitted (when it is properly slaughtered) is included; a
non-kosher animal, whose flesh is never permitted, is not.

**A sharp question for Rabbi Yehuda — why does he even need the verse?** He holds **אִיסּוּר חָל עַל
אִיסּוּר**, a prohibition takes effect on top of an existing one. Then let the *ever* prohibition simply
land on the non-kosher-meat prohibition on its own strength — it is the more stringent, **שֶׁכֵּן
אִיסּוּרוֹ נוֹהֵג בִּבְנֵי נֹחַ**, since it binds the descendants of Noah as well.

**The answer — and it quietly reassigns the whole derivation.** Indeed so: *Rabbi Yehuda* needs no
verse. The verse was needed only for **Rabbi Elazar**, who holds a prohibition does **not** take
effect on an existing one even when it is more stringent. A baraita confirms exactly this: it is
**Rabbi Elazar** who learns from **״רַק חֲזַק לְבִלְתִּי אֲכֹל הַדָּם״** that *ever min hachai* follows the
blood prohibition into every species, while the Rabbis confine it to kosher species from *"the flesh
alone."*

**Rabbi Meir narrows it further** — only a kosher **domesticated** animal, not a wild animal or a
bird. After a mnemonic for a chain of uncertain attributions (**שְׁמוּאֵל, שֵׁילָא, שִׁימִי**), Rabba
bar Shmuel — or bar Sheila, or bar Shimi — in the name of Rav Chisda (or Rav Yosef) gives his
reason: the verse **״וְזָבַחְתָּ מִבְּקָרְךָ וּמִצֹּאנְךָ״** sits right beside it, so the prohibition tracks only
cattle and sheep.

### 102a — The one place everyone agrees: a descendant of Noah

**Rav Giddel said Rav:** the entire dispute about non-kosher species is only **בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל**, about
Jews. **בְּבֶן נֹחַ** — for a descendant of Noah — **דִּבְרֵי הַכֹּל**, all agree he is warned about the limb
of a non-kosher animal exactly as about a kosher one. A baraita says the same, and a textual variant
even lets you tell which tanna a copy follows: *"a kosher animal"* in the singular reads like Rabbi
Meir; *"kosher animals"* in the plural reads like the Rabbis.

**Rav Sheizvi finds it in a mishnah too** (*Teharot* 1:3): one who ate a limb from a living
non-kosher bird **אֵינוֹ סוֹפֵג אֶת הָאַרְבָּעִים**, does not incur forty lashes, **וְאֵין שְׁחִיטָה מְטַהַרְתָּהּ**,
and slaughter does not render it permitted. For a Jew, "slaughter does not help" would be obvious —
it is non-kosher. So the clause must be about a **ben Noach**, proving the limb of a non-kosher
animal is forbidden to him. **Rabbi Mani bar Pattish** reads the same mishnah as two clauses in
tension — no lashes, yet slaughter does not purify — and resolves it the same way: the first clause
speaks of a Jew, the latter of a descendant of Noah.

### 102a–102b — How much is "eating"? An olive-bulk, made of odd parts

**Rav:** *ever min hachai* requires a **כְּזַיִת**, an olive-bulk, for lashes — because the Torah wrote
**אֲכִילָה**, "eating," and eating means an olive-bulk.

**Rav Amram objects** from that same *Teharot* mishnah: it says one who ate the limb of a non-kosher
bird gets no lashes — but if a *kezayit* is required, then in the very case the mishnah describes he
*did* eat a *kezayit*, and should be flogged for the non-kosher bird itself. **The answer,** in Rav
Nachman's phrase: **בְּמַשֶּׁהוּ בָּשָׂר גִּידִין וַעֲצָמוֹת** — read it as a limb with only a trace of *meat*,
whose olive-bulk is made up of flesh **together with sinews and bones**. There is no olive-bulk of
edible flesh, so no lashes for the bird; but there is a whole limb.

**A second challenge, from Rav's own shiurim.** Rav taught: eat a *kosher* bird **alive** and you are
liable for **any amount**; eat it **dead** (unslaughtered) and only for an olive-bulk; a **non-kosher**
bird, alive or dead, for any amount. If a limb needs an olive-bulk, how can a whole live kosher bird
be "any amount"? Same rescue: sinew and bone fill out the *kezayit*.

**And the case that makes it concrete.** Is there really a bird with no olive-bulk of meat in its
whole body, yet an olive-bulk of flesh-with-sinew-and-bone in a single limb? **Rav Sherevya:** yes —
the **קְלָנִיתָא**, a tiny, scrawny bird. But the baraita's end (strangle it and eat it and all agree
you need an olive-bulk) can't be a real *kelanita*, which is non-kosher and liable "for any amount."
So read it as **כְּעֵין קְלָנִיתָא** — a *kosher* bird as small as a *kelanita*.

### 102b — Does your private intention change the law?

The same baraita had a live-bird dispute: one who ate a kosher bird smaller than an olive-bulk —
**Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi exempts, Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Shimon holds him liable** by a *kal vachomer*
(if a single limb makes you liable, surely the whole bird does). The Gemara locates the real
disagreement:

> **מָר סָבַר: בְּחַיֶּיהָ לְאֵבָרִים עוֹמֶדֶת, וּמָר סָבַר: בְּחַיֶּיהָ לָאו לְאֵבָרִים עוֹמֶדֶת.**
> One holds that in its lifetime the creature *already stands divided into limbs* — so eating it
> whole is eating its limbs; the other holds it does not.

**Now Rava's two thought-experiments** (each opened with **אִם תִּמְצֵי לוֹמַר**, "if you should say"). If
Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi holds **מַחְשֶׁבֶת אֳכָלִין שְׁמָהּ מַחְשָׁבָה**, that a person's *thought* about food has
halachic weight, then someone who **planned to eat the bird limb by limb** and then ate it whole
would be liable — his intention carved the bird into limbs. And conversely, if Rabbi Elazar bar
Rabbi Shimon holds thought counts, one who **planned to eat it dead** but ate it alive would be
**exempt**.

**Abaye presses both times** with the same needle: *is there anything where the identical act makes
one man liable and another not?* **Rava's answer** is the memorable line:

> **זֶה לְפִי מַחְשַׁבְתּוֹ, וְזֶה לְפִי מַחְשַׁבְתּוֹ.**
> "This one according to his thought, and that one according to his thought."

The deed is the same; the person is not. Intention is part of the act.

### 102b — Which verse carries which prohibition

Finally, a clean four-cornered dispute over sourcing. **Rabbi Yochanan:** **״לֹא תֹאכַל הַנֶּפֶשׁ עִם
הַבָּשָׂר״** is *ever min hachai* (a whole limb from the living); **״וּבָשָׂר בַּשָּׂדֶה טְרֵפָה לֹא תֹאכֵלוּ״**
(Shemot 22:30) covers both *flesh* torn from the living **and** flesh of a *tereifa*. **Reish Lakish:**
the first verse covers *ever min hachai* **and** flesh from the living; the second is *tereifa* alone.

The stakes are counted in lashes. Eat a limb from the living **and** flesh from the living: two sets
for Rabbi Yochanan (two verses), one for Reish Lakish (one verse). Eat flesh from the living **and**
flesh of a *tereifa*: two sets for Reish Lakish, one for Rabbi Yochanan. Eat a limb from the living
**and** flesh of a *tereifa*: everyone agrees, two sets — and it is against *that* last agreement that
the Gemara raises a contradiction, which is where tomorrow opens.

## The distinctions, side by side

Two verses, three prohibitions — and the whole dispute is only about which verse carries which. **R' Yochanan:** *לֹא תֹאכַל הַנֶּפֶשׁ עִם הַבָּשָׂר* is the limb, and *וּבָשָׂר בַּשָּׂדֶה טְרֵפָה* covers flesh-from-the-living **and** *tereifa*. **Reish Lakish:** the first verse covers the limb **and** flesh-from-the-living, and the second is *tereifa* alone. Count the lashes and the split becomes visible:

| Eaten together | R' Yochanan | Reish Lakish | Where the difference comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| A limb from the living **+** flesh from the living | **Two** sets | **One** set | Two verses for R' Yochanan; for Reish Lakish both sit in the same one |
| Flesh from the living **+** flesh of a *tereifa* | **One** set | **Two** sets | R' Yochanan puts both in *וּבָשָׂר בַּשָּׂדֶה*; Reish Lakish reads that verse as *tereifa* alone |
| A limb from the living **+** flesh of a *tereifa* | **Two** sets | **Two** sets | Different verses on either reading — and it is against *this* agreement that tomorrow's contradiction is raised |

## One line to carry with you

> Whether swallowing a tiny bird whole is "eating a limb from the living" turns not on the eater but
> on the creature: is a living thing already, in the eye of the law, come apart into its limbs?
