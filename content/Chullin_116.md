## The big picture (read this first)

Today's page is three sugyot back to back, and each one is really asking the same quiet question in a
different key: **what makes something count as the forbidden thing itself, rather than a look-alike beside
it?**

First the daf finishes yesterday's argument about **diverse kinds in a vineyard**. Is a growing plant
forbidden from the moment it roots, or only once it visibly adds bulk? Abaye settles it with two words in one
verse — and the answer turns on *how* the seed got there.

Then meat-in-milk is pushed to its edges. Everyone agrees a kid boiled in its mother's milk is forbidden; the
fight is over the animals at the margin — an **undomesticated animal** and a **bird**. Is a bird in milk
Torah-forbidden, rabbinically forbidden, or fair game? Levi is served a peacock's head in milk and says
nothing, and Rebbi wants to know why.

Then the chapter's closing mishna reaches the deepest form of the question. Cheese is curdled with **rennet**
— the milky curd taken from a young animal's stomach. If that animal was a carcass, is its rennet forbidden
like its flesh? The daf's answer is a principle that will echo far beyond cheese:

> **חָלָב הַמְכוּנָּס בָּהּ פִּירְשָׁא בְּעָלְמָא הוּא.** "The milk pooled in the stomach is mere secretion."
> It is not the animal's body and never was — so it does not carry the animal's prohibition. A carcass is
> forbidden; the milk it happened to be holding is not the carcass.

Four movements, in order:

1. **Closing the vineyard question** (116a). *Ikaran ne'esar* — even the roots are forbidden — versus the
   perforated pot; Abaye's two verses reconcile them.
2. **Benefit, and the animals at the margin** (116a). R' Shimon permits benefit from meat-in-milk; R' Akiva
   and R' Yosei HaGelili divide over the undomesticated animal and the bird; Levi and the peacock's head.
3. **The rennet mishna** (116b). Is the stomach-milk of a gentile's animal or a carcass forbidden? Rav Huna,
   Shmuel, and the *chazara* — R' Yehoshua's retraction on gentile cheese.
4. **The final halacha** (116b). What you may and may not curdle with — and why the pooled milk is *pirsha*.

## Key concepts & terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **קֵבָה** *(keivah)* | The **stomach** of a young animal, and the milky **rennet** curd found in it — used as a coagulant to set cheese. The whole second half of the daf turns on whether that curd shares the animal's status. |
| **שְׁעַת הַכּוֹשֶׁר** *(sh'at hakosher)* | A "**moment of fitness**" — a stage at which the thing was permitted. Carried over from 115: did diverse kinds in a vineyard ever have one? |
| **עִיקָּרָן נֶאֱסָר** *(ikaran ne'esar)* | "**Their very roots are forbidden.**" Rav Adda bar Ahava's claim — the seed itself, not only its later growth, falls under the vineyard ban, so it *did* have a fit moment before rooting. |
| **עָצִיץ נָקוּב** *(atzitz nakuv)* | A **perforated pot.** A plant in it draws from the ground below, so carrying it into a vineyard can make it diverse kinds — but, the mishna says, only if it then adds growth. |
| **בָּחֲלֵב אִמּוֹ** *(bachalav imo)* | "**In its mother's milk.**" R' Yosei HaGelili reads the phrase to exclude a **bird**, which has no mother's milk. |
| **מוּתָּר בַּהֲנָאָה** *(mutar bahana'a)* | "**Permitted for benefit.**" R' Shimon's ruling on meat-in-milk — forbidden to eat, but one may still profit from it — against the mishna. |
| **בְּנוֹתֵן טַעַם** *(b'noten ta'am)* | "**Enough to impart flavor.**" The threshold at which the skin of a kosher stomach, used to curdle, gives the cheese a meat taste and forbids it as meat-in-milk. |
| **פִּירְשָׁא** *(pirsha)* | **Mere secretion** — waste matter, not food. The daf's verdict on the milk pooled in a stomach: it is not the animal's flesh, so it carries no prohibition. |
| **כָּנוּס בְּמֵעֶיהָ** *(kanus b'me'eha)* | "**Collected in its innards.**" The mishna's reason milk a young animal nursed takes the status of the animal it *came from*, not the one holding it — it is merely stored there. |
| **חֲזָרָה** *(chazara)* | A "**retraction.**" R' Yehoshua changed his mind about *why* gentile cheese is forbidden; "before" and "after" his retraction splits the mishna's contradictory clauses. |
| **מִשְׁנָה לֹא זָזָה מִמְּקוֹמָהּ** *(mishna lo zaza mimkoma)* | "**A mishna does not budge from its place.**" Once a clause is fixed in the Mishnah it stays, even after a later ruling overturns it — so one mishna can hold both. |
| **סְתָם מַחְשֶׁבֶת גּוֹי לַעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה** *(stam machshevet goy...)* | "**A gentile's unstated intent is for idolatry.**" R' Eliezer's presumption — the basis for forbidding rennet from a gentile's slaughter, since idolatrous offerings are barred even from benefit. |

## Who's who in today's daf

**Tannaim**

- **Rabbi Akiva** — third-generation Tanna of Bnei Brak, the towering figure of his age; here he reads three
  mentions of "kid" to exclude an undomesticated animal, a bird, and a non-kosher animal from the Torah's
  meat-in-milk ban.
- **Rabbi Yosei HaGelili** — a contemporary and frequent disputant of R' Akiva; reaches nearly the same
  result from "in its mother's milk," but reads it more leniently on the bird.
- **Rabbi Shimon** (bar Yochai) — third-generation Tanna, student of R' Akiva; quoted here (through R' Shimon
  ben Yehuda) as permitting **benefit** from meat cooked in milk.
- **Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda** — a Tanna who transmits rulings in R' Shimon's name; appears only as that
  tradent here.
- **Rabbi Eliezer** (ben Hyrcanus) — second-generation Tanna, student of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai; his
  locale prepared circumcision tools on Shabbat, and it is his presumption that a gentile slaughters with
  idolatry in mind.
- **Rabbi Yehuda ben Beteira** — a Tanna of Netzivin in Babylonia; Levi guesses that the peacock-eaters were
  in *his* locale and ruled like R' Yosei HaGelili.
- **Rabbi** (Yehuda HaNasi, "Rebbi") — last-generation Tanna and redactor of the Mishnah; presses Levi on why
  he did not excommunicate the peacock-eaters.
- **Rabbi Yehoshua** (ben Chananya) — second-generation Tanna; his **retraction** on why gentile cheese is
  forbidden is the hinge that reconciles the mishna's two clauses.
- **Levi** (bar Sisi) — a transitional figure, disciple of Rebbi; served the peacock's head in milk, and held
  his peace rather than condemn a community's ruling.

**Babylonian Amoraim**

- **Rav Adda bar Ahava** — an early Amora, in Rav's circle; argues that the roots of vineyard kinds are
  already forbidden.
- **Rav Huna** — second-generation Amora of Sura, foremost student of Rav; reads the rennet mishna as a Jew
  who bought a kid from a gentile and fears it nursed from a non-kosher animal.
- **Shmuel** — first-generation Amora of Nehardea, Rav's great colleague; reads the mishna as a single ruling
  and elsewhere holds the rennet itself permitted.
- **Rav Chisda** — third-generation Amora of Sura; the carcass-rennet is forbidden only because eating it
  "**looks like** eating carcasses."
- **Rava** — fourth-generation Amora of Meḥoza; presses Rav Chisda with an a fortiori. (**Rava**, רבא — not
  **Rabbah**, רבה, the earlier teacher of Abaye.)
- **Rav Shemaya bar Zeira** — objects from a mishna on the perforated pot. A minor figure; beyond this I would
  not claim much.
- **Rav Yitzchak** — transmits the resolution in R' Yochanan's name. Several Amoraim carry this name; which
  one this is, I would not state with confidence.

**Eretz-Yisrael Amoraim**

- **Rabbi Yochanan** (bar Nafcha) — second-generation Amora and head of the Tiberias academy; his "before and
  after the retraction" settles the mishna, and his rulings on rennet are debated by his students.
- **Rabbi Chiyya bar Abba** — third-generation Amora, a leading student of R' Yochanan; reports his teacher's
  rennet rulings. (**Rabbi Chiyya bar Abba** the Amora — not **Rabbi Chiyya** the Tanna, R' Yehuda HaNasi's

- **Rabbi Shimon bar Abba** — an Eretz-Yisrael Amora, colleague of R' Chiyya bar Abba; asks whose opinion the

- **Rav Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak** — an Amora who came up from Eretz Yisrael to Babylonia carrying R'
  Yochanan's more lenient version. (Distinct from **Rav Yitzchak** just above.)

## Walking through the sugya, step by step

### 116a — When is a vineyard plant forbidden?

The daf opens still inside yesterday's rules of method — *Rav Mordechai's* account of when a derivation can be
knocked down "by any distinction at all" and when only "by a leniency-and-stringency." The live objection: a
derivation stumbled over diverse kinds in a vineyard, because those kinds are unique — **they never had a
moment of fitness** (*shelo hayta lahen sh'at hakosher*): the produce is forbidden the instant it grows,
unlike meat and milk, which are each fine until cooked together.

**Rav Adda bar Ahava's answer.** Not so — **״כִּלְאֵי הַכֶּרֶם עִיקָּרָן נֶאֱסָר״**, "the roots of diverse
kinds in a vineyard are themselves forbidden," seeds and saplings included. And those *did* have a fit
moment: before they took root. So the objection collapses.

**Rav Shemaya bar Zeira's objection.** A mishna in *Kilayim* teaches: carry a **perforated pot** of seeds
into a vineyard, and it is forbidden only **if it adds** one two-hundredth in growth — **הוֹסִיף — אִין, לֹא
הוֹסִיף — לָא**, "added, yes; did not add, no." That says only the *new growth* is forbidden, not the seed.
Contradiction.

**Abaye's resolution.** **תְּרֵי קְרָאֵי כְּתִיבִי** — two expressions are written. "Lest the growth be
forfeited [*pen tikdash hamelei'ah*]" implies it must have *grown*; "of the seed [*hazera*]" implies it bites
the moment it is sown. How can both hold? **זָרוּעַ מֵעִיקָּרוֹ — בְּהַשְׁרָשָׁה** — sown *in the vineyard from
the start*, it is forbidden on taking root; **זָרוּעַ וּבָא** — sown elsewhere and *brought in* (the
perforated pot), only if it then adds growth.

### 116a — Benefit, and the animals at the margin

**The mishna isn't like R' Shimon.** A baraita: **Rabbi Shimon ben Yehuda in the name of Rabbi Shimon** —
meat in milk is **forbidden to eat but permitted for benefit** (*asur ba'achila u'mutar bahana'a*), by a
verbal analogy on *kadosh* with the torn-animal (*tereifa*) verse, "you shall throw it to the dogs." Since
that carcass may be given to dogs — benefit — so too meat-in-milk.

**R' Akiva.** An undomesticated animal and a bird are **not** Torah-forbidden in milk, from the threefold
"you shall not cook a kid." *But those three were already spent* on Shmuel's derivations (113b)! **The
answer:** R' Akiva holds a prohibition can land on an existing prohibition (*issur chal al issur*), so
forbidden fat and a dead animal need no verse; a fetus is a full-fledged kid; hence all three "kid"s are free
to exclude the undomesticated animal, the bird, and the non-kosher animal.

**R' Yosei HaGelili.** From "you shall not eat any carcass… you shall not cook a kid in its mother's milk":
only what can be a *carcass* is forbidden to cook — and **״בַּחֲלֵב אִמּוֹ״**, "in its mother's milk,"
excludes a bird, which has none. **What separates him from R' Akiva?** Two possibilities: over the
**undomesticated animal** (R' Yosei HaGelili — Torah-forbidden; R' Akiva — only rabbinic), or over the
**bird** (R' Akiva — rabbinically forbidden; R' Yosei HaGelili — permitted even rabbinically).

**Levi and the peacock.** A baraita confirms the split: in R' Yosei HaGelili's locale **they ate bird meat in
milk.** Then the story — **Levi** visited Yosef the bird-hunter, was served **a peacock's head in milk**
(*reisha d'tavvasa bachalava*), and said nothing. Rebbi challenged him: why not excommunicate them? Levi:
it was **R' Yehuda ben Beteira's** locale — perhaps he taught them like R' Yosei HaGelili, that a bird has no
mother's milk. One does not condemn a community for following its teacher's ruling.

### 116b — The rennet mishna: is stomach-milk the animal's body?

**The mishna.** The **rennet of a gentile's animal and of a carcass** is forbidden. Curdle cheese with the
**skin** of a kosher stomach and, if it imparts flavor, the cheese is forbidden. A kosher animal that nursed
from a *tereifa* — its stomach-milk is forbidden; a *tereifa* that nursed from a kosher animal — permitted,
**מִפְּנֵי שֶׁכָּנוּס בְּמֵעֶיהָ**, "because it is merely collected in its innards."

**Rav Huna.** Why single out "gentile's animal" — isn't that just a carcass? Rather, the case is a Jew who
**bought a kid from a gentile and slaughtered it himself**; the worry is that it nursed from a *tereifa*. An
objection — we buy eggs from gentiles without such worry — corrects it: the real fear is that it nursed from a
**non-kosher animal**, which is *common*, where a *tereifa* is not. And why not fear this for our own herds?
**We** keep our animals clear of non-kosher ones; gentiles do not.

**Shmuel** reads it as one ruling: the rennet of a gentile's slaughter simply *is* carcass. But didn't Shmuel
say gentile cheese is forbidden only because it is set with the *skin* of a carcass's stomach — implying the
**rennet itself is fine**? **The answer:** **כָּאן — קוֹדֶם חֲזָרָה, כָּאן — לְאַחַר חֲזָרָה** — one statement
is before **R' Yehoshua's retraction** on gentile cheese (*Avoda Zara* 29b), the other after it.

**The second clause.** How is a *tereifa*'s stomach-milk permitted when the first clause forbids a carcass's?
Rav Chisda: the carcass case is forbidden only because it **looks like eating carcasses** — but here there was
slaughter. Rava objects with an *a fortiori*: a carcass is so repulsive that permitting its rennet would not
tempt anyone to eat it, yet you forbid it — a slaughtered *tereifa* is less repulsive and *would* tempt, so
forbid it all the more! So Rav Chisda's reason cannot stand. Instead **Rav Yitzchak in R' Yochanan's name**:
before and after the retraction — **וּמִשְׁנָה לֹא זָזָה מִמְּקוֹמָהּ**, "and a mishna does not budge from its
place," so both clauses stay, though they contradict.

### 116b — The final halacha

**R' Chiyya bar Abba in R' Yochanan's name:** curdle with the rennet of a **carcass**, but not with the rennet
of a **gentile's slaughter** — following **R' Eliezer**, that a gentile's unstated intent is idolatry, which
forbids even benefit. **But Rav Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak** brought R' Yochanan's other version: curdle with
**either**, ignoring R' Eliezer. The daf rules:

> **וְהִלְכְתָא: אֵין מַעֲמִידִין בָּעוֹר קֵבַת נְבֵלָה, אֲבָל מַעֲמִידִין בְּקֵבַת נְבֵלָה…** — you may **not**
> curdle with the *skin* of a carcass's stomach, but you **may** curdle with the *rennet* of a carcass, of a
> gentile's slaughter, of a kosher animal that nursed from a *tereifa*, and all the more so of a *tereifa*
> that nursed from a kosher animal. **מַאי טַעְמָא? חָלָב הַמְכוּנָּס בָּהּ פִּירְשָׁא בְּעָלְמָא הוּא** — the
> pooled milk is mere secretion.

## The distinctions, side by side

The final halacha, laid against the one coagulant it forbids. The skin is a piece of the animal; the rennet is
only milk it was holding.

| Coagulant | May you curdle with it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Skin** of a carcass's stomach (*or keivat nevela*) | **No** | The skin is the carcass's own flesh |
| **Rennet** of a carcass (*keivat nevela*) | **Yes** | Pooled milk is *pirsha*, not the body |
| **Rennet** of a gentile's slaughter | **Yes** | No need to fear R' Eliezer's idolatry presumption |
| **Rennet** of a kosher animal that nursed from a *tereifa* | **Yes** | Still only *pirsha* collected in the stomach |
| **Rennet** of a *tereifa* that nursed from a kosher animal | **Yes — all the more so** | The source milk was itself kosher |

## One line to carry with you

> **חָלָב הַמְכוּנָּס בָּהּ פִּירְשָׁא בְּעָלְמָא הוּא** — the milk pooled in the stomach is mere secretion.
> The carcass is forbidden; the milk it happened to be holding never was its body, and so is not the carcass.
